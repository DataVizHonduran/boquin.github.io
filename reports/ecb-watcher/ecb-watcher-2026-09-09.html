Executive Summary

The last 30 days have seen a decisive shift toward a more hawkish posture within the Governing Council. Market consensus and official signals point toward a near-certain interest rate hike at the September 10 meeting, framed largely as an "insurance" move. This shift is driven by persistent inflation concerns, specifically higher German inflation and warnings regarding geopolitical risks (Iran) impacting energy prices. While Isabel Schnabel has explicitly called for further hikes to contain inflation, Joachim Nagel has signaled a hike for next week while remaining cautious about the trajectory beyond. The prevailing sentiment suggests a "hike and hold" approach: raising rates now to anchor expectations, but avoiding a commitment to a prolonged tightening cycle.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation remains the primary concern. Recent data showing higher German inflation has bolstered the case for tightening. There is a specific focus on "underlying inflation," which Nagel views as improving, though Schnabel remains concerned that the economy is too strong to allow for a pause. Geopolitical risks, specifically a "conflict of attrition" in Iran, are flagged as potential drivers of renewed inflation.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is mixed. Schnabel suggests the economy is "strong" enough to warrant higher rates, while the broader market is debating whether a hike is merely "insurance" against a potential rebound rather than a response to overheating.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
No specific new data on wage growth was provided in the last 30 days, though the baseline concern regarding wage-driven inflation remains a pillar for the hawkish camp (Wunsch, Nagel).

4. Financial Conditions & Credit

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
