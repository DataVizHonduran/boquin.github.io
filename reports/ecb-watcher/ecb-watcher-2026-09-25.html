Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a decisive hawkish pivot for the ECB. On September 10, the Governing Council raised interest rates by 25 basis points to 2.5%, citing an inflation jump fueled by energy costs and geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war). While the ECB raised both inflation and growth forecasts, the primary focus remains the upside risks to price stability. A significant structural shift occurred on September 24 with the announcement of Isabel Schnabel's resignation to join the IMF, removing one of the Executive Board's most consistent hawkish voices. Despite the hike, Philip Lane continues to emphasize data dependency, while Joachim Nagel has signaled that further hikes may be necessary to tame persistent inflation.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as having significant upside risks. The September 10 hike was a direct response to an "inflation jump" attributed to energy costs and the Iran war. The ECB is closely monitoring "second-round effects," particularly as the Wage Tracker shows a modest uptick in negotiated wage growth (2.7% for H1 2027).

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is mixed. While the ECB raised growth forecasts in September, President Lagarde explicitly noted "downside risks to growth" during the September 10 press conference, suggesting a precarious balance between fighting inflation and supporting the economy.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The focus has shifted toward wage-push inflation. The ECB Wage Tracker's report of a 2.7% uptick in negotiated wages provides the fundamental justification for the recent rate hike and the potential for further tightening.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
The ECB is increasingly focused on the intersection of AI and financial stability. Fabio Panetta warned of a potential "correction risk" regarding optimistic AI pricing, which could impact financial conditions.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)
No specific updates on the pace of APP/PEPP rundown were provided in the current data set.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has shifted from "easing" to "vigilance." The September 10 decision to hike to 2.5% and Philip Lane's subsequent comments on September 22 regarding a "worsening inflation squeeze" indicate that the ECB is now open to further hikes.
