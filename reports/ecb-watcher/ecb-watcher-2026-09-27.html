Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a decisive pivot toward monetary tightening. On September 10, the ECB raised interest rates to 2.5%, citing an inflation spike fueled by geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war). The Governing Council is now grappling with "upside risks to inflation and downside risks to growth." While Philip Lane and Christine Lagarde maintain a data-dependent approach, Joachim Nagel has emerged as a leading voice for further hikes. A significant structural shift occurred on September 24 with the announcement of Isabel Schnabel's resignation to join the IMF, removing one of the Executive Board's most consistent hawkish anchors.
