Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a significant pivot in ECB policy. On June 11, the Governing Council raised interest rates by 25 basis points—the first hike in three years—driven by an inflation surge resulting from the war in Iran and Middle East energy shocks. While President Lagarde describes the move as "necessary," the Council remains divided on the path forward. Hawkish members, led by Isabel Schnabel and Joachim Nagel, signal that further hikes may be required as energy prices remain volatile. Conversely, Philip Lane and Fabio Panetta emphasize a "measured response" and a "fragile" economic outlook. The "Sintra speech" by Lagarde suggests a "back to basics" approach, prioritizing data-dependency over rigid forward guidance.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as being under upward pressure due to external shocks (Iran war/Middle East). While some sources suggest a "quick oil price retreat" may ease urgency, the ECB's official stance is that energy shocks have created a "mid-sized inflation shock" with potential delayed effects.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is characterized as "fragile" (Panetta). There is a tension between the need to fight inflation and the risk of stifling growth, though Lagarde noted that "economic resilience" provides the ECB room to move rates.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
