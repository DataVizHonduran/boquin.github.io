Executive Summary

The Governing Council (GC) has pivoted to a tightening bias, raising interest rates by 25 basis points on September 10, 2026, bringing the deposit rate to 2.50%. This move was driven by a spike in inflation fueled by rising energy costs and geopolitical instability (specifically citing the Iran war). While the ECB raised both inflation and growth forecasts, President Lagarde emphasized a precarious balance between upside inflation risks and downside growth risks. Forward guidance has shifted hawkishly, with officials signaling that a further hike in December remains "on the table." The internal divide has widened, with Isabel Schnabel and Joachim Nagel pushing for aggressive action to contain inflation, while the broader consensus remains data-dependent.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently the primary driver of policy. The ECB has identified a significant "inflation spike" driven by energy costs and geopolitical tensions (Iran war). While Joachim Nagel noted that a slowdown in underlying inflation is positive, the headline risks are skewed to the upside, necessitating the September hike.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is contradictory. While Morningstar reports that the ECB raised its growth forecasts, President Lagarde explicitly warned of "downside risks to growth." This suggests a "stagflationary" concern where energy shocks threaten output even as prices rise.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
Limited specific data in the last 30 days, though Nagel's focus on "underlying inflation" suggests continued vigilance regarding second-round effects and wage-price spirals.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The guidance has shifted from a "wait-and-see" or "easing" posture to an active tightening cycle. The explicit mention that a December hike is "still on the table" indicates that the September move was not a one-off "insurance" hike but potentially the start of a new tightening phase.
