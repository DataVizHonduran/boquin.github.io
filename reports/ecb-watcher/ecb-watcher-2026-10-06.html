Executive Summary

The past 30 days have seen a significant shift toward a more hawkish posture within the ECB. On September 10, the Governing Council raised interest rates by 25 bps to 2.5%, citing upside inflation risks fueled by geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war). Recent data has exacerbated this pressure, with Eurozone inflation surging to 3.8% as of October 2. While Chief Economist Philip Lane suggests that growth drags may limit the need for further action, Bundesbank President Joachim Nagel and other hawks are signaling that additional hikes may be necessary to tame inflation. A major institutional shift occurred on September 24 with the announcement of Isabel Schnabel's resignation to join the IMF, removing one of the Executive Board's most consistent hawkish voices.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is the primary driver of current policy. The surge to 3.8% (Oct 2) and the impact of the Iran war on energy prices have created a "squeeze" that Philip Lane warned about in mid-September. The ECB has officially raised its inflation forecasts.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is mixed. While the September projections raised growth forecasts, President Lagarde explicitly noted "downside risks to growth." Philip Lane is currently emphasizing these drags as a reason to potentially limit further rate hikes.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
No specific new data provided in the last 30 days, though the baseline remains vigilant on wage-driven inflation (Wunsch/Schnabel).

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
Focus has shifted toward AI's impact on the economy. Fabio Panetta warned of a "correction risk" regarding optimistic AI pricing and a "ferocious rally" in stocks. Olli Rehn noted that soaring yields are acting as a natural curb on inflation.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The guidance has shifted from "easing/pause" to "active hiking." The September 10 hike to 2.5% and subsequent comments from Nagel suggest the ECB is in a new tightening cycle to combat a second wave of inflation.
