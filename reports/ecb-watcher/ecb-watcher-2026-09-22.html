Executive Summary

The ECB has pivoted back to a tightening cycle, raising interest rates to 2.5% on September 10th. This move was driven by an inflation spike attributed to rising energy costs and geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war). The Governing Council has signaled that further hikes remain "on the table" for December. While the ECB raised both inflation and growth forecasts, the primary focus has shifted toward containing upside inflation risks. Internal dynamics show a strengthening hawkish consensus, led by Isabel Schnabel and Joachim Nagel, while dovish members express concern over growth risks and the efficacy of rate hikes against supply-side shocks.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as having significant upside risks. The September 10th hike was a direct response to an "inflation jump" fueled by energy costs and the Iran war. The ECB has revised its inflation forecasts upward.

2. Growth Outlook
The ECB has raised its growth forecasts, yet President Lagarde explicitly noted "downside risks to growth," suggesting a precarious balance between fighting inflation and maintaining economic activity.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (released 2026-09-16) shows negotiated wage growth at 2.7% for H1 2027. The "modest uptick" suggests that wage-push inflation remains a concern for the Council.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
The ECB is focusing on the risks of "optimistic AI pricing" (Panetta) and the broader impact of AI on productivity and capital.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
