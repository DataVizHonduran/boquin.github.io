Executive Summary

The past 30 days indicate a shift toward a more hawkish tilt within the Governing Council, driven by persistent inflation concerns and a willingness to prioritize price stability over bond market volatility. While the September meeting accounts suggested a dampening of immediate hike bets, recent rhetoric from key figures like Pierre Wunsch and Joachim Nagel suggests the ECB may be on course for another rate increase in late 2026. A recurring theme is the "transmission" of policy; officials like Panetta and Lagarde noted that rising bond yields are actively contributing to economic restraint and inflation reduction, potentially reducing the need for aggressive new hikes but justifying a "higher for longer" or additional tightening stance.

Growth is viewed as being under pressure, partly by design. Fabio Panetta explicitly noted that rising bond yields are acting as a restraint on the economy, which aligns with the goal of cooling demand to fight inflation.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
No specific new data on wage growth was provided in the last 30 days, though the general hawkish tilt of members like Wunsch suggests underlying concerns about wage-push inflation.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
There is a clear consensus that the "bond selloff" is doing some of the heavy lifting for monetary policy. Lagarde and Panetta both highlighted that higher yields are tightening financial conditions, which may influence the timing of future rate moves.
