Executive Summary

The ECB has pivoted back to a tightening bias, raising the deposit rate by 25 basis points to 2.50% on September 10. This move was driven by a spike in inflation fueled by geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war) and persistent energy price risks. President Lagarde highlighted a challenging "upside risks to inflation and downside risks to growth" trade-off. Hawkish sentiment has intensified, led by Isabel Schnabel and Joachim Nagel, with the Governing Council signaling that a further hike in December remains "on the table." While underlying inflation showed some signs of slowing earlier in the month, a modest uptick in negotiated wage growth (2.7% in H1 2027 projections) and energy volatility have shifted the consensus toward a more restrictive stance to prevent inflation from becoming unanchored.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as having significant upside risks. The primary driver is the "Iran war," which is fueling energy prices and creating a spike in headline inflation. The ECB is concerned about these shocks becoming embedded.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is precarious. President Lagarde explicitly noted "downside risks to growth," suggesting the ECB is navigating a stagflationary environment where it must fight inflation despite weakening economic activity.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (2026-09-16) shows negotiated wage growth at 2.7% for H1 2027. This "modest uptick" provides a justification for the hawkish pivot, as wage-push inflation remains a key concern for the GC.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
Limited specific data in this period, though the rate hike to 2.5% is intended to further tighten financial conditions to cool price pressures.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
