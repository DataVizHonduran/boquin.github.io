Executive Summary

The ECB has pivoted back to a tightening cycle, raising the deposit rate to 2.5% on September 10. This move was driven by a spike in inflation, exacerbated by geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war) and upside risks to price stability. While the Governing Council acknowledged downside risks to growth, the priority has shifted toward containing inflation. Isabel Schnabel and Joachim Nagel emerged as primary drivers of this hawkish shift, with Nagel signaling that further hikes may be necessary depending on energy prices. Forward guidance remains cautious, with a December hike remaining "on the table."

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as the primary risk. The September 10 hike was a direct response to an "inflation jump" and "inflation spike." Geopolitical instability, specifically the Iran war, is explicitly cited as a driver fueling inflation.

2. Growth Outlook
The ECB is managing a delicate balance; while growth forecasts were revised upward on Sept 10, Lagarde explicitly noted "downside risks to growth." There is a recognized tension between the need to fight inflation and the risk of stifling economic activity.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (Sept 16) shows negotiated wage growth at 2.7% for H1 2027. The "modest uptick" in wage growth is a key metric the GC is monitoring for second-round effects.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
Recent focus has shifted toward the impact of AI on productivity and the role of synthetic securitization in supporting growth. However, the immediate priority is the transmission of the 2.5% rate to the broader economy.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
