Executive Summary

The ECB maintained interest rates unchanged at the July 23 meeting, but the policy tone has shifted markedly toward a hawkish bias. The primary driver is a resurgence of inflation risks linked to geopolitical instability (specifically the "Iran war"), which has reignited concerns over energy-driven price shocks. While the ECB Wage Tracker shows stable negotiated wage pressures (2.7% in Q1 2027), the Governing Council has signaled that the door remains open for further rate hikes. Market sentiment and reports from the Financial Times suggest the ECB is now on course for a potential rate increase in September. The focus has shifted from "when to cut" to "how to anchor expectations" amidst external supply shocks.
