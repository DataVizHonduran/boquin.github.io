Executive Summary

The ECB has pivoted back to a tightening cycle, raising interest rates to 2.5% on September 10. This move was driven by a spike in inflation fueled by geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war) and persistent upside risks. While the Governing Council acknowledges downside risks to growth, the priority has shifted toward containing an inflation jump. Hawkish sentiment has intensified, led by Isabel Schnabel and Joachim Nagel, who both signaled that further hikes may be necessary depending on energy prices and economic strength. Conversely, some members and external critics (e.g., Italy's Giorgetti) express concern that rate hikes may not address the current supply-side inflation drivers.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as having significant "upside risks," specifically linked to the Iran war and energy price volatility. The ECB has officially raised its inflation forecasts, signaling that the current spike is viewed as a threat to the 2% target.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is characterized by "downside risks." While the ECB raised growth forecasts in September, the overall sentiment remains cautious, with a recognized tension between fighting inflation and supporting a fragile economy.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (H1 2027) shows negotiated wage growth at 2.7%. The "modest uptick" suggests that while wages aren't spiraling, they remain a point of vigilance for the GC regarding second-round effects.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
The ECB is monitoring the impact of the 2.5% rate on credit. There is an ongoing focus on how "big tech" debt and synthetic securitization affect the broader financial stability of the euro area.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has shifted from "data-dependent" to "actively tightening." The September 10 hike to 2.5% and subsequent comments from Nagel and Schnabel suggest the ECB is prepared for further hikes if energy prices remain volatile.
