Executive Summary

The past 30 days have been marked by a significant shift toward a more restrictive posture. On September 10, the ECB raised interest rates by 25 bps to 2.5% (deposit rate), citing upside inflation risks fueled by geopolitical tensions (Iran war). Recent data has amplified these concerns, with Eurozone inflation surging to 3.8% (Oct 2), putting immediate pressure on the Governing Council to consider further hikes. While Philip Lane suggests growth drags may limit the need for action, Joachim Nagel has explicitly signaled that further hikes may be necessary. A major institutional shift occurred on September 24 with the announcement of Isabel Schnabel's resignation to join the IMF, removing one of the Executive Board's most consistent hawkish voices.
