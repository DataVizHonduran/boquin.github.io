Executive Summary

The European Central Bank (ECB) pivoted to a tightening bias during the coverage period, raising interest rates by 25 basis points to 2.5% on September 10, 2026. This move was driven by a spike in inflation, primarily fueled by rising energy costs and geopolitical tensions, specifically the impact of the Iran war. While President Lagarde acknowledged downside risks to growth, the Governing Council signaled that inflation upside risks currently dominate. Forward guidance has shifted significantly, with policymakers indicating that a further rate hike in December remains "on the table." Hawkish members, led by Isabel Schnabel and Joachim Nagel, had signaled this move well in advance, emphasizing the need to contain persistent inflation.
