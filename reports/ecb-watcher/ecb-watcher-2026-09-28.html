Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a significant hawkish pivot for the ECB. On September 10, the Governing Council raised the deposit rate to 2.5%, citing an inflation jump fueled by geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war). President Lagarde and Chief Economist Philip Lane have signaled that further hikes remain on the table, with a potential move in December. A critical structural shift occurred on September 24 with the announcement of Isabel Schnabel's resignation to join the IMF, removing one of the Executive Board's most consistent hawks. Despite the rate hike, the ECB continues to monitor AI's impact on productivity and inflation, while the "wage tracker" suggests a modest uptick in negotiated wage growth for H1 2027.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as an upside risk, specifically driven by geopolitical shocks (Iran war). The ECB has revised inflation forecasts upward, leading to the September 10 rate hike.

2. Growth Outlook
Growth forecasts have been revised, but President Lagarde explicitly noted "downside risks to growth" during the September 10 press conference, suggesting a delicate balance between fighting inflation and avoiding recession.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (Sept 16) shows a modest uptick in negotiated wage growth (2.7% for H1 2027), which may sustain second-round inflation effects.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
