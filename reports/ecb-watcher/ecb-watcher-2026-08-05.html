Executive Summary

The ECB maintained interest rates at 2.25% during the July 23 meeting, but the tone has shifted markedly toward a hawkish bias. The primary driver is a resurgence of inflation linked to the "Iran war," which has reignited price pressures. While the Governing Council (GC) opted for a hold, President Lagarde and other officials have signaled that the ECB is "primed" for a potential rate hike in September. This represents a pivot from previous easing expectations. While wage pressures remain stable (ECB wage tracker at 2.7%), the geopolitical shock to energy and supply chains is currently dominating the policy discourse, overriding growth concerns.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is the dominant concern. The "Iran war" is cited as a primary driver of reigniting inflation, pushing the GC to debate a rate hike in July and signal a potential move in September.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is described as "fragile" (Panetta). There is a tension between the need to curb geopolitical inflation and the risk of further stifling a weak economy.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
Wage pressures appear contained. The ECB wage tracker for Q1 2027 is at 2.7%, suggesting that second-round effects from wages are not the primary driver of the current inflation spike.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
Credit conditions are tightening. Both the July Bank Lending Survey and the Survey on Access to Finance of Enterprises indicate that lending conditions have tightened, suggesting the restrictive stance is being felt by borrowers.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
