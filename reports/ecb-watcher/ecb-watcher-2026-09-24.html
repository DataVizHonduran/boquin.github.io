Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a decisive hawkish pivot for the ECB. On September 10, the Governing Council raised interest rates by 25 basis points to 2.5%, citing an inflation spike fueled by energy costs and geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war). While the ECB raised growth forecasts, the "upside risks to inflation" now outweigh "downside risks to growth." Key officials, including Joachim Nagel and Isabel Schnabel, have signaled that further hikes may be necessary, with market analysts (BofA) already pricing in a December move. The narrative has shifted from easing to containment, driven by energy shocks and a resilient economy that complicates the rate outlook.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as having significant "upside risks." The primary drivers are energy prices, exacerbated by the Iran war. The ECB is closely monitoring "second-round effects," particularly as the wage tracker shows a modest uptick.

2. Growth Outlook
The Eurozone economy is showing surprising resilience, "defying energy shocks." While Lagarde noted downside risks, the overall growth forecasts were revised upward on September 10, which removes some of the pressure to ease rates to support the economy.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (H1 2027) reported 2.7% growth. This modest uptick in negotiated wages is a key metric for the GC in determining if inflation is becoming embedded.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
