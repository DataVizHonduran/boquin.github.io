Executive Summary

The ECB maintained interest rates unchanged at the July 23 meeting, but the policy narrative has shifted sharply toward a hawkish bias. The primary driver is a resurgence of inflation fueled by geopolitical instability (Iran war/Hormuz hostilities), which has led to upside surprises in July inflation data. President Lagarde has signaled that the ECB is "primed" for a potential rate hike in September. While the ECB Wage Tracker suggests stable negotiated wage pressures (2.7% in Q1 2027), the Governing Council is increasingly concerned with price expectations and supply-side shocks. Market pricing now reflects a higher probability of two hikes by early 2027.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is the dominant concern. July data showed an upside surprise, bolstered by "Iran war inflation" and renewed hostilities in the Strait of Hormuz. The focus has shifted from domestic demand to supply-side energy shocks.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook is described as "fragile" (Panetta). There is a clear tension between the need to fight inflation and the risk of stifling a weak economy.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (2.7% in Q1 2027) provides a silver lining, suggesting that negotiated wage pressures are not currently the primary driver of the inflation uptick.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
Both the Bank Lending Survey and the Survey on Access to Finance of Enterprises indicate that lending conditions have tightened, suggesting that previous rate hikes are continuing to transmit to the real economy.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)
No specific updates on the pace of APP/PEPP rundown were provided in the current data set.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has pivoted from "data-dependent" to "primed for action." The market and the President are now openly discussing a September rate hike, a significant departure from the previous easing/pause trajectory.
