Executive Summary

The past 30 days marked a significant policy pivot as the ECB raised interest rates to 2.5% on September 10, citing an inflation jump fueled by energy costs and geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war). The Governing Council is now balancing upside inflation risks against downside growth risks. While the hike signals a return to a restrictive stance, internal divisions persist: Joachim Nagel remains strongly hawkish, warning of further hikes, while Philip Lane and Christine Lagarde emphasize a data-dependent approach. A major institutional shift occurred on September 24 with the announcement of Isabel Schnabel's resignation to join the IMF, removing one of the Executive Board's most consistent hawkish voices.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as having significant upside risks. The September 10 rate hike was a direct response to an "inflation jump" attributed to energy costs and the Iran war. Philip Lane has explicitly warned that the "inflation squeeze will worsen," suggesting that the 2% target remains under threat.

2. Growth Outlook
The ECB acknowledges "downside risks to growth." While the September 10 meeting saw an increase in growth forecasts, the overall sentiment remains cautious, with the Council balancing the need to fight inflation against the risk of stifling economic activity.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (Sept 16) shows negotiated wage growth at 2.7% for H1 2027. The "modest uptick" in wage growth is a key metric the ECB is monitoring for second-round effects on inflation.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
Focus has shifted toward financial stability and new asset classes. Fabio Panetta has warned of a potential "correction" in stock markets following a "ferocious rally" in AI-related pricing.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
