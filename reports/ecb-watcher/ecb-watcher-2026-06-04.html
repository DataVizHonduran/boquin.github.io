Executive Summary

The monetary policy landscape has shifted dramatically over the last 30 days, moving from a regime of easing/pause to an imminent hiking cycle. The primary catalyst is a geopolitical shock—the war in Iran—which has pushed Eurozone inflation to 3.2% (the highest since 2023) via surging energy costs. A significant consensus has emerged among both hawkish and previously dovish members (including Panetta and Villeroy) that a rate hike in June is necessary to prevent inflation from becoming persistent. Chief Economist Philip Lane has signaled an upward revision of inflation forecasts, and Isabel Schnabel has explicitly called for a June hike regardless of potential peace deals. The Governing Council is now pivoting toward a restrictive stance to combat a new supply-side shock.
