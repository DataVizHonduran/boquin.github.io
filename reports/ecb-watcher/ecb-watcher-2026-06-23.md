### Executive Summary
The ECB has pivoted to a restrictive stance, implementing its first interest rate hike since 2023 at the June 11 meeting. This policy shift is a direct response to a "mid-sized inflation shock" triggered by the escalation of the Iran war, which has significantly increased energy costs and pressured short-term inflation expectations. While the Governing Council remains cautious about second-round effects—with Christine Lagarde playing down immediate wage-price spiral concerns—the tone has shifted toward proactivity. Even traditionally dovish members, such as Fabio Panetta, have signaled support for hikes to head off persistent inflation. The ECB is now operating without a pre-set path, maintaining a data-dependent approach while keeping all options open for the July meeting to ensure the 2% target is maintained.
