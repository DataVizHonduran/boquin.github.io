Executive Summary

The ECB has pivoted to a restrictive stance, implementing its first interest rate hike since 2023 at the June 11 meeting. This policy shift is a direct response to a "mid-sized inflation shock" triggered by the escalation of the Iran war, which has significantly increased energy costs and pressured short-term inflation expectations. While the Governing Council remains cautious about second-round effects—with Christine Lagarde playing down immediate wage-price spiral concerns—the tone has shifted toward proactivity. Even traditionally dovish members, such as Fabio Panetta, have signaled support for hikes to head off persistent inflation. The ECB is now operating without a pre-set path, maintaining a data-dependent approach while keeping all options open for the July meeting to ensure the 2% target is maintained.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently driven by an external supply shock (Iran war) impacting energy prices. The ECB acknowledges that short-term inflation expectations have risen, necessitating a proactive response. However, there is a distinction being made between this energy-led shock and "second-round effects."

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook has deteriorated. The ECB explicitly warned that the Middle East crisis is "squeezing euro area growth," creating a challenging environment where the bank must fight inflation despite slowing economic activity.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The June 17 Wage Tracker provides a silver lining, indicating that negotiated wage pressures are stable. This supports Lagarde's view that second-round effects are not yet the primary driver of the current inflation surge.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
The return to rate hikes is intended to tighten financial conditions. The ECB continues to monitor consolidated banking data to ensure the financial system can absorb higher rates.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)
No specific updates on the pace of APP/PEPP rundown were provided in the current 30-day data set.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The ECB has abandoned any "pre-set path." Guidance is now strictly data-dependent and "proactive." The phrase "keeping all options open for July" suggests that the June hike may not be a one-off event if energy prices remain elevated.
