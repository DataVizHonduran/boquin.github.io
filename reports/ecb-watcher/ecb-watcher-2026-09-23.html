Executive Summary

The ECB has pivoted back to a tightening cycle, raising the deposit rate by 25 bps to 2.5% on September 10. This move was driven by an inflation spike fueled by energy costs and geopolitical tensions (specifically the Iran war). The Governing Council has signaled that further hikes remain "on the table" for December. While the ECB raised both inflation and growth forecasts, the primary focus has shifted toward containing upside inflation risks. Hawkish sentiment is intensifying, led by Isabel Schnabel and Joachim Nagel, while Chief Economist Philip Lane has shifted toward a more cautious, "open door" stance regarding further hikes.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as having significant upside risks. The primary drivers are energy costs and geopolitical instability (Iran war). The ECB has revised its inflation forecasts upward, and the "inflation squeeze" is expected to worsen.

2. Growth Outlook
Growth forecasts have been revised upward, but President Lagarde explicitly noted "downside risks to growth," suggesting a delicate balance between fighting inflation and avoiding a recession.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The ECB Wage Tracker (Sept 16) shows negotiated wage growth at 2.7% for H1 2027. The "modest uptick" in wage growth is a key metric the ECB is monitoring for second-round effects.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
There is emerging concern regarding asset bubbles. Fabio Panetta warned of a potential "correction" in AI-related pricing and the "ferocious rally" in stocks.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)
No specific updates on the pace of APP/PEPP rundown were provided in the current data set.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The ECB has moved from a neutral/easing bias to an active tightening phase. Forward guidance now suggests that a December hike is "still on the table," moving away from the previous focus on rate cuts.
