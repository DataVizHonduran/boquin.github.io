Executive Summary

The last 30 days have seen a notable shift toward hawkishness among key Governing Council members, with a growing consensus that a rate hike may be necessary in December 2026. While Philip Lane suggests growth drags may limit the need for action, a strong contingent—including Joachim Nagel and Pierre Wunsch—has explicitly signaled that further tightening cannot be ruled out. This hawkish tilt is contrasted by a deteriorating credit environment, as the October Bank Lending Survey and the Survey on the Access to Finance of Enterprises both indicate tightening lending conditions. The Governing Council remains divided between those prioritizing the 2% inflation target and those concerned that rising bond yields are already overly restraining the economy.
