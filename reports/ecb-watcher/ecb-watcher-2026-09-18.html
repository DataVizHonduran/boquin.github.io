Executive Summary

The ECB has pivoted back to a tightening cycle, raising the deposit rate by 25 bps to 2.5% on September 10, 2026. This move was driven by a spike in inflation fueled by energy price shocks and geopolitical tensions (specifically citing the Iran war). While the Governing Council acknowledges downside risks to growth, the prevailing concern is the risk of higher inflation and a modest uptick in negotiated wage growth (2.7% in H1 2027). Forward guidance remains cautious, with a December hike remaining "on the table." The policy shift is supported by a hawkish alignment among key board members like Schnabel and Nagel, while the general consensus has shifted toward combating a new inflation impulse.

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is seeing a renewed spike, primarily driven by energy price shocks and geopolitical instability (Iran war). The ECB has raised its inflation forecasts, and the focus has shifted toward preventing these shocks from becoming embedded.

2. Growth Outlook
The ECB acknowledges "downside risks to growth," yet simultaneously raised its growth forecasts in the September projections. There is a tension between the energy-driven inflation spike and the potential for reduced consumption.

The Wage Tracker (2026-09-16) shows negotiated wage growth at 2.7% for H1 2027. This "modest uptick" is being monitored as a potential driver of second-round inflation effects.
