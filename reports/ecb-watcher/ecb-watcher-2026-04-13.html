Executive Summary

The past 30 days have been dominated by a significant exogenous shock: the outbreak of conflict involving Iran, which has triggered an energy crisis and pushed Eurozone inflation up to 2.5% (as of March 31). A critical tension has emerged between the need to combat energy-driven price spikes and the risks to financial stability and growth highlighted by Fabio Panetta. Wage data remains a primary dovish anchor, with the ECB Wage Tracker suggesting easing negotiated pressures for 2026.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation has surged to 2.5% due to the "Gulf war" energy shock. The primary concern among the GC is no longer the path to 2%, but whether this spike becomes "entrenched." Philip Lane has specifically flagged March and April as periods of heightened price pressure.

2. Growth Outlook
The outlook has darkened. Fabio Panetta has explicitly warned that the "damage has been done" by the energy crisis, suggesting that the economic cost of the shock may persist even if geopolitical tensions ease.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
This remains the most positive data point for the dovish camp. The March 23 Wage Tracker indicates that negotiated wage pressures are easing, which may provide the GC with the justification to avoid aggressive hikes if the inflation spike is deemed purely supply-driven.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
Concerns are shifting toward stability. Panetta has warned that the energy crisis is raising risks for financial stability, which could complicate the ECB's ability to hike rates without triggering systemic stress.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)
No specific updates provided in the current coverage period.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The guidance has pivoted from a focus on "gradual easing" to "readiness to act." The mention of an April hike as an "option" by Nagel and Lagarde's openness to hiking even for short-lived surges represents a significant hawkish tilt in communication.
