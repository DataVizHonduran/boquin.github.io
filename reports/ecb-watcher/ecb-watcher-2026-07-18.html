The past 30 days have been characterized by a significant shift in the ECB's risk assessment, driven by renewed geopolitical volatility in the Hormuz Strait and its impact on energy prices. Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
The primary concern has shifted back to energy volatility. While oil prices briefly retreated in late June, renewed hostilities in the Hormuz Strait (July 15) have reintroduced upside risks. Isabel Schnabel is leading the call for vigilance, arguing that geopolitical peace does not automatically resolve energy price instability.

2. Growth Outlook
There is growing concern regarding economic fragility. Fabio Panetta has explicitly labeled the outlook as "fragile," suggesting that the cost of fighting inflation must be balanced against growth risks.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
While not the primary focus of recent speeches, the focus on "second-round effects" by Panetta and the "Is inflation back?" inquiry by Schnabel indicate that the GC remains focused on whether energy shocks are translating into wage-price spirals.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
NCB research (July 15) indicates that banks are adjusting lending in response to trade tensions, suggesting that geopolitical friction is beginning to tighten credit conditions independently of ECB rate moves.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)
No specific updates or changes to the APP/PEPP rundown schedules were provided in the coverage period.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The ECB is moving further away from forward guidance. Lagarde's "back to basics" rhetoric and Lane's insistence that the ECB not be "boxed in" signal a commitment to pure data-dependency and a rejection of pre-committed paths or reliance on a theoretical "neutral rate."
