Executive Summary

The last 30 days mark a pivotal shift in Eurozone monetary policy. On June 11, the ECB raised interest rates for the first time since 2023, driven by a surge in energy costs resulting from the conflict in Iran. President Lagarde characterized the move as a "necessary decision," warning that inflation risks are spreading and second-round effects are emerging. While Chief Economist Philip Lane advocates for a "measured response," the Governing Council's tone has shifted decisively toward hawkishness, with Isabel Schnabel explicitly calling for further hikes to reach the 2% target. Notably, previously dovish members like Fabio Panetta have pivoted, arguing for the necessity of rate hikes without a pre-set path.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
The primary driver is a geopolitical energy shock (Iran war). The ECB has shifted from monitoring a decline in inflation to fighting a resurgence. President Lagarde has explicitly noted the emergence of "second-round effects," suggesting the shock is migrating from energy prices into the broader economy.

2. Growth Outlook
While specific GDP forecasts were not detailed in the provided data, the decision to hike rates despite growth risks indicates that price stability has regained absolute priority over economic expansion.

3. Labor Markets & Wages
The June 17 Wage Tracker suggests that negotiated wage pressures are stable. This provides a potential ceiling for second-round effects, though the Governing Council remains vigilant.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
The June 11 rate hike has tightened financial conditions. The ECB is also normalizing its collateral framework by integrating non-financial credit claims, signaling a move away from pandemic-era temporary measures.

5. Balance Sheet (APP/PEPP rundown)

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Forward guidance has shifted from "data-dependent easing" to "proactive tightening." President Lagarde has explicitly stated that the ECB is not guided by a specific "neutral rate" estimate, emphasizing flexibility and responsiveness to the current inflation surge.
