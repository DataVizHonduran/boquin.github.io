Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a significant pivot in ECB policy, characterized by the first interest rate hike in three years (June 11) triggered by an inflation surge linked to the Iran war and energy costs. President Lagarde has signaled a "back to basics" approach, emphasizing a proactive stance against inflation. While the June hike was described as "necessary" rather than an "insurance hike," recent data showing a retreat in oil prices has slightly eased the immediate urgency for further tightening. However, the Governing Council remains divided; hawks like Joachim Nagel warn that inflation relief is not yet in sight, while Chief Economist Philip Lane advocates for a "measured response," maintaining that the ECB will not be "boxed in" regarding its future rate path.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently the primary driver of policy. The "Iran war" and subsequent energy cost spikes have forced the ECB to lift inflation forecasts and raise rates. While oil prices have recently retreated, the ECB (specifically Nagel and Lane) warns of "delayed effects" and "ripple effects" that may keep prices elevated even if geopolitical tensions ease.

2. Growth Outlook
Growth concerns are secondary to the inflation shock, though Philip Lane's call for a "measured response" suggests a desire to avoid over-tightening that could unnecessarily stifle the economy.

3. Labor Markets & Wages

4. Financial Conditions & Credit
