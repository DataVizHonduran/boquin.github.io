Executive Summary

The ECB maintained interest rates at 2.25% during the July 23 meeting, but the policy narrative has shifted abruptly from a focus on easing to inflation vigilance. This pivot is driven by a geopolitical shock—the reignited Iran war—which has triggered energy price spikes and renewed inflation fears. While the Governing Council (GC) opted for a hold, President Lagarde signaled the ECB is "primed" for a potential move in September, with markets and several officials now pricing in a rate hike. Internal divergence is widening: Executive Board member Isabel Schnabel is explicitly calling for further hikes, while Fabio Panetta warns of a "fragile" economic outlook and risks to central bank independence amid rising defense spending.
