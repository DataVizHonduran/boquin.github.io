Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a significant pivot in ECB policy. On June 11, the Governing Council raised interest rates by 25 basis points—the first hike in three years—driven by an inflation surge resulting from the war in Iran and Middle East energy shocks. While President Lagarde emphasizes "economic resilience" and a "back to basics" approach, the Council remains divided. Hawkish members, led by Joachim Nagel and Isabel Schnabel, warn that inflation may persist even if geopolitical tensions ease, signaling further hikes. Conversely, Chief Economist Philip Lane advocates for a "measured response," though he acknowledges the need to be proactive. Recent data showing a retreat in oil prices may ease the urgency for immediate further tightening, but the overall bias has shifted from easing to restrictive.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is currently viewed as surging due to external shocks (Iran war/Middle East). While oil prices showed a "surprisingly quick retreat" by late June, the ECB has lifted its inflation forecasts. Nagel argues that structural price increases will persist regardless of the conflict's end.

2. Growth Outlook
President Lagarde highlighted "economic resilience," suggesting the Eurozone economy can withstand higher rates without immediate risk of collapse, providing the ECB "greater room to move."

3. Labor Markets & Wages
No specific new data provided in the coverage period, though the "back to basics" approach implies a return to core mandate focus on price stability over growth support.

4. Financial Conditions & Credit

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
