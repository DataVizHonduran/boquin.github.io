1. **[STL] System wide — Macro-Financial Linkages:** This paper examines the transmission mechanism of current monetary tightening on regional credit markets. It suggests a non-linear acceleration of credit contraction in mid-sized banks, which may force the Fed to pivot sooner than the dot plot suggests to avoid a systemic liquidity event.

2. **[STL] Board of Governors — Inflation Dynamics:** This research analyzes the "last mile" of inflation, specifically the stickiness of services-sector pricing. The insight is that wage-price spirals are more entrenched in urban hubs than previously modeled, implying a "higher for longer" regime for the federal funds rate.

3. **[STL] New York Publications — Global Dollar Funding:** This piece explores the current stress in offshore dollar markets and the efficacy of swap lines. It highlights a growing fragility in non-US financial institutions, increasing the probability of a sudden volatility spike in the FX markets.

4. **[STL] San Francisco Publications — Labor Market Mismatch:** The authors argue that structural shifts in AI adoption are creating permanent productivity gains but temporary frictional unemployment. This suggests that the "natural rate" of unemployment (u*) may be shifting upward, giving the Fed more room to keep rates high without triggering a recession.

5. **[STL] Chicago Publications — Industrial Production & Supply Chains:** This analysis indicates that the "deglobalization" trend is creating permanent cost floors for domestic manufacturing. For our outlook, this means structural inflation is higher than the 2% target, potentially necessitating a formal revision of the Fed's long-term inflation goal.

**Synthesis:** The collective research suggests a tension between structural productivity gains and entrenched service-sector inflation. We should prepare for a policy environment where the Fed remains hawkish on rates despite pockets of financial instability in the regional banking sector.
