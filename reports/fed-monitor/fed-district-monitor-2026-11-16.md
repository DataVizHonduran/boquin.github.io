Based on the recent monitoring window, the volume of actionable research is unusually lean. I have extracted the primary analytical signals from the available publications:

1. **[NY] Can Workers Predict Job Finding and Job Loss?**
This research highlights a decoupling between aggregate labor market data and individual worker perceptions, noting systemic optimism or pessimism regarding employment prospects. For our outlook, this suggests that "vibes-based" consumer sentiment may diverge from hard data, potentially creating lagged effects in consumer spending and labor mobility that traditional unemployment metrics fail to capture.

2. **[STL] Risky College Admissions and the (Mis)Allocation of Talent**
The paper examines how the volatility and risk associated with elite college admissions may lead to a suboptimal distribution of human capital. From a macro perspective, this points to long-term structural inefficiencies in labor productivity and a potential misallocation of talent that could dampen potential GDP growth over a generational horizon.

***

**Synthesis:** The current research cycle is sparse, focusing more on micro-behavioral frictions and long-term human capital allocation than immediate monetary catalysts. We should remain cautious of "sentiment gaps" in the labor market, as worker perceptions may be masking underlying fragility or resilience not yet visible in the headline prints.
