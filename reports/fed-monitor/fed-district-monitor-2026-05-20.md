## 🔦 Today's Most Interesting Insights

**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Investment Team
**FROM:** Senior Economist
**DATE:** May 20, 2026
**RE:** Federal Reserve District Research Briefing

After reviewing the recent publication cycle, the vast majority of the monitoring window consisted of administrative indexing from the St. Louis Fed without accompanying substantive papers. However, there are three critical pieces of analysis from the New York and Richmond districts that warrant our immediate attention for portfolio positioning.

1. **[NY] The Global Credit Cycle in Corporate Bond Returns**
This research analyzes the $19 trillion nonfinancial corporate bond market to identify systemic drivers of returns. For our team, this is critical for timing duration and credit risk, as it suggests that global credit cycles are driving returns more than idiosyncratic firm fundamentals.

2. **[NY] Honey, Who Shrunk the U.S. Income Surplus?**
The paper examines the widening gap between the $69 trillion in foreign-held U.S. assets and the smaller pool of U.S.-held foreign assets. This is a vital signal for our FX and Treasury desks, as a shrinking income surplus may signal shifting global capital flows and potential pressure on the USD's long-term reserve status.

3. **[RIC] Modelling Unemployment Insurance in the Real World**
The Richmond Fed suggests that current U.S. unemployment insurance structures are near-optimal for balancing worker support with labor market reentry. From a policy perspective, this indicates that the Fed and Treasury are unlikely to push for structural UI overhauls, suggesting labor market frictions will remain at current baseline levels.

**Synthesis:**
The current research focus suggests a pivot toward monitoring global capital imbalances and systemic credit cycles rather than domestic structural labor reform. We should prioritize hedging against shifts in the U.S. net international investment position (NIIP) while utilizing global credit cycle indicators to manage our corporate bond exposure.
