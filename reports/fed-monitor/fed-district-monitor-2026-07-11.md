**MEMORANDUM**
**TO:** Investment Committee
**FROM:** Senior Economist
**DATE:** July 11, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Analysis of Recent Federal Reserve District Research

Based on the recent monitoring window, the available data is heavily skewed toward the New York Fed's analysis of trade policy. Most other district entries are currently placeholders or index listings without substantive findings. Consequently, the analytical focus is concentrated on the inflationary and operational impacts of the current tariff regime.

1. **[NY] Effect of Tariffs on U.S. Small Businesses**
This research utilizes 2025 data to fill a critical information gap regarding how small-to-medium enterprises (SMEs) are absorbing trade costs. It is significant because SMEs lack the hedging capabilities of multinationals, meaning their failure or contraction could signal a broader systemic risk to employment and domestic supply chain resilience.

2. **[NY] More Tariff Pass‑Through Is in the Pipeline**
The Fed identifies a lagging effect where initial tariff costs are only now being passed through to consumers following a pivotal Supreme Court decision. This suggests that the "inflationary impulse" from trade policy is not a one-time shock but a sustained trend, likely complicating the Fed's path toward its inflation target.

3. **[RIC] When the Mortgage Becomes the Backup Plan: Debt and Disaster Insurance**
This paper highlights a correlation between high home equity and a decline in flood insurance maintenance, as homeowners treat their equity as a self-insurance fund. For our outlook, this indicates a potential increase in systemic credit risk and a higher likelihood of catastrophic losses hitting the banking sector during climate events.

**Synthesis:** The prevailing research suggests a dual-threat environment: a delayed but persistent inflationary wave driven by tariff pass-through and a hidden vulnerability in the housing market regarding disaster risk. We should brace for "sticky" inflation and a potential re-evaluation of mortgage risk premiums in flood-prone regions.
