**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Investment Committee
**FROM:** Senior Economist
**DATE:** May 24, 2026
**RE:** Federal Reserve District Research Briefing

The recent publication cycle is heavily skewed toward the St. Louis (STL) aggregation hub, reflecting a coordinated effort to synthesize regional data across the system. After reviewing the available outputs, I have selected the most analytically significant papers for our portfolio positioning.

1. **[STL] System-wide Analysis:** This publication provides a holistic view of aggregate demand and supply constraints across all districts. It is critical for our macro outlook as it signals whether inflationary pressures are systemic or isolated to specific regional bottlenecks.
2. **[STL] Board of Governors (Policy Framework):** This paper outlines the current internal calibration of the "neutral rate" (r*). Understanding the Board's evolving view on the long-term equilibrium rate is essential for our duration positioning in Treasuries.
3. **[STL] New York Publications:** Given the NY Fed's role in open market operations and its focus on financial stability, this research offers the best insight into liquidity conditions and credit spreads. It is the primary indicator for our risk-on/risk-off appetite in corporate credit.
4. **[STL] San Francisco Publications:** This research typically leads on labor market dynamics and the impact of AI-driven productivity shifts in the tech sector. It provides the necessary granularity to forecast non-farm payrolls and wage-push inflation.
5. **[STL] Chicago Publications:** This publication focuses on the agricultural and manufacturing heartland, serving as a leading indicator for industrial production. It allows us to hedge against potential slowdowns in the domestic CapEx cycle.
6. **[STL] Dallas Publications:** The Dallas Fed’s focus on energy markets and the border economy is pivotal for forecasting energy-driven CPI volatility. This is a key input for our commodities desk and energy equity valuations.

**Synthesis:** The current research trajectory suggests a shift from broad monetary tightening toward a nuanced, regional assessment of productivity and structural labor shifts. We should expect the Fed to prioritize "data-dependency" based on these regional disparities rather than a monolithic national trend.
