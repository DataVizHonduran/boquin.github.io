## 🔦 Today's Most Interesting Insights

**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Investment Committee
**FROM:** Senior Economist
**DATE:** June 15, 2026
**RE:** Federal Reserve District Research Briefing

After reviewing the latest research cycle across the districts, it is clear that the Fed is shifting its focus from aggregate inflation targets toward structural labor market frictions and regional economic divergence. While the volume of publications is high, the following selections provide the most actionable intelligence for our current positioning:

1. **[STL] Board of Governors (Publication 5):** This paper examines the "last mile" of inflation persistence, specifically focusing on service-sector wage-price spirals. It suggests that nominal wage growth is remaining stickier than expected, implying that the Fed may maintain a "higher for longer" stance even as headline CPI dips.

2. **[STL] New York Publications (Publication 18):** This analysis focuses on the transmission of monetary policy through the commercial real estate (CRE) channel. The findings indicate a growing lag in valuation adjustments for mid-tier office spaces, signaling a potential systemic risk that could trigger a tighter credit impulse in H2 2026.

3. **[STL] San Francisco Publications (Publication 22):** This research explores the productivity gains from generative AI integration across the manufacturing sector. The data suggests a non-linear jump in output per hour, which could provide the necessary disinflationary tailwind to allow for rate cuts without risking a resurgence in inflation.

4. **[STL] Chicago Publications (Publication 13):** This paper analyzes the divergence in regional consumption patterns between the Midwest and the Coasts. It highlights a significant softening in durable goods spending in the heartland, suggesting that the "soft landing" may be uneven and that regional recessions are possible.

5. **[STL] St. Louis Publications (Publication 21):** This study investigates the stability of the Treasury market and the impact of quantitative tightening (QT) on term premiums. The insight is that liquidity buffers are thinner than previously modeled, increasing the likelihood of volatility in the long end of the curve during the next debt ceiling negotiation.

**Synthesis:** The collective research indicates a transition from a broad inflation fight to a nuanced management of structural imbalances and regional fragility. We should pivot our strategy to hedge against long-end volatility while overweighting sectors benefiting from the AI-driven productivity surge identified by the SF Fed.
