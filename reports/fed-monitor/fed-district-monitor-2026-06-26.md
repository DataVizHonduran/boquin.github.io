## 🔦 Today's Most Interesting Insights

Based on the available research from the monitoring window, only three publications contained substantive analytical data. The remaining entries were administrative headers or empty. Here are the significant findings:

1. **[NY] The Post‑COVID Decline in the Labor Share**: This research highlights that the labor share of income has dropped to its lowest level since the post-war era. This suggests a structural shift in income distribution toward capital, which could dampen long-term aggregate consumption and signal a fundamental change in the bargaining power of workers.

2. **[NY] Synthetic Stablecoins and Financial Stability**: The paper analyzes a "risk-off" event triggered by 2025 tariff threats, demonstrating how digital assets and Treasuries reacted in tandem. This underscores the systemic fragility of synthetic stablecoins and suggests that geopolitical shocks can transmit rapidly through digital financial plumbing, increasing the likelihood of contagion.

3. **[RIC] Forecasting Wage Growth Using Months Supply**: This publication introduces a new metric for labor market tightness to improve the accuracy of wage growth projections. For our team, this means the Fed may shift its policy calibration away from traditional unemployment rates toward this "months supply" measure to better time interest rate pivots.

**Synthesis:** The current research indicates a precarious macroeconomic environment characterized by structural declines in worker income and heightened systemic vulnerability to geopolitical shocks. Consequently, the Fed is prioritizing more granular labor market metrics to navigate the narrow path between inflation control and financial stability.
