SUBJECT: Analysis of Recent Federal Reserve District Research

After reviewing the latest publications from the Federal Reserve districts, I have filtered out the noise and administrative listings to focus on the high-signal research. The current focus is heavily weighted toward trade volatility and systemic financial fragility.

1. [NY] Effect of Tariffs on U.S. Small Businesses

This research utilizes 2025 data to quantify the specific impact of recent trade barriers on the SME sector. It is critical for our macro outlook because small businesses often lack the hedging capabilities of multinationals, meaning their distress could lead to a broader drag on domestic employment and GDP.

2. [NY] More Tariff Pass‑Through Is in the Pipeline

The authors argue that the full inflationary impact of recent tariffs and Supreme Court rulings has not yet hit the consumer. For our policy outlook, this suggests a "second wave" of inflation is likely, which may force the Fed to maintain a higher-for-longer rate posture despite cooling growth.

3. [NY] What Do Over 3,000 Bank Runs Teach Us About Banking Crises?

This study re-evaluates the causal relationship between bank runs and systemic collapse. Understanding whether runs are a symptom or a primary driver of crises allows us to better price risk in the regional banking sector and anticipate the scale of potential liquidity injections from the Fed.

4. [NY] Using AI to Let History Speak About Bank Runs

By applying LLMs to historical data, the Fed is uncovering previously hidden patterns in bank-level failures. This suggests the Fed is refining its supervisory tools to detect early warning signs of instability, which may reduce the likelihood of a blind-side systemic event but increase regulatory pressure on banks.

5. [RIC] When the Mortgage Becomes the Backup Plan: Debt and Disaster Insurance

This paper highlights a dangerous correlation between low home equity and the lapse of flood insurance. From a risk perspective, this indicates a growing vulnerability in the housing market where climate shocks could trigger a wave of defaults that the insurance market is unprepared to absorb.

Synthesis:

The prevailing theme is a heightened sensitivity to "lagged" shocks, specifically the delayed inflationary pass-through of tariffs and the systemic fragility of the banking and housing sectors. We should position for a volatile second half of 2026, characterized by persistent inflation and a heightened regulatory focus on financial stability.
