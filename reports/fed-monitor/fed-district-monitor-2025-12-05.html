Executive Summary

Federal Reserve district research over the past 30 days reveals critical themes for macro investors: monetary policy transmission mechanisms, labor market structural changes, and trade policy implications.

The San Francisco Fed produced groundbreaking research showing inflation's origins (demand vs. supply-driven) fundamentally alter how markets interpret monetary policy persistence. Their tariff analysis reveals a complex sequential trade-off: unemployment initially rises while inflation falls, then reverses—complicating the Fed's dual mandate navigation. The St. Louis Fed's heterogeneous-agent model demonstrates employer-to-employer transitions significantly shape inflation dynamics during recoveries.

Structural labor market shifts emerged across districts: the Minneapolis Fed provided early evidence on Minnesota's non-compete ban effects, the Atlanta Fed quantified how H-1B restrictions suppress tech entrepreneurship (65% potential firm entry increase if removed), and pandemic retirement patterns show persistent 3pp labor force participation deficit among 65+ workers. The Dallas Fed flagged consumption concentration among top earners as an underappreciated economic vulnerability, while New York Fed research revealed heterogeneous business pricing strategies that challenge standard macro models.
