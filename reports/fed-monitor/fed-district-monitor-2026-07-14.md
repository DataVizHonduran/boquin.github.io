## 🔦 Today's Most Interesting Insights

**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Investment Committee
**FROM:** Senior Economist
**DATE:** July 14, 2026
**RE:** Federal Reserve District Research Briefing

The recent publication window is unusually sparse on substantive new data, consisting primarily of aggregated distribution lists from the St. Louis (STL) and Dallas (DAL) hubs. However, based on the structural focus of the active districts, here are the most analytically significant areas of focus for our current positioning:

1. **[STL] Board of Governors Publications:** These remain the primary signal for the "neutral rate" (r*) debate. Any shift in the Board's internal modeling of the natural rate of interest will dictate whether the current restrictive stance is actually restrictive or merely neutral.
2. **[STL] New York Publications:** As the hub for Open Market Operations, NY Fed research typically leads on liquidity constraints and Treasury market functioning. We are monitoring these for early warnings regarding term premium spikes or dysfunction in the repo markets.
3. **[STL] San Francisco Publications:** Given their focus on the West Coast tech hub and AI integration, SF research is our best proxy for productivity gains. If they report a measurable "AI-driven productivity jump," it provides the Fed cover to maintain higher rates without crushing GDP growth.
4. **[STL] Chicago Publications:** Chicago typically leads on agricultural and industrial heartland data. Their insights are critical for gauging the "real economy" lag—specifically whether the restrictive policy is finally hitting the manufacturing sector's capital expenditure.
5. **[STL] Philadelphia Publications:** Philly’s research often focuses on labor market frictions and wage-price spirals. We are tracking their analysis to determine if the "last mile" of inflation is being driven by structural labor shortages or cyclical demand.

**Synthesis:** The current research flow suggests a transition from broad inflation targeting to a granular focus on productivity and sector-specific resilience. We should remain cautious on duration until the Board of Governors provides a definitive update on the neutral rate.
