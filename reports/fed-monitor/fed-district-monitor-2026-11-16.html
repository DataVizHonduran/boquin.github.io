Source excerpt

Workers’ perceptions of the labor market may differ from aggregate labor market statistics. Individuals might be overly optimistic about their chance of finding a job, or too pessimistic about the odds of losing their current position. In this post, we compare subjective beliefs of future job-finding and job-loss from the New York Fed’s Survey of Consumer Expectations (SCE) with their realized counterparts in the Current Population Survey (CPS). We then investigate how closely aggregate perceptions of job-finding and job-loss prospects align with realized rates.
