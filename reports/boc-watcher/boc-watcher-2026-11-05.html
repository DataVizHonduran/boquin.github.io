Executive Summary

The Bank of Canada has pivoted back to a tightening bias, raising the policy rate by 25 basis points to 2.5% on October 28, 2026. This move marks a significant shift from the previous easing cycle, driven by a "tick higher" in September inflation and persistent inflation fears. Governor Macklem has signaled that further hikes may be necessary, with market expectations pointing toward another increase by March 2027. While the Governor maintains that monetary and fiscal policies are not at odds, the Bank is now balancing a "stagflation squeeze" characterized by rising rates and a softening labor market (110k jobs lost). The tone has shifted from data-dependent neutrality to a proactive hawkish stance to anchor inflation expectations.

Thematic Analysis

1. CPI-trim / CPI-median & Inflation Outlook
Inflation is the primary driver of the current pivot. Data from October 19 indicates that Canadian inflation "ticked higher" in September, reigniting fears that inflation is not yet fully contained. The Bank's decision to hike suggests that core measures (CPI-trim/median) are likely trending above the 2% target.

2. Labor Market (employment, participation, wages)
There are emerging signs of significant labor market slack. Reports indicate 110,000 jobs have been lost, creating a "stagflation squeeze" where the Bank must raise rates to fight inflation despite a weakening employment backdrop.

3. Housing Market & Mortgage Conditions
The housing market is retreating. GTA home sales fell nearly 8% in October. The rate hike to 2.5% is increasing pressure on variable-rate mortgages and overall affordability, contributing to the decline in sales volume.

4. CAD / REER & External Sector (trade, US tariffs)
The CAD initially strengthened following the October 28 hike. However, the external sector faces headwinds from a "trade war escalation" and potential US tariffs, which are colliding with domestic rate hikes to pressure the economy.

5. Neutral Rate Estimate & Real Rate Stance
The Bank is moving the policy rate upward to 2.5%, suggesting a belief that the previous rate was too restrictive or that the neutral rate has shifted. The stance is becoming more restrictive to combat the September inflation uptick.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has shifted from "data-dependent" to "proactively hawkish." The Bank explicitly stated that "more could be needed," and market polls suggest a trajectory toward further hikes by March 2027.
