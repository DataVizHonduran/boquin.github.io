Executive Summary

The Bank of Canada has executed a significant policy pivot, raising the overnight rate by 25 basis points to 2.5% on October 28, 2026. This marks the first rate hike in three years, signaling a departure from the previous easing cycle. The decision was driven by projections that inflation is likely to worsen, necessitating a more restrictive stance to anchor expectations. Governor Macklem and the Governing Council have indicated that further hikes are possible, though they acknowledge the "pain" this will cause households. The accompanying October Monetary Policy Report (MPR) underscores a shift toward hawkishness as the Bank prioritizes inflation control over the struggling housing market.

There is a clear tension between monetary policy and housing. Senior Deputy Governor Rogers noted that the policy rate is "too blunt" for housing affordability. The CAD saw volatility leading up to the decision, hitting five-week highs as markets bet on a hike. Analysts suggest the hike "buys the Loonie" in the short term, though Q4 headwinds remain due to a weak domestic economy.

5. Neutral Rate Estimate & Real Rate Stance
The Bank has moved from a cutting cycle (which peaked in June 2024) back into a tightening phase. By raising the rate to 2.5%, the Bank is increasing the real rate to combat the projected inflation uptick.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
