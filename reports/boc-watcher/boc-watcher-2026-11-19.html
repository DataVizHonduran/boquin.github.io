Executive Summary

The Bank of Canada (BoC) has pivoted back toward a restrictive stance, raising the policy rate to 2.50% on October 28, 2026. This move marks a significant shift from the previous cutting cycle, driven by persistent inflation fears. While October CPI data (3.1%) came in slightly below expectations, it remains above the 2% target. Governor Macklem has defended the hike, stating that scenario analyses as far back as April telegraphed this move. A major institutional development is the announcement that Governor Macklem will step down in June 2027, triggering a search for his successor. The Governing Council remains focused on the "inflection point" of inflation, balancing the need to curb price pressures against a cooling housing market and stalling consumer confidence.

4. CAD / REER & External Sector (trade, US tariffs)

5. Neutral Rate Estimate & Real Rate Stance

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
