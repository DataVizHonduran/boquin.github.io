Executive Summary

The Bank of Canada (BoC) maintained the policy rate at 2.25% on September 2, 2026, signaling a pause in its previous cutting cycle. The primary narrative has shifted from domestic demand cooling to external volatility, specifically the risk of U.S. tariffs and geopolitical tensions. Governor Macklem has adopted a cautious tone, warning that delaying potential rate hikes for too long could be risky if inflation re-accelerates due to supply shocks. While headline inflation held steady at 3% in August, the BoC is now balancing a "trade war" growth headwind against "tariff-driven" inflation risks. The divergence between the BoC's hold and the U.S. Federal Reserve's recent rate hike (Sept 16) is creating significant pressure on the CAD.
