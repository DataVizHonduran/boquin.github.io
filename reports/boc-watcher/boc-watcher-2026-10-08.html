Executive Summary

The past 30 days have seen a notable shift in communication, with Governor Macklem flagging the risks of delaying rate hikes for too long, signaling a potential pivot toward a more hawkish stance. This is driven by persistent inflation (holding at 3% in August) and external pressures, including the threat of new tariffs which Macklem warns could halve Q4 growth. Simultaneously, Senior Deputy Governor Rogers has explicitly distanced monetary policy from housing affordability, stating the policy rate is "too blunt" a tool to address the crisis. While the Governor emphasizes domestic data over Fed movements, market pressure and a weakening CAD are increasing calls for an October hike.
