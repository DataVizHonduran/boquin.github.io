2. Thematic Shifts

Inflation
There is a clear shift toward optimism. The Committee has moved from describing inflation as simply "running below" the target to noting that it has "increased somewhat." Crucially, the removal of the phrase "expected to remain low in the near term" suggests the Committee no longer views a period of stagnation as inevitable, but rather sees a more direct trajectory toward the 2% goal.

Labor Markets & Growth
The labor market remains a pillar of strength ("continued to strengthen"). However, there is a subtle tempering of the growth narrative; changing "growing strongly" to "rising moderately" regarding household spending suggests the Committee is seeing a more sustainable, less overheated pace of consumption.

Forward Guidance
The guidance has shifted from "waiting" to "almost ready." By adding the word "continued" to the strengthening case for a rate hike and qualifying the need for "some" further evidence, the Committee is signaling that the window for a policy lift is narrowing. The structural guidance (gradualism) remains unchanged, but the timing signal has accelerated.

3. Tonal Assessment

The Committee has shifted Hawkish.

While the federal funds rate remained unchanged, the textual evidence points toward an imminent hike. The acknowledgment that inflation is moving upward, the admission that market-based inflation measures have "moved up," and the linguistic shift from "further evidence" to "some further evidence" all indicate that the Committee's threshold for tightening has lowered. Furthermore, the voting record shows a shift in internal consensus: Eric Rosengren moved from the "dissenting/hawkish" camp to the "majority" camp, reducing the number of dissenters from three to two. This indicates a growing internal alignment toward normalization.
