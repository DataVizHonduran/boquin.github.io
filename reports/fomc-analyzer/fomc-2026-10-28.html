2. Thematic Shifts

Inflation
The characterization of inflation remains "elevated," indicating that the Committee does not yet believe the battle is won. However, the language has shifted from active intervention ("Today's policy action will support...") to passive observation ("accumulation of more information"). This suggests the Committee is moving into a "wait-and-see" mode to assess the lagged effects of previous hikes.

Labor Markets & Growth
There is a subtle but important upgrade in the growth narrative. Changing "solid pace" to "strong pace" suggests the economy is performing better than previously anticipated. Combined with the fact that unemployment has "changed little," the Committee is signaling that the economy has sufficient cushion to withstand current restrictive rates without triggering a recession.

Forward Guidance
The guidance has shifted from a deterministic path (raising rates) to a highly data-dependent stance. By removing the direct link between the current meeting's action and the 2% goal, and replacing it with a need for "more information," the Fed is signaling that the terminal rate may have been reached, but the duration of the hold is undecided.

3. Tonal Assessment

Overall Shift: Dovish (with Hawkish Undercurrents)

The primary action—holding rates steady after a series of hikes—is fundamentally Dovish. The shift to "data-dependency" and the upgrade of economic growth to "strong" suggest the Committee feels it has enough room to pause without risking a collapse in activity.

However, the 10–2 vote is a critical nuance. The explicit mention of two members who preferred a further hike introduces a "Hawkish undercurrent." This tells the market that while the majority is pausing, there is significant internal pressure to continue tightening if the "accumulation of information" shows inflation is stickier than expected. The Committee is pausing, but it is not yet pivoting.
