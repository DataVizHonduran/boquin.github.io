2. Thematic Shifts

Inflation

There is a significant shift in the Committee's perception of inflation. In June, the Fed was passive, stating inflation would "likely run at or below" 2%. By July, the language became proactive and cautionary. By stating that inflation "persistently below" the objective "could pose risks to economic performance," the Fed is signaling a fear of deflationary pressures or a "low-inflation trap." This is a direct response to the concerns previously raised by James Bullard.

Labor Markets & Growth

The tone regarding growth has deteriorated. The shift from "moderate" to "modest" pace is a classic central bank signal of decelerating momentum. Furthermore, the addition of "mortgage rates have risen somewhat" indicates that the Fed is now monitoring financial conditions as a potential drag on the housing recovery, adding a new risk factor to the economic outlook.

Forward Guidance

The forward guidance remains largely unchanged in terms of the specific thresholds (6.5% unemployment), but the phrase "today reaffirmed its view" serves to anchor market expectations. It tells the market that despite the "modest" growth and rising mortgage rates, the Fed is not yet ready to deviate from its highly accommodative path.

3. Tonal Assessment

The Committee has shifted Dovish.

While the growth outlook was downgraded (which usually suggests a need for more stimulus), the most critical change is the inflation language. The Fed moved from accepting low inflation to explicitly recognizing the risks of inflation remaining too low. This shift, combined with the fact that James Bullard (who previously dissented for wanting a stronger commitment to the inflation goal) now votes in favor, indicates that the Committee has moved toward a more aggressive stance in defending its 2% target. By acknowledging the risks of "persistently low" inflation, the Fed is effectively signaling that it is more likely to keep rates low for longer or increase stimulus if inflation does not recover.
