2. Thematic Shifts

Inflation
The Committee has upgraded its assessment of inflation expectations. By changing "moved up" to "moved up considerably" regarding market-based measures, the Fed is signaling that the risk of inflation undershooting is diminishing. While inflation remains below the 2% target, the removal of the qualifier "somewhat" when describing the increase in inflation suggests a more confident trajectory.

Labor Markets & Growth
There is a clear shift from a "recovery" narrative to a "strength" narrative. The previous statement noted the unemployment rate was "little changed"; the current statement explicitly states it "has declined." Furthermore, the description of economic activity shifted from "picked up from the modest pace" to "expanding at a moderate pace," indicating that the Committee now views the current growth rate as a sustainable baseline rather than a temporary acceleration.

Forward Guidance
The most significant shift is the removal of the "wait and see" language. The Committee has moved from judging that the case for a hike "continued to strengthen" to actually executing the hike. However, the guidance remains anchored in "gradualism." The boilerplate language regarding the "gradual increases" and the rate remaining "below levels... in the longer run" remains untouched, signaling that while the Fed is now hiking, it is not yet in a "fast-track" tightening cycle.

3. Tonal Assessment

The Committee has shifted decisively Hawkish.

The transition is evidenced by the move from a hold to a 25-basis-point rate increase and the elimination of the "wait for further evidence" clause. The tonal shift is further reinforced by the upgraded language regarding the unemployment rate (declining) and inflation compensation (moving up "considerably"). Most tellingly, the previous dissenters (George and Mester) joined the majority, signaling a unified Committee consensus that the economy had reached a level of strength sufficient to withstand a tightening of monetary policy.
