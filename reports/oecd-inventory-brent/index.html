🛢️ OECD Commercial Crude Inventories vs. Brent Price

Monthly since 2010 — latest point: Sep 2026 (inventory 2,683 mn bbl, Brent $114.2)

Rule of thumb: every 100 mn barrel draw in OECD commercial crude inventories ≈ +$13.9/bbl on Brent (and symmetrically, a 100 mn barrel build ≈ -$13.9/bbl).
Sanity check: inventories fell from 2,824 mn bbl (Feb 2026) to 2,683 mn bbl (Sep 2026) — a 141 mn barrel draw — which the regression would put at roughly +$20/bbl, while Brent actually moved from $71 to $114, a $43/bbl jump. The actual move ran hotter than the historical relationship alone would predict — consistent with a geopolitical risk premium layered on top of the inventory effect.

Fit: y = 469.1 + (-0.1392)·x, R² = 0.75 — every 100 mn barrel draw ≈ +$13.9/bbl on fair value.
Latest residual: Sep 2026 actual Brent $114.2 vs. fair value $95.7 — a $+18.4/bbl residual, above what the inventory relationship alone implies (e.g. geopolitical risk premium or demand/positioning effects not captured by inventories).
