### 1. Crude Oil Positioning
*   **NUS Total:** A healthy 4.5 MMBbl draw brings total stocks to 424.5 MMBbl. Positioning is extremely lean, sitting at only 9.6% of the 5-year seasonal range, suggesting a tight physical market.
*   **PADD 3 Highlight:** The Gulf Coast remains the primary buffer with 245.1 MMBbl (41.9% of range). While PADD 3 is relatively well-supplied, the overall national deficit creates a strong floor for prices.

### 2. Gasoline Positioning
*   **Seasonal Tightness:** Gasoline stocks are in a severe deficit, sitting at -43.2% of the 5-year seasonal range. 
*   **Demand Signal:** The WoW draw of 1.2 MMBbl, coupled with extreme seasonal undersupply, indicates that late-summer demand is outstripping refinery throughput. PADD 2 (Midwest) is particularly stressed at -27.1%.

### 3. Distillate Positioning
*   **Seasonal Tightness:** Stocks are moderately lean at -11.4% of the 5-year range.
*   **Demand Signal:** A marginal build of 0.8 MMBbl suggests a temporary lull in diesel demand or a slight uptick in production. However, the East Coast (PADD 1) remains tight (-22.7%), signaling potential regional volatility as heating oil prep begins.

### 4. Cross-Product Divergences
*   **Gasoline vs. Distillates:** There is a stark divergence in tightness. Gasoline is in a critical deficit (Strongly Bullish), while Distillates are seeing slight builds (Neutral). This suggests refineries may be optimizing yields toward middle distillates or facing specific gasoline feedstock constraints.

### 5. Price Implications
*   **WTI:** **Bullish.** Low overall NUS crude stocks and strong gasoline draws provide significant upward pressure on the front-month contract.
*   **Refined Products:** **Bullish (Gasoline) / Neutral (Diesel).** RBOB futures should see a premium due to the extreme seasonal deficit. ULSD remains supported by low stocks but lacks the immediate catalyst of the gasoline market.

### 6. Watchlist for Next Wednesday
*   **PADD 2 Gasoline:** Monitor if the Midwest deficit deepens; any further draw here could trigger regional price spikes and prompt emergency imports/transfers from PADD 3.
