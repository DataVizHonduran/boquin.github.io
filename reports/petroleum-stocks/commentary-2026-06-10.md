# EIA Weekly Petroleum Analysis: Week Ending 2026-05-29

## Executive Summary
The latest EIA data reveals a stark contrast between crude oil depletion and refined product builds. While crude stocks are leaning toward the lower end of seasonal norms, gasoline and distillates remain critically tight, suggesting strong underlying demand despite the weekly builds.

## 1. Crude Oil Positioning
*   **NUS Total:** A healthy draw of 8.0 MMBbl pushes total stocks to 16.1% of the 5-year seasonal range, indicating a lean inventory environment heading into the summer.
*   **PADD 3 Highlight:** The Gulf Coast remains the primary buffer at 36.2% of its seasonal range. The divergence between PADD 3 (surplus) and PADD 2 (deficit at -2.2%) suggests internal logistical bottlenecks or localized demand spikes in the Midwest.

## 2. Gasoline Positioning
*   **Seasonal Tightness:** Despite a WoW build of 3.4 MMBbl, total stocks are -2.7% below the 5-year seasonal floor.
*   **Demand Signal:** The severe deficit in PADD 3 (-22.8%) is a critical red flag, suggesting that Gulf Coast refining output is struggling to keep pace with summer driving season demand.

## 3. Distillate Positioning
*   **Seasonal Tightness:** Distillates mirror gasoline with a -1.6% seasonal position.
*   **Demand Signal:** The modest 1.5 MMBbl build is insufficient to move the needle. With most PADDs showing positive but low percentages, the market remains vulnerable to any diesel demand surge or refinery outages.

## 4. Cross-Product Divergences
*   **Crude vs. Products:** We are seeing a "squeeze" pattern. Crude is being drawn down while refined products remain below seasonal norms. This suggests high refinery utilization rates (runs) are attempting to cure the product deficit, which provides strong fundamental support for WTI.

## 5. Price Implications
*   **WTI:** **Bullish.** The combination of a significant crude draw and critically low product inventories creates a strong floor for prices.
*   **Refined Products:** **Bullish.** Gasoline and Diesel are trading in a deficit relative to historical norms; any further draws next week will likely trigger price spikes.

## 6. Watchlist for Next Wednesday
**PADD 3 Gasoline Stocks:** Monitor if the Gulf Coast deficit deepens. If PADD 3 continues to slide while NUS totals build, it indicates a regional crisis that could drive up national gasoline benchmarks.
