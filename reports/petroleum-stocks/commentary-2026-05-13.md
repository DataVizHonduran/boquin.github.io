### 1. Crude Oil Positioning
*   **NUS Total:** A healthy 4.3 MMBbl draw brings total stocks to 452.9 MMBbl. Positioning at 29% of the 5-year range suggests a lean inventory environment heading into the summer.
*   **PADD 3 Highlight:** The Gulf Coast remains the primary anchor of US stocks (263.2 MMBbl). At 60.2% of its seasonal range, PADD 3 is significantly more flushed than the Midwest (10.9%) or West Coast (10.1%), suggesting that while national levels are low, the refining hub is well-supplied.

### 2. Gasoline Positioning
*   **Seasonal Tightness:** Gasoline is in a deficit, sitting 7.6% below the 5-year seasonal norm. 
*   **Demand Signal:** The 4.1 MMBbl draw indicates strong early-season driving demand. The critical weakness is in **PADD 3 (-18.3%)**, signaling that the primary export and distribution hub is running lean, which typically supports higher RBOB prices.

### 3. Distillate Positioning
*   **Seasonal Tightness:** Stocks are slightly below the 5-year norm (-2.9%).
*   **Demand Signal:** A marginal build of 0.2 MMBbl suggests a balance between diesel demand and refinery output. However, the **West Coast (PADD 5)** is severely tight at -30.9% of its seasonal range, indicating localized diesel scarcity.

### 4. Cross-Product Divergences
*   **Gasoline vs. Distillates:** We are seeing a clear "summer shift." Gasoline is drawing aggressively to meet seasonal demand, while distillates have flattened out.
*   **Regional Divergence:** PADD 3 is the "inverse" hub—it is relatively oversupplied in crude (60.2%) but undersupplied in gasoline (-18.3%), suggesting refineries are processing heavily but struggling to keep pace with gasoline outflows.

### 5. Price Implications
*   **WTI:** **Bullish.** Low national crude stocks and consistent draws support a price floor.
*   **Refined Products:** **Strongly Bullish.** Gasoline is the primary driver here; sub-seasonal levels combined with PADD 3 tightness should push crack spreads higher.

### 6. Watchlist for Next Wednesday
*   **PADD 3 Gasoline Recovery:** Watch if the Gulf Coast gasoline deficit deepens. If PADD 3 continues to draw while crude remains high, expect a spike in gasoline futures.
