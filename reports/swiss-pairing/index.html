Lose a few, then the wins come

In a Swiss tournament nobody gets knocked out. Every round, you play a kid who has the same number of points as you. So the games keep getting closer to just right.

Same points play each other

Kids who keep losing meet kids who keep losing. Kids who keep winning meet each other. Soon everyone plays someone at their level.

The ratings are real: every kid on the King Registration entry list for the chosen grade section, as of Nov 19, 2026. Rafael starts at 710, his live rating on Glitchess after his Nov 15 tournament (the entry list still shows 581). The games haven't been played yet, so who wins each one is decided by chance, weighted by rating, the way chess ratings predict. Opponent names are made up. Unrated kids count as 400. The field will grow until the event closes registration. Draws come from the ratings too: they are likelier when two kids are close in rating, and when both are stronger (about 4 games in 100 for two 600s, 13 in 100 for two 1800s). Type a different rating in the box to see what changes. The bar chart replays the tournament 2,000 times for that rating and counts Rafael's final points (a win is 1, a draw is ½).
