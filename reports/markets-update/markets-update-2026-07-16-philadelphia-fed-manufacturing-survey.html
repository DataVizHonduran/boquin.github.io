We view this as a signal for the Federal Reserve to remain cautious; while the headline data may discourage immediate easing, the lack of forward-looking demand prevents a bullish case for further tightening.

  • Negative Forward Momentum: The failure of New Orders to trend upward despite the activity surge suggests a looming contraction in production as the "buffer" of old orders evaporates.

(i) Growth: Current growth is superficially robust but structurally hollow. The jump from 10.3 in June to 41.4 in July represents a massive acceleration in output, yet the lack of corresponding new order growth indicates this is a temporary surge rather than a sustainable expansion.

(iii) Inflation: The surge in activity to the 98.3rd percentile suggests significant upward pressure on input costs and pricing power in the immediate term. However, the persistent weakness in new orders acts as a long-term deflationary drag, limiting the duration of any price spikes.
