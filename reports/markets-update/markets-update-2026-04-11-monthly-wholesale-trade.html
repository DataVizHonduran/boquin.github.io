1. Executive Summary

The latest wholesale trade data reveals a robust demand environment characterized by a widening divergence between sales growth and inventory accumulation. Wholesale sales continued their upward trajectory into January 2026, while inventories saw a notable monthly contraction, suggesting a lean-inventory regime and strong B2B throughput.

Overall, the data signals a "demand-pull" scenario. The combination of accelerating sales and disciplined inventory management indicates that businesses are responding to genuine end-user demand rather than speculative stockpiling, reducing the risk of a future "bullwhip effect" correction.

2. Five Main Views

  • Robust Top-Line Growth: Wholesale sales grew by 7.5% year-over-year (YoY), rising from $676.6bn in January 2025 to $727.5bn in January 2026, confirming sustained commercial activity.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The data describes a growth environment that is resilient and accelerating. The consistent MoM increase in sales throughout the latter half of 2025, culminating in a $727.5bn print for January 2026, suggests that the real economy is absorbing goods and services at a healthy clip.

(ii) Labor Market: While not directly measured, the sustained growth in wholesale sales volumes implies a stable-to-tight labor market within the logistics and distribution sectors. The need to move higher volumes of goods with leaner inventories typically requires high operational efficiency and steady staffing levels.

(iii) Inflation: The divergence between sales (+7.5% YoY) and inventory growth (+1.0% YoY) suggests that price increases are being passed through the supply chain effectively. The lack of inventory buildup suggests that inflation is being driven by demand rather than supply-side bottlenecks or "just-in-case" hoarding.

4. Policy Outlook

Given the strength of the wholesale sales data and the efficiency of inventory turnover, the balance of risks has shifted toward economic overheating rather than recession. The data suggests that the private sector is not yet feeling the restrictive weight of current monetary policy.

Forecast: We expect the Federal Reserve to maintain a "Hawkish Hold" at the next meeting. With sales growing at a robust 7.5% YoY and no sign of a demand slump, there is no immediate catalyst for rate cuts. We project the Fed will keep rates steady for at least the next two meetings to ensure inflation is fully anchored before considering any easing, as the current data provides no evidence of a growth slowdown.
