3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The data depicts a significant drag on GDP. Given the multiplier effect of home sales on furniture, appliances, and financial services, a print at the 7.7th percentile of the last decade suggests a sharp contraction in residential investment and consumption.

(ii) Labor Market: While home sales are a lagging indicator of income, the severity of this drop suggests a decline in consumer confidence and potentially a tightening of mortgage credit standards, which often precedes a broader softening in employment within the construction and real estate sectors.

(iii) Inflation: This print is decisively disinflationary. The collapse in transaction volume reduces pressure on home prices and slows the growth of "Owners' Equivalent Rent" (OER), providing the Fed with significant headroom to pivot.
