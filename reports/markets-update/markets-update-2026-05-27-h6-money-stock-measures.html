1. Executive Summary

The latest H.6 release reveals a consistent and accelerating expansion in US monetary aggregates, with M2 reaching a 10-year peak of $22.80 trillion in April 2026. The data indicates a decisive reversal of the quantitative tightening (QT) era, characterized by a steady monthly climb in liquidity that suggests a broadening of the monetary base.

The overall tone is one of increasing systemic liquidity. The synchronized rise in both M1 and M2 suggests that the "liquidity drain" has not only ceased but has transitioned into a phase of active expansion, which may create headwinds for the Federal Reserve’s inflation mandates if not balanced by productivity gains.

2. Five Main Views

  • Peak Liquidity Levels: M2 has hit the 100th percentile of its 10-year range, signaling that the economy is operating at the absolute ceiling of historical monetary availability.
  • Accelerating Momentum: M2 grew by approximately $1.18 trillion (5.2%) over the 13-month period from April 2025 to April 2026, with the most aggressive jump occurring between January and February 2026 (+$197.7bn).
  • M1-M2 Convergence: M1 is tracking closely with M2, rising from $18.62tn to $19.53tn, indicating that the liquidity is residing primarily in highly liquid deposits rather than shifting toward longer-term savings instruments.
  • Positive Drift: The Z-score of +1.30$\sigma$ for M2 confirms a sustained upward deviation from the long-term mean, though it remains below the critical +2.0$\sigma$ threshold for an extreme outlier event.
  • Monetary Impulse: The consistent month-on-month growth (averaging ~0.4% per month) suggests a persistent monetary impulse that is likely to support nominal spending in the coming quarters.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The steady expansion of M2 is a bullish signal for nominal GDP. The increase in available money stock typically precedes a rise in aggregate demand, suggesting that the growth trajectory is being supported by a robust liquidity backdrop.

(ii) Labor Market: While M2 is a lagging indicator for employment, the abundance of liquidity often correlates with corporate capacity for expansion and payroll growth. The lack of monetary contraction suggests there is no immediate "liquidity crunch" that would force systemic layoffs.

(iii) Inflation: This is the primary area of concern. The transition of M2 to its 100th percentile suggests a potential "over-supply" of money. If this monetary expansion outpaces the growth of real goods and services, it will likely exert upward pressure on core inflation.

4. Cyclical Alignment

Based on the 10-year Z-scores (M2: +1.30$\sigma$; M1: +0.90$\sigma$) and the M2 100th percentile ranking, the current regime is classified as Late-Cycle Overheating. While the Z-scores have not yet breached the +2.0$\sigma$ "regime shift" threshold, the fact that M2 is at the absolute top of its 10-year range indicates a saturation of liquidity. This alignment is typical of a late-cycle environment where monetary abundance fuels nominal growth but increases the risk of an inflationary spike.

5. Policy Outlook

The data suggests the Federal Reserve is facing a tightening "policy box." With M2 at a 10-year high and accelerating, the risk of an inflationary overshoot now outweighs the risk of a growth slowdown.

Forecast: We expect the Fed to maintain a Hawkish Hold or consider a 25bps hike in the next 2-3 meetings. The balance of risks has shifted; the Fed cannot justify further easing—or even a neutral stance—while the money stock is hitting 100th percentile levels. We anticipate the Fed will prioritize "draining the pool" to prevent the current late-cycle liquidity from crystallizing into entrenched inflation.
