Subject: CPI Analysis: Energy Shock Masks Core Disinflation

1. Executive Summary

The March 2026 CPI print reveals a stark divergence between headline volatility and underlying price stability. While the headline index surged by 0.86% month-on-month (MoM), this acceleration is almost exclusively driven by a violent spike in energy and transportation costs. The data suggests a supply-side shock rather than a broad-based resurgence of demand-pull inflation.

For the Federal Reserve, the signal is mixed. While the headline year-on-year (YoY) print has climbed to 3.28%, the Core CPI—which strips out the volatile food and energy components—remains significantly more anchored at 2.60% YoY. We expect the FOMC to "look through" the energy volatility, though the magnitude of the transportation jump may raise concerns regarding second-round effects on freight and logistics.

2. Five Main Views

  • Energy-Driven Headline Spike: Headline CPI rose 0.86% MoM in March, driven by an extraordinary 10.87% MoM surge in the Energy index, which now stands at 12.59% YoY.
  • Core Stability: Core CPI remains the primary anchor for policy, rising only 0.20% MoM and maintaining a disciplined 2.60% YoY trajectory, suggesting the "last mile" of disinflation is still intact.
  • Transportation Contagion: The Energy shock has bled directly into Transportation, which spiked 4.28% MoM in March, contributing significantly to the headline overshoot.
