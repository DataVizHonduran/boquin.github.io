3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: While PCE is a price index, the steady decline in trimmed mean inflation—which strips out volatile outliers—suggests a cooling of aggregate demand. The absence of price stickiness in the most recent prints implies that growth is likely moderating toward a sustainable trend rather than overheating.

(ii) Labor Market: The data suggests a softening of the wage-price spiral. The transition of 1-month PCE from >3% in Q2 to <2% in Q3 indicates that service-sector pricing power is waning, likely reflecting a more balanced labor market with diminishing nominal wage pressure.

(iii) Inflation: Inflation is characterized by a successful "mean reversion." We have moved from a period of high volatility (1-month range of 1.52% to 3.20%) to a period of stability. The 12-month trend is now firmly anchored, moving from the 50th percentile of the 10-year range toward the lower half.

4. Cyclical Alignment

Based on the provided Z-scores (-0.44$\sigma$ for 12M; -0.61$\sigma$ for 1M) and the 50th percentile ranking, the current regime is classified as a 'mid-cycle' pause.

We are not seeing the extreme positive Z-scores (>2.0) associated with late-cycle overheating, nor the extreme negative Z-scores associated with a structural regime shift or deflationary shock. Instead, the data describes a "normalization phase" where inflation is returning to its long-term historical average, suggesting the economy is transitioning from a restrictive environment to a neutral one.

5. Policy Outlook

Forecast: 25bps Rate Cut | Timing: Next FOMC Meeting

The balance of risks has shifted decisively toward the downside.
