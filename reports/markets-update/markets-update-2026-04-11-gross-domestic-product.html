Subject: US GDP Analysis: Growth Stalls as Inflation Persistence Limits Policy Room

1. Executive Summary

The latest GDP data reveals a significant deceleration in economic momentum, with Real GDP growing by a marginal 0.12% QoQ in the final quarter of 2025. While the economy continues to expand on a year-over-year basis (+1.99%), the trajectory suggests a transition from a robust post-pandemic recovery to a period of stagnation. The primary concern for policymakers is the "sticky" nature of price pressures, as the GDP Deflator continues its steady ascent, complicating the path toward a neutral monetary stance.

The overall tone is one of fragility. The divergence between cooling growth and persistent inflation suggests the economy is entering a narrow corridor where the Federal Reserve must balance the risk of a growth collapse against the risk of entrenched inflation.

2. Five Main Views

  • Growth Momentum is Evaporating: Real GDP growth has slowed to a near-standstill, recording just 0.12% growth in the most recent quarter (24,055.7bn vs. 24,026.8bn), down sharply from the 1.05% growth seen between Q2 and Q3 2025.
  • Inflation Remains Entrenched: The GDP Deflator has risen to 130.624, representing a 0.92% increase QoQ and a 3.30% increase YoY (from 126.450 in Oct 2024), indicating that price pressures are not yet aligned with the Fed's 2% target.
  • Private Investment as the Sole Engine: Real Private Investment remains a relative bright spot, growing 0.57% QoQ and 2.15% YoY (4,408.3bn vs. 4,315.6bn), suggesting that corporate capital expenditure is providing a floor for the economy.
  • Fiscal Drag Emerging: Real Government Consumption has turned contractionary, falling 1.44% QoQ (3,957.1bn vs. 4,015.0bn) and declining 1.17% YoY, signaling a tightening of public spending that is weighing on aggregate demand.
  • Trade Balance Remains a Headwind: The trade deficit continues to drag on GDP; while imports fell 0.2% QoQ, exports declined more sharply by 1.1% QoQ, widening the net trade drag.
