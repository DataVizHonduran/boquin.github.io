1. Executive Summary

The latest data indicates a transition from a restrictive easing cycle to a period of policy stabilization. After a consistent downward trajectory in the Effective Fed Funds Rate (EFFR) throughout the second half of 2025, the Federal Reserve has established a new target range of 3.50% to 3.75%.

The overall tone is one of "calculated pause." The shift from the 4.33% plateau seen in early 2025 to the current 3.64% effective rate suggests the FOMC has successfully navigated a landing phase, moving away from aggressive cuts to a neutral or near-neutral stance to calibrate the long-term equilibrium rate.

2. Five Main Views

  • Cycle Transition: The easing cycle that began in September 2025 has concluded, with the target rate now locked at 3.50%–3.75%.
  • Aggressive Late-2025 Descent: The EFFR saw a sharp decline from 4.33% in August 2025 to 3.72% by December 2025, representing a 61bps drop in just four months.
  • Effective Rate Convergence: The EFFR has stabilized at 3.64% as of March 2026, sitting comfortably within the new target range.
  • End of Volatility: The stability of the target rates throughout March and April 2026 signals a high degree of FOMC confidence in the current policy setting.
  • Real Rate Calibration: The move from 4.33% to 3.64% (a total reduction of 69bps) suggests a shift toward supporting growth without reigniting inflationary pressures.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The aggressive rate cuts in Q4 2025 suggest that the Fed perceived a risk of growth deceleration or a restrictive real rate burden. The current stabilization at 3.64% implies that growth has likely returned to a sustainable trend, removing the urgency for further stimulus.

(ii) Labor Market: While direct employment data is not provided, the trajectory of the EFFR—specifically the acceleration of cuts between September (4.22%) and December (3.72%)—points to a period where the Fed was likely reacting to softening labor market conditions to prevent a hard landing.

(iii) Inflation: The decision to halt cuts at 3.50%–3.75% suggests that inflation is likely approaching the 2% target or has stabilized sufficiently to allow the Fed to stop easing. The Fed is now avoiding the risk of "over-easing," which could trigger a secondary inflationary spike.

4. Fiscal Commentary

The stabilization of the Fed Funds Rate at 3.64% increases the importance of fiscal policy in driving GDP growth. With monetary policy now in a "hold" pattern, any significant fiscal expansion would risk putting upward pressure on inflation, potentially forcing the Fed to abandon this plateau. Conversely, fiscal tightening would now be the primary lever for cooling the economy, as the monetary "easy win" of rate cuts has been exhausted.

5. Policy Outlook

Forecast: Hold

We expect the FOMC to maintain the target range of 3.50%–3.75% for the next two quarters. The data shows absolute stability in the target rates through April 2026. Given that the EFFR has converged to 3.64%, the balance of risks has shifted from "growth preservation" to "inflation vigilance." Unless there is a significant exogenous shock to the labor market, we see no catalyst for further cuts or a pivot to hikes in the immediate term.
