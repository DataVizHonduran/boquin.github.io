3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The data describes a growth environment characterized by high "churn" and entrepreneurial optimism. The fact that applications remain well above the 10-year median suggests a strong belief in future demand and market opportunities, supporting a positive outlook for long-term productivity.

(ii) Labor Market: The labor market is likely experiencing sustained upward pressure. High business application volumes typically translate into increased hiring needs for entry-level and managerial roles, contributing to the "tightness" of the employment landscape and supporting nominal wage growth.

(iii) Inflation: From an inflationary lens, these figures are "hawkish." High business formation increases the demand for commercial real estate, equipment, and labor—all of which are cost-push drivers that can keep inflation sticky even as headline indices fluctuate.

5. Policy Outlook

The data supports a "Higher for Longer" stance. Given that business formation—a leading indicator of economic activity and labor demand—remains in the 90th percentile, the Fed has little incentive to pivot toward aggressive easing. The balance of risks is tilted toward persistent inflation driven by structural demand for resources.
