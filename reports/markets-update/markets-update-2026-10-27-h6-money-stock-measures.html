3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The data suggests a robust, liquidity-driven expansion. The consistent monthly increases in M2—particularly the jump from April ($22.73tn) to May ($22.98tn)—point to a surge in nominal aggregate demand that likely supports strong headline GDP growth.

(ii) Labor Market: While H.6 is a monetary measure, the expansion of M1 and M2 typically supports higher corporate payrolls and wage growth as liquidity eases the cost of capital for firms and increases consumer purchasing power.

(iii) Inflation: The outlook is skewed to the upside. The fact that M2 is at its 10-year ceiling suggests that the "monetary overhang" is returning, which creates a structural floor for inflation and may prevent a return to the 2% target in the near term.

4. Cyclical Alignment

With M2 at the 100th percentile and a Z-score of +1.37$\sigma$, the current regime is classified as late-cycle overheating. While the Z-score has not yet crossed the +2.0$\sigma$ "regime shift" threshold, the absolute positioning at the top of the 10-year range indicates that the economy is operating in a high-liquidity environment that is unsustainable without triggering significant price corrections or aggressive policy intervention.

5. Policy Outlook

Forecast: Hawkish Pivot / Rate Hike

Timing: Q4 2026

The balance of risks has shifted decisively toward inflation. The Fed cannot ignore a money stock that is hitting 10-year highs while simultaneously accelerating. Given that M2 grew by roughly $1.26 trillion in a single year, the Fed is likely to view this as a primary driver of nominal overheating. We expect the FOMC to move toward a rate hike or a "higher-for-longer" stance in the next meeting to drain excess liquidity and curb the inflationary impulse generated by this monetary surge.
