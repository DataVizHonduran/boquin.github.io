Subject: PCE Analysis: Consumption Resilience Amid Sticky Inflation

1. Executive Summary

The latest PCE data reveals a US economy characterized by surprising consumption resilience and persistent inflationary pressures. While headline inflation remains below 3%, the Core PCE index continues to track higher, suggesting that underlying price pressures are not yet converging toward the Fed's 2% target. The data indicates a widening gap between nominal spending growth and income growth, fueled by a steady erosion of the personal saving rate.

Overall, the policy signal is hawkish. The combination of robust real-time spending (0.48% MoM in February) and a sticky core inflation print (2.97% YoY) limits the Federal Reserve's flexibility to pivot toward easing. We view the current macro environment as "overheated" in the consumption sector, which will likely necessitate a "higher-for-longer" interest rate regime to cool aggregate demand.

2. Five Main Views

  • Inflation Persistence: Headline PCE is printing at 2.80% YoY, but Core PCE is stickier at 2.97% YoY, indicating that the removal of volatile food and energy components reveals a more stubborn inflationary trend.
  • Consumption Outpacing Income: Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) grew by 5.33% YoY, significantly outstripping the 3.72% YoY growth in Personal Income, suggesting a reliance on balance sheet drawdowns.
  • Savings Erosion: The Personal Saving Rate has trended downward from 5.2% in February 2025 to 4.0% in February 2026, confirming that consumers are sacrificing future security to maintain current living standards.
  • Momentum Acceleration: February 2026 saw a notable MoM acceleration in the PCE Price Index (+0.37%) and Core PCE (+0.36%), suggesting that inflation is not decelerating in a linear fashion.
  • Income Plateau: Personal Income showed its first meaningful stagnation/dip in the recent cycle, falling 0.07% MoM in February, which may signal a cooling in the labor market's pricing power.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: Economic growth remains aggressively driven by the consumer. With PCE spending increasing from \$20.52tn to \$21.62tn over the last 12 months (+5.33%), the domestic demand engine is firing strongly. However, the quality of this growth is questionable, as it is increasingly decoupled from income growth.

(ii) Labor Market: While we lack direct payroll data, Personal Income serves as a proxy for labor market health. The 3.72% YoY increase in income suggests a generally tight market, but the February MoM contraction (-0.07%) is a cautionary signal that wage growth may be peaking or that hours worked are beginning to soften.
