1. Executive Summary

The latest Dallas Fed Trimmed Mean PCE data confirms a sustained disinflationary trajectory, with the 12-month rate continuing its descent toward the Federal Reserve's 2% target. The February 2026 print reveals a significant cooling in monthly price pressures, suggesting that the "sticky" components of inflation—which the trimmed mean is designed to isolate—are finally yielding to restrictive monetary policy.

The overall tone is dovish. The convergence of the 12-month rate toward 2.3% and a monthly print dipping below the 2% threshold indicates that the risk of an inflation rebound is diminishing, shifting the Fed's primary concern from price stability to the prevention of an over-tightened policy stance.

2. Five Main Views

  • Sustained 12-Month Decline: The 12-month trimmed mean PCE has fallen from a peak of 2.80% in August 2025 to 2.33% in February 2026, representing a 47bps contraction in the annual trend over six months.
  • Sharp Monthly Deceleration: The February 2026 1-month print of 1.82% marks a sharp reversal from January’s 2.71%, a month-on-month drop of 89bps that suggests a rapid cooling of underlying price momentum.
  • Consistent Downward Momentum: The 12-month rate has declined for three consecutive months (December 2.44% $\rightarrow$ January 2.42% $\rightarrow$ February 2.33%), confirming that the disinflationary process is not a one-off event but a trend.
  • Volatility Compression: While 1-month prints were highly volatile in mid-2025 (peaking at 3.59% in June), the recent readings (October through February) show a stabilization of monthly prints, mostly oscillating between 1.5% and 2.7%.
  • Target Convergence: The gap between the current 12-month trimmed mean (2.33%) and the Fed's 2.0% target has narrowed significantly from the 73bps gap seen in August 2025.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The steady decline in the trimmed mean PCE suggests a cooling of aggregate demand. The transition from 1-month prints averaging above 2.5% in mid-2025 to a February print of 1.82% indicates that consumer spending power is normalizing and the economy is likely shifting from an overheating phase to a more sustainable, slower growth path.

(ii) Labor Market: As the trimmed mean PCE strips out volatile food and energy to focus on the "core" of the economy, the current decline reflects a softening in service-sector pricing. This is a strong proxy for a cooling labor market, suggesting that wage-push inflation is receding and the bargaining power of labor is moderating.

(iii) Inflation: Inflation is in a clear state of deceleration. The 12-month rate has dropped 40bps year-over-year (from 2.73% in Feb 2025 to 2.33% in Feb 2026). The February 1-month print of 1.82% is particularly critical, as it indicates that the current pace of price increases is now running below the Fed's long-term target.

4. Policy Outlook

Based on the data, we forecast a 25bps rate cut at the next FOMC meeting.

The balance of risks has shifted; the probability of an inflation resurgence is now outweighed by the risk of maintaining real rates that are too restrictive as inflation falls. With the 12-month trimmed mean at 2.33% and the most recent monthly momentum at 1.82%, the Fed has sufficient evidence that the "last mile" of disinflation is underway. We expect a series of measured cuts through the first half of 2026 to normalize the federal funds rate in line with a 2% inflation environment.
