Subject: Credit Impulse Analysis: C&I Acceleration and Deposit Stability

1. Executive Summary

The latest H.8 data reveals a notable acceleration in credit extension, led primarily by a surge in Commercial and Industrial (C&I) lending. While real estate credit has effectively plateaued, the sharp uptick in corporate borrowing and steady growth in consumer credit suggest a broadening of economic activity and a strengthening of the credit impulse.

From a policy perspective, this trend signals a resilient private sector that is increasingly comfortable with current borrowing costs. The simultaneous rise in total deposits suggests that banking system liquidity remains ample, reducing the likelihood of a credit crunch but potentially fueling inflationary pressures through increased aggregate demand.

2. Five Main Views

  • C&I Loans are Accelerating: Business borrowing has shifted into high gear, with March 2026 figures showing a +1.4% MoM increase and a robust +5.7% YoY expansion (from \$2,675.3bn to \$2,827.9bn).
  • Real Estate Stagnation: Real estate loans have hit a ceiling, recording a negligible +0.01% MoM change in March 2026, reflecting the continued impact of higher long-term rates on mortgage and commercial property demand.
  • Resilient Consumer Credit: Consumer borrowing remains on a steady upward trajectory, growing +3.5% YoY to \$1,886.7bn, indicating that household balance sheets are supporting continued spending.
  • Liquidity Buffer Expanding: Total deposits continue to climb, reaching \$19,084.6bn in April 2026, a +0.47% MoM increase from March, ensuring banks have the funding capacity to support the current loan growth.
  • Divergent Credit Drivers: The credit impulse is now decoupled; growth is being driven by corporate investment and consumer resilience rather than the housing market.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The data points to a strengthening growth outlook. The acceleration in C&I loans—particularly the jump from \$2,738.1bn in January to \$2,827.9bn in March—suggests a ramp-up in capital expenditure (CapEx) and business operations. This corporate-led expansion is the primary engine of current GDP support.

(ii) Labor Market: While H.8 is a balance sheet release, the correlation between C&I loan growth and payroll expansion is high. The +5.7% YoY increase in business lending typically precedes increased hiring and operational scaling, suggesting the labor market will remain tight as firms expand capacity.

(iii) Inflation: The credit environment is currently skewed toward the inflationary side. The combination of rising consumer credit (+3.5% YoY) and accelerating business loans increases the money supply in the real economy, which may sustain price pressures and complicate the path toward a 2% inflation target.

4. Policy Outlook

Given the acceleration in C&I lending and the stability of the deposit base, the Federal Reserve has limited incentive to aggressively ease policy. The "credit impulse" is currently positive, meaning the private sector is doing the heavy lifting for growth, reducing the need for monetary stimulus.

Forecast: We expect the Fed to maintain a "Hold" or "Hawkish Pause" stance at the next meeting. While the stagnation in real estate loans prevents a systemic crisis, the heat in corporate and consumer borrowing suggests that premature cuts could reignite inflation. We project no rate cuts until a clear deceleration in C&I loan growth is observed.
