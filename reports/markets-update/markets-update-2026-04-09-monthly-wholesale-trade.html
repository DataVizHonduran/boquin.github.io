1. Executive Summary

The latest wholesale trade data reveals a robust demand environment coupled with an aggressive shift toward leaner inventory management. Wholesale sales continued their upward trajectory into January 2026, while inventories saw a notable monthly contraction, suggesting that wholesalers are successfully aligning stock levels with strong end-market demand.

The overall tone is one of resilience. The divergence between rising sales and falling inventories indicates a healthy "pull-through" effect in the supply chain, reducing the risk of a bullwhip-induced glut. For policymakers, this suggests that aggregate demand remains firm, potentially limiting the urgency for monetary easing.

2. Five Main Views

  • Robust Top-Line Growth: Wholesale sales grew by 7.5% year-on-year (Jan '25: \$676.6bn to Jan '26: \$727.5bn), signaling sustained commercial activity.
  • Inventory Destocking: Following a peak in November 2025 (\$915.7bn), inventories contracted by 0.55% month-on-month in January 2026, falling to \$909.3bn.
  • Improving Efficiency: The inventory-to-sales ratio has compressed from 1.33 in January 2025 to 1.25 in January 2026, reflecting higher capital efficiency and faster turnover.
  • Positive Momentum: January 2026 sales rose 0.5% month-on-month from December (\$723.9bn), confirming that the year-end rally was not merely seasonal but a continuing trend.
  • Controlled Accumulation: Despite the January dip, inventories remain 1.0% higher year-on-year, suggesting a baseline level of stock replenishment is maintaining a safety buffer against supply shocks.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The data points to a strong growth regime. The consistent rise in wholesale sales—which serve as a leading indicator for broader GDP—suggests that business-to-business (B2B) demand is accelerating. The fact that sales are growing significantly faster than inventories (+7.5% YoY vs +1.0% YoY) indicates that growth is being driven by actual consumption rather than speculative stockpiling.

(ii) Labor Market: While this dataset does not provide direct employment figures, the sustained increase in sales volume typically correlates with stable or expanding headcount in logistics, warehousing, and distribution. The transition to leaner inventory ratios suggests a shift toward "just-in-time" efficiency, which may stabilize labor demand rather than drive aggressive hiring.

(iii) Inflation: The strength of the sales data presents a potential inflationary headwind. Robust demand in the wholesale sector often translates to pricing power for suppliers. With sales continuing to climb MoM (+0.5% in Jan), there is a risk that demand-pull inflation remains sticky in the intermediate goods sector, potentially filtering through to the CPI.

4. Policy Outlook

Based on the current data, we expect the Federal Reserve to maintain a hawkish hold or adopt a very cautious approach to rate cuts. The balance of risks has shifted toward inflation rather than growth; the strong 7.5% YoY growth in wholesale sales suggests the economy is not yet signaling the cooling necessary to justify aggressive easing.

We forecast the next move to be a hold at the upcoming meeting, with any potential pivot to cuts delayed until there is clear evidence of a slowdown in sales momentum or a significant spike in inventory accumulation (indicating a demand drop). The current "lean and fast" movement of goods supports a "higher for longer" narrative to ensure inflation is fully extinguished.
