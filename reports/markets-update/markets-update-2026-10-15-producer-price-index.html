Subject: PPI Analysis: Persistent Upward Pressure and Regime Extremes

1. Executive Summary

The latest Producer Price Index (PPI) print confirms a troubling trend of persistent inflationary pressure at the wholesale level. With the PPI All Commodities index reaching the 99.2nd percentile of its 10-year range and Core PPI (ex-food & energy) hitting a 10-year peak (100th percentile), the data suggests that cost-push inflation remains deeply embedded in the supply chain.

The policy signal is clear: the "last mile" of disinflation has stalled. The simultaneous rise in both headline and core measures indicates that price pressures are no longer driven by volatile commodities alone but are now structural, limiting the Federal Reserve's room for monetary easing.

2. Five Main Views

  • Core Inflation Peak: PPI Final Demand ex Food & Energy has reached an absolute 10-year high of 156.172, signaling that core price pressures are accelerating rather than mean-reverting.
  • Accelerating Momentum: The MoM increase in Core PPI from August (155.115) to September (156.172) represents a +0.66% jump, indicating a re-acceleration of wholesale price growth.
  • Headline Volatility: While the All Commodities index saw a brief dip in mid-2026, the recovery to 287.928 in August shows a resilient upward trajectory, remaining well above the 10-year median.
  • Regime Extremes: With Z-scores of +1.80$\sigma$ (Headline) and +1.88$\sigma$ (Core), the current price level is nearly two standard deviations above the decade average, placing the economy in a high-inflation regime.
  • Pass-Through Risk: The sustained climb in Core PPI suggests a high probability of these costs being passed through to the Consumer Price Index (CPI) in the coming quarters.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: The data suggests a high-nominal-growth environment. The consistent rise in producer prices, particularly the climb from 261.333 in Dec '25 to 287.928 in Aug '26, reflects strong aggregate demand that is continuing to outpace supply capacity.

(ii) Labor Market: While PPI is a price measure, the persistence of core inflation (100th percentile) typically correlates with a tight labor market where wage-push inflation is fueling higher production costs, creating a feedback loop of rising input prices.

(iii) Inflation: Inflation is currently characterized by "stickiness" and acceleration. The Core PPI's move to the 100th percentile of its 10-year range indicates that inflation is no longer transitory or energy-led, but is now broad-based across final demand categories.

4. Cyclical Alignment

Based on the 10-year Z-scores (+1.80$\sigma$ to +1.88$\sigma$) and the fact that Core PPI is at the 100th percentile, the current regime is classified as late-cycle overheating. While the Z-scores have not yet crossed the $|2.0\sigma|$ threshold for a definitive structural regime shift, the proximity to that level combined with the absolute 10-year peak suggests the economy is operating beyond its sustainable potential, characteristic of the final stages of a credit/growth cycle.

5. Policy Outlook

Forecast: Hawkish Hold / Potential Rate Hike

The balance of risks has shifted decisively toward inflation overshoot. Given that Core PPI is at a 10-year high and accelerating (+0.66% MoM), the Fed cannot justify any rate cuts in the near term. We expect the FOMC to maintain the current restrictive stance (Hold) for the next meeting, with a high probability of a 25bps hike if the next CPI print confirms the PPI pass-through. The Fed is now forced to prioritize price stability over growth concerns, as the "late-cycle" signals are becoming undeniable.
