1. Executive Summary

The latest industrial production (IP) data reveals a robust expansionary trend, with the headline index reaching the 95.8th percentile of its 10-year range. While manufacturing shows a steady, moderate recovery, the primary driver of current growth is a parabolic surge in utilities production, which has reached a decade-high peak.

The overall tone is one of resilience, though the divergence between manufacturing and utilities suggests that the "industrial" recovery is uneven. From a policy perspective, the lack of significant capacity constraints (TCU remains near the median) suggests that this growth is not yet fueling systemic inflationary pressures, providing the Fed with some breathing room despite the high headline IP levels.

2. Five Main Views

  • Headline Strength: The Industrial Production Index rose to 103.951 in September 2026, representing a +0.66% MoM increase and a sustained climb from the 101.034 trough seen in November 2025.
  • Utilities Outlier: Utilities production is in a state of extreme expansion, printing at 117.451—the absolute 100th percentile of the 10-year range—driven by a significant jump from August (113.266).
  • Manufacturing Recovery: Manufacturing production (IPMAN) has recovered from a low of 96.986 (Dec '25) to 99.436, a steady climb that indicates a return to growth, though it remains well below its historical ceiling of 102.04.
  • Underutilized Capacity: Despite the high IP index, Total Capacity Utilization (TCU) sits at 76.85%, which is nearly the 10-year median (51.7th percentile), suggesting significant slack remains in the industrial base.
  • Momentum Shift: The data shows a clear acceleration phase starting in April 2026, with consecutive monthly gains in the headline index through September.

3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: Industrial growth is currently aggressive but skewed. The headline index's Z-score of +1.26$\sigma$ indicates strong above-trend growth. However, the growth is heavily concentrated in the utilities sector rather than a broad-based industrial renaissance.

(ii) Labor Market: While direct employment data is not provided, the steady rise in Manufacturing Production (up ~2.5% since Dec '25) and the surge in Utilities suggest tightening labor demand within these specific industrial verticals to support increased output.

(iii) Inflation: The capacity utilization data (Z-score +0.09$\sigma$) is the key inflation signal here. Because TCU is not overheating (remaining far below the 79.93% ceiling), the current increase in production is unlikely to trigger supply-side "bottleneck" inflation.

4. Cyclical Alignment

The current regime is classified as a mid-cycle expansion.

While the headline IP index is at the 95.8th percentile, the critical "overheating" metric—Capacity Utilization—is almost exactly at its 10-year average (51.7th percentile). A "late-cycle" designation would require TCU to be pushing toward the 80% mark. The extreme Z-score in Utilities (+3.43$\sigma$) is a significant regime-defining event, suggesting a structural shift in energy demand or infrastructure utilization rather than a cyclical peak of the broader economy.

5. Policy Outlook

Next Move: Hold / Neutral

The balance of risks currently favors a "wait-and-see" approach. The Fed is faced with strong production growth (bullish for GDP) but no evidence of capacity constraints (neutral for inflation). Given that TCU is only +0.09$\sigma$ from the mean, there is no immediate pressure to hike rates to cool the industrial sector. Conversely, the strong momentum in IP and Manufacturing suggests the economy is not sliding toward a recession, removing the urgency for aggressive cuts. We expect the Fed to maintain current rates in the next meeting, monitoring whether the utilities surge translates into broader inflationary pressure.
