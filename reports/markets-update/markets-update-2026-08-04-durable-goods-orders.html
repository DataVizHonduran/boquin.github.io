3. Macro Characterization

(i) Growth: Growth is currently in an expansionary phase driven by fixed investment. The consistent MoM climb in Core Capex suggests that firms are aggressively investing in productivity-enhancing equipment, which provides a strong floor for GDP growth.

(ii) Labor Market: While direct employment data is not provided, the surge in capital goods orders typically precedes increased labor demand for installation, maintenance, and operation of new capacity, suggesting continued tightness in skilled technical labor markets.

(iii) Inflation: The data points toward persistent demand-pull inflation. When capital goods orders hit the 100th percentile of a decade-long range, it typically leads to bottlenecks in supply chains and upward pressure on producer prices (PPI), complicating the path to a 2% inflation target.

The current regime is classified as a Structural Regime Shift.

The data exceeds the threshold for simple "late-cycle overheating." With Total Durable Goods at the 98.3rd percentile (Z-score +2.00$\sigma$) and Core Capex at the 100th percentile (Z-score +2.35$\sigma$), we are witnessing a fundamental shift in the investment baseline. This level of sustained, extreme deviation from the 10-year mean suggests a structural reallocation of capital—likely driven by technological transformation (e.g., AI/Automation)—rather than a standard cyclical peak.

5. Policy Outlook

Forecast: Hawkish Hold / Potential Rate Hike

The balance of risks has shifted heavily toward overheating. The Fed cannot justify rate cuts while business investment is operating at a 10-year maximum. Given that Core Capex is now +2.35$\sigma$ above the mean, the risk of "investment-led inflation" is acute.
