## Executive Summary

Chile enters the December 14 presidential runoff with decisively bullish market positioning favoring far-right candidate José Antonio Kast over leftist Jeannette Jara, despite Jara's first-round victory. The Chilean peso rallied 1.8% and the IPSA surged 3.4% following the November 16 election on expectations of fiscal consolidation and investment-friendly policies under a Kast presidency. **Macro fundamentals present a mixed picture**: inflation declined from 4.9% (January) to 3.4% (November), approaching but still above the BCCh's 3% target, while core inflation remains stubbornly elevated at 3.8-4.0% due to sticky services costs and wage pressures. Consensus forecast: Kast wins Dec 14 runoff with 54-58% of the vote, triggering further CLP appreciation and equity rally, but delivery execution risks loom given fragmented right-wing Congress.

## Fiscal & Political Developments

### 2025 Budget and Consolidation Path

Congress approved the 2025 Budget Law in late November 2024, targeting a **structural balance deficit of -1.6% of GDP**, representing a 1.5 percentage point consolidation versus 2024 — the largest since the fiscal rule was applied in 2022. The budget projects:

- **Gross debt:** 42.2% of GDP (end-2025), smallest annual growth since 2007
- **Subsequent deficit targets:** -1.1% (2026), -0.75% (2027), -0.5% (2028), 0.0% (2029)
- **First quarter 2025 execution:** Central Government spending surged 11.8% YoY, prompting a preventive $600+ million cut
  - Total revenue grew 9.6% annually, driven by 16.4% increase in tax revenues

**Rating agency consensus**: S&P, Moody's, and Fitch affirmed Chile's A/A2/A- ratings with stable outlooks, projecting fiscal deficit to narrow from 2.2% of GDP (2025) to 1.3% (2027), with government debt stabilizing around 37-42% of GDP through 2028.

**IMF skepticism**: While the government targets 1.1% deficit in 2025 and 0.5% in 2026, IMF staff considers these "difficult to reach without additional measures," despite welcoming fiscal restraint commitments.

### Codelco Fiscal Burden

State copper giant **Codelco** presents the most significant structural fiscal risk:

- **Debt escalation:** Gross financial debt reached $23.1 billion (end-2024), up 12.1% from $20.6 billion
  - Net debt/EBITDA: 4.1x (improved from ~4.8x but still elevated)
  - $400 million short-term export advance loans secured in December 2024

- **Profitability turnaround:** FY 2024 EBITDA of $5.4 billion (+30% YoY) and net income of $245 million (vs. $591 million loss in 2023)
  - Driven by higher copper prices (averaged $4.15/lb in 2024, +8% YoY) and improved Q4 production

- **Production recovery:** Total copper output 1.33 million tons (+0.3% YoY), breaking multi-year decline
  - December 2024 surge: 172,700 tonnes (+21.6% YoY), strongest monthly performance

- **Fiscal contribution:** $1.534 billion (+7.6% vs. 2023) transferred to government
  - Legal obligations: 70% of profits + 10% of sales to government coffers, throttling reinvestment capacity
  - Historically 10-20% of government revenue depends on Codelco performance

- **Structural investment burden:** $20+ billion projects ongoing (Chuquicamata underground, El Teniente expansion, Andina upgrades)

- **Credit rating:** Moody's Baa1 (negative outlook), S&P/Fitch BBB+ (stable), one notch below Chile sovereign

**Fiscal sustainability stress test**: At $3.50/lb copper, Codelco's Net Debt/EBITDA would spike to 6.4x, likely requiring government capital injections. Current copper price ($4.35/lb actual 2025 avg vs. $4.09/lb budget) provides temporary cushion.

### Pension Reform Implementation

Pension reform achieved historic congressional approval (January 29, 2025, 110-38 vote) after 26 months of negotiation:

**Key Provisions:**
- **6% employer contribution** split between:
  - 3% → Individual AFP accounts
  - 3% → Social Insurance (collective solidarity pool)
- **PGU (Universal Guaranteed Pension) increase** to $250,000 monthly:
  - Sept 2025: $250,000 for ages 82+ ($212,000 for 65-81)
  - Sept 2026: $250,000 for ages 75+
  - Sept 2027: $250,000 for all beneficiaries (65+)

**Fiscal Impact:**
- **2026 cost: $1.2 trillion CLP (~0.4% of GDP annually)**
- 2025 Budget solidarity pillar allocation: $7.09 trillion CLP (~2.4% of GDP)
- Long-term trajectory: Pension spending projected to rise from 2.4% GDP (2025) to 3.5-4.0% GDP (2040) with aging

**Political compromise:**
- AFPs allowed to distribute dividends again (restriction lifted) to secure industry support
- Public AFP proposal abandoned in final bill
- Reform expected to improve median replacement rate from 17% (self-financed) to 29%, total replacement rate (incl. PGU) to 73%

**AFP system metrics (End-2024):**
- Total AUM: $186.4 billion (+10.9% vs. 2023), still 17% below pre-withdrawal levels (~$225B)
- All five funds posted positive 2024 returns: Fund A +9.59%, B +8.00%, C +4.02%
- Reform to add $10-15 billion annually to AUM from employer contributions through 2027

### Structural Constraints

**Chile's economic sovereignty is structurally limited** by deep U.S. integration through USMCA (>80% of exports to U.S.), making the country vulnerable to unilateral U.S. trade policy changes. This dependency constrains independent economic policymaking while the ruling MORENA party's judicial reforms have weakened institutional checks.

**Constitutional impasse persists**: Following rejection of two proposed constitutions (2022, 2023), Chile operates under the amended 1980 Pinochet-era constitution embedding neoliberal economic model. This makes it extremely difficult to address demands for expanded social rights without supermajorities — near-impossible in Chile's fragmented party system. Current reform focus shifted to targeted judicial governance amendments rather than comprehensive constitutional replacement.

### Macroeconomic Implications

**Investment implications — Sectors likely to benefit:**
1. **Mining & Copper Producers:** Bank credit flowing to well-capitalized miners; elevated copper prices ($9,500-$12,000/tonne forecasts) supporting credit quality
2. **Residential Real Estate/Construction:** Mortgage lending stable (+3% YoY); supply shortage supporting prices

4. **Telecommunications:** Banco de Chile expects +102% profit growth for telecom sector within IPSA in 2025

**Sectors likely to suffer:**
1. **Retail/Consumer Discretionary:** 18% consumer credit contraction constraining household purchasing power
2. **SME Services (Non-Tradable):** Weak domestic demand and tight credit standards
3. **Labor-Intensive Industries:** Pension reform employer contribution increases (phased to 8.5% by 2033) raising costs

- **Verdict:** Chilean banks trade at premium valuations justified by quality and capital, but growth upside limited vs. larger LatAm peers; best as defensive quality play
