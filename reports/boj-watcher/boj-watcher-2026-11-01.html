Executive Summary

The Bank of Japan (BOJ) maintained its policy rate at the October 30 MPM, but the meeting marked a pivotal shift in narrative. For the first time since 2013, the BOJ officially declared that the 2% inflation target has been "generally achieved." While Governor Ueda maintained a cautionary tone regarding the sustainability of this achievement, the overall communication signaled that further rate hikes remain on the table. Notably, Deputy Governor Himino signaled readiness to raise borrowing costs, and the BOJ has accelerated its ETF liquidation timeline. The board remains divided, with a clear hawkish contingent pushing for faster normalization as underlying inflation aligns with targets.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The Yen remained under pressure leading up to Governor Ueda's October speeches, suggesting that market participants are closely monitoring the pace of hikes to narrow the interest rate differential.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
Deputy Governor Uchida highlighted that the AI boom may have eased financial conditions, though he warned of associated market risks. This suggests the BOJ is monitoring non-monetary drivers of liquidity.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
The BOJ is accelerating the wind-down of its legacy assets. The tripling of the ETF sales pace (reducing the liquidation window to 43 years) indicates a strong desire to reduce the BOJ's footprint in the equity market.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has shifted from "evaluating the target" to "declaring the target hit." While the October hold was expected, the explicit signal that "further rate hikes" are in play suggests the BOJ is moving toward a terminal rate discussion.
