Executive Summary

The last 30 days mark a historic shift in Japanese monetary policy, with the BOJ raising the policy rate to 1.0% on June 15, 2026—the highest level since 1995. The move was driven by heightened inflation risks and yen volatility. While the Board signaled further tightening guidance, internal divisions are widening. Hawkish members (Tamura, Takata) are pushing for a neutral rate of ~2% and hikes every few months, while new member Toichiro Asada remains a notable dissenter, demanding demand-driven inflation before further hikes. Market expectations for the July meeting are currently skewed toward a hold, though the BOJ remains vigilant regarding inflation overshoot and geopolitical shocks (US-Iran tensions).

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment (CPI, Core, Wages)
The BOJ is increasingly concerned about an "inflation overshoot." May Core CPI (excluding special factors) rose 2.7%, easing slightly from April but remaining well above target. The Board is shifting focus from achieving 2% to preventing an overshoot.

2. Growth Outlook
Reports indicate the BOJ may raise its growth forecasts in the upcoming outlook, providing the macroeconomic justification for higher nominal rates.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The June hike to 1.0% was explicitly linked to "yen and inflation worries." Market volatility and a "slow motion currency crisis" continue to pressure the BOJ to maintain a tightening bias to support the currency.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The move to 1.0% is a 31-year high. While the BOJ is managing the transition, the focus has shifted toward reaching the "neutral rate" (estimated at 2% by Tamura).

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
The BOJ has signaled that the current tapering of JGB purchases is expected to conclude by 2027.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has evolved from "cautious normalization" to "accelerated hikes should inflation risks grow." The BOJ is now explicitly signaling that further rate increases are on the table.
