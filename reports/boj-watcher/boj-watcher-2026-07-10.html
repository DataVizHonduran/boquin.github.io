Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a historic pivot for the Bank of Japan, with the policy rate being raised to 1.0% (the highest since 1995) during the June MPM. The move was driven by heightened inflation risks and yen volatility. While the Board has signaled a commitment to further normalization, a clear divide has emerged: hawks (Tamura, Takata) are pushing for a neutral rate of ~2% and hikes every few months, while the new external member, Toichiro Asada, remains a firm dissenter, demanding evidence of demand-driven inflation. The BOJ is now balancing a "war inflation" shock against the need for sustainable wage-price growth.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment (CPI, Core, Wages)
The BOJ is increasingly concerned about an "inflation overshoot." While real wages rose for a fifth straight month (as of July 7), the pace is slowing. The Board is distinguishing between "war inflation" (cost-push) and the desired demand-driven inflation.

2. Growth Outlook
Reports indicate the BOJ may raise its growth forecasts in upcoming outlooks, providing more headroom for further rate hikes.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
Yen volatility was a primary catalyst for the June hike to 1.0%. The Board remains vigilant regarding the yen's impact on imported inflation.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The move to 1.0% is the most significant tightening in three decades. The focus has shifted to managing the transition to a neutral rate without destabilizing the JGB market.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
Reports indicate the BOJ intends to end its JGB tapering process by next year (2027).

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has shifted from "cautionary" to "active normalization." The BOJ is now explicitly signaling that further hikes are on the table if inflation risks persist.
