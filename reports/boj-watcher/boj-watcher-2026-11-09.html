Executive Summary

The last 30 days have seen a marked shift toward a more hawkish tone within the Bank of Japan. September meeting minutes reveal active internal debates regarding the scope for faster rate hikes, while Governor Ueda has explicitly acknowledged "some upside risks" to inflation. This hawkish tilt is supported by fundamental data: real wages rose for the ninth consecutive month in September, and inflation broke above 2% for the first time this year in October. Notably, Deputy Governor Himino—previously neutral—has signaled readiness to raise borrowing costs. While Governor Ueda remains cautious about the duration of the 2% inflation trend, the consensus is shifting toward a December hike to continue the normalization trajectory.
