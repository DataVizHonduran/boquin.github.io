Executive Summary

The Bank of Japan maintained the policy rate at 1% during the July 31 MPM. While the decision was a "hold," the accompanying communications were notably hawkish. Governor Ueda signaled that the BOJ remains open to faster rate hikes if economic and price projections are met, specifically citing the risks posed by a weakening yen. The yen has neared 40-year lows, creating a "monetary conundrum" where currency-driven inflation risks are clashing with the need for financial stability. Market sentiment is shifting toward a September hike, supported by reports that the BOJ is open to accelerating the normalization pace to counter FX-induced inflation.
