Executive Summary

The past 30 days have highlighted a widening divergence within the BOJ Policy Board. A hawkish contingent, led by External Members Takata and Tamura, is aggressively pushing for faster normalization, with Tamura explicitly identifying the neutral rate at approximately 2% and Takata advocating for hikes every few months. Conversely, the newly appointed Toichiro Asada has solidified the dovish flank, insisting on evidence of demand-driven inflation before supporting further hikes. Governor Ueda remains the central pivot, reiterating risks of inflation exceeding the 2% target while balancing political pressure for restraint from PM Takaichi. Market sentiment remains cautious as the BOJ prepares potential growth forecast upgrades and monitors JGB yield volatility.

Inflation remains a primary driver of the hawkish push. May's Core CPI (excluding special factors) rose 2.7%, though it eased slightly from April. Governor Ueda continues to emphasize the risk of inflation overshooting the 2% target, while the hawkish wing views this as justification for accelerated normalization.

2. Growth Outlook
Internal sources indicate the BOJ may raise its growth forecasts in upcoming reports. This would likely provide the "economic strength" justification needed by the Governor to move forward with hikes despite political headwinds.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
Market analysis suggests a "high bar" for a hawkish repricing of the Yen, indicating that while the BOJ is leaning hawkish, the market is not yet pricing in a rapid acceleration of hikes.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
Concerns persist regarding JGB market stability. Former policymakers have warned that the BOJ may face pressure to increase bond-buying if yields spike too sharply, highlighting the delicate balance between hiking rates and maintaining market function.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
No specific new updates on the tapering schedule were provided in the last 30 days, though the general trajectory remains focused on normalization.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The BOJ is preparing for "accelerated hikes" should inflation risks grow. This represents a shift from a purely data-dependent approach to a more proactive stance, though it is tempered by PM Takaichi's public calls for policy restraint.
