Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a historic pivot for the Bank of Japan. On June 16, the BOJ raised its key interest rate to 1.0%, the highest level since 1995, citing heightened inflation risks. Governor Ueda has shifted into an "inflation-fighting mode," signaling a path for steadier hikes. The board is currently split: a hawkish contingent led by Naoki Tamura is pushing for a neutral rate of ~2% and hikes every few months, while Deputy Governor Uchida remains focused on market stability. The policy shift is driven by concerns over inflation overshooting and the yen's impact on prices, despite some uncertainty remaining regarding geopolitical shocks (US-Iran deal).

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The yen remains a critical catalyst. Reports indicate the BOJ is vigilant regarding the yen's impact on inflation. Despite a rate hike to 1% and reported interventions (~$70bn+), the yen's weakness continues to fuel inflation anxiety.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The move to 1% is the most aggressive shift in three decades. While the BOJ is raising rates, some analysts suggest the "dovish revamp" of the process may cast doubt on the long-term speed of the hike path.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
The BOJ has signaled that it will end its tapering process next year, indicating a structured wind-down of its massive JGB holdings.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has evolved from "cautionary/neutral" to "inflation-fighting." Governor Ueda's pivot suggests a move toward a more predictable, steadier cadence of rate hikes rather than sporadic adjustments.
