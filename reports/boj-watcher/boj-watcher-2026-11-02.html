Executive Summary

The Bank of Japan (BOJ) maintained interest rates at the October 30 MPM but delivered a landmark shift in rhetoric, declaring that the 2% inflation target has been "generally achieved" for the first time since 2013. While Governor Ueda maintains a cautious tone regarding the sustainability of this achievement, the official communication signals a clear path for further rate hikes. Notably, Deputy Governor Himino has signaled readiness to raise borrowing costs, and the BOJ is accelerating its balance sheet normalization by nearly tripling the pace of ETF sales. The focus has shifted from "whether" the target will be hit to "how" to sustain it, cementing the case for near-term normalization.

1. Inflation Assessment (CPI, Core, Wages)
The BOJ has officially declared "victory" on the 2% inflation target, noting that underlying inflation aligns with the target for the first time in over a decade. However, Governor Ueda distinguishes between "hitting" the target and "sustainably achieving" it, suggesting the BOJ is looking for evidence of a virtuous wage-price cycle.

2. Growth Outlook
No specific growth data provided in the current period, though the focus on "anchoring" inflation suggests a belief that the economy can withstand higher rates.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The Yen remained under pressure leading into October speeches. Market participants are now pricing in the pace of hikes rather than the initial move, as the "inflation win" removes the primary justification for extreme accommodation.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The BOJ is monitoring the impact of AI and Big Data on the neutral rate. There is a warning that the AI boom may have artificially eased financial conditions, which could necessitate a more proactive rate response to maintain stability.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
A significant acceleration in ETF sales was reported on Oct 30, with the liquidation timeline nearly tripled. This indicates a desire to shrink the balance sheet more aggressively alongside rate hikes.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has evolved from "monitoring the trend" to "signaling further rate hikes." The declaration that the target is reached effectively ends the "reflationary" era of BOJ policy.
