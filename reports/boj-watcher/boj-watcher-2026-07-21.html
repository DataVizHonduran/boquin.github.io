Executive Summary

The past 30 days have highlighted a widening divergence within the Policy Board. A hawkish contingent, led by External Members Masu and Tamura, is aggressively pushing for faster normalization, with calls for rate hikes "every few months" and a target rate closer to 2%. Governor Ueda has reinforced this by reiterating risks of inflation overshooting the 2% target. Conversely, new member Toichiro Asada has established a dovish anchor, explicitly requiring evidence of demand-driven inflation before supporting further hikes. Despite rates hitting 31-year highs, the Yen's continued weakness (40-year low) presents a significant policy conundrum, while rising household inflation expectations provide the fundamental justification for the hawkish wing's urgency.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment (CPI, Core, Wages)
There is a growing consensus that inflation risks are skewed to the upside. Governor Ueda and Member Tamura both highlighted the risk of inflation "overshooting" the 2% target. This is supported by external data showing rising household inflation expectations, which strengthens the case for further normalization.

2. Growth Outlook
Internal reports suggest the BOJ may raise its growth forecasts, indicating a more robust economic backdrop that could provide the necessary cover for higher interest rates.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The BOJ faces a "monetary conundrum." Despite the policy rate reaching a 31-year high, the Yen has hit a 40-year low. This disconnect suggests that current rate hikes are not yet sufficient to offset carry trade incentives or global yield differentials.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The market is adjusting to a higher rate environment, but the "Accommodative Hike" philosophy (Uchida) remains a guiding principle to ensure market stability during the transition.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
No specific updates on the quantitative tapering schedule were provided in the last 30 days of data.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance is shifting from "whether to hike" to "how fast to hike." The hawkish wing is now explicitly discussing a path toward a 2% neutral rate and a cadence of hikes every few months.
