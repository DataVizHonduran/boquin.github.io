Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a historic milestone as the Bank of Japan raised its key interest rate to 1.0% on June 16, the highest level since 1995. The move was driven by heightened inflation risks and a desire to ward off "war inflation." While the Board signaled further tightening intent, the internal divide has widened. Hawkish members (Tamura, Takata) are pushing for a neutral rate of ~2% and hikes every few months, while new member Toichiro Asada has emerged as a clear dovish dissenter, demanding demand-driven inflation before further hikes. Market focus now shifts to the July meeting, where rates are expected to hold steady despite a potential upward revision to growth forecasts.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment (CPI, Core, Wages)
Core CPI (excluding special factors) rose 2.7% in May, showing a slight easing from April. However, the BOJ remains vigilant regarding "war inflation" and the risk of an inflation overshoot. Real wages have risen for five straight months, though the pace is slowing.

2. Growth Outlook
Reports suggest the BOJ may raise its 2026 growth forecast in the upcoming outlook report, providing more headroom for further rate hikes.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The yen's recent plunge is noted as a factor that changes the case for rate hikes, potentially adding urgency to the BOJ's normalization to prevent imported inflation.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The move to 1% represents a 31-year high. The Board is balancing the need for normalization with the stability of the JGB market.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
Analysis indicates the BOJ is on track to end its tapering process by next year (2027).

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has shifted from "cautionary" to "active tightening." The BOJ now explicitly flags the risk of inflation overshooting and signals that further hikes are likely if risks grow.
