Executive Summary

The Bank of Japan has entered a "new phase" of monetary policy, raising the benchmark interest rate by 25bps to 1.25% on September 18, 2026—the highest level in 31 years. This move signals a pivot toward a preemptive fight against persistent inflation. The decision was preceded by a surge in hawkish rhetoric from board members, most notably Hajime Takata, who urged "nimble" hikes. While Governor Ueda maintains a cautious tone regarding the outlook, the consensus has shifted toward continued normalization to cap the price trend at 2%. Market reactions indicate a definitive end to the era of ultra-low rates, though the BOJ continues to provide mixed signals regarding the exact pace of future increases.
