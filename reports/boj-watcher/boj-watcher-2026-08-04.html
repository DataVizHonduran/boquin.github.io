Executive Summary

The Bank of Japan (BOJ) maintained the policy rate at 1.00% at the July 31 MPM. While the decision to hold was expected, the accompanying communications were notably hawkish. Governor Ueda warned that core inflation could exceed the 2% target, signaling a commitment to further rate hikes if risks materialize. The primary tension remains the "monetary conundrum": interest rates are at 31-year highs, yet the Yen has neared 40-year lows, fueling imported inflation. While the BOJ upgraded GDP forecasts, it remains vigilant regarding the weak Yen's impact on price stability. The board remains split, with a hawkish minority pushing for faster normalization and a dovish wing, led by Toichiro Asada, demanding stronger demand-driven inflation.

The BOJ upgraded its GDP forecasts during the July 31 meeting, indicating a more resilient domestic economy than previously modeled, which provides the necessary "growth cushion" for higher rates.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The Yen is a critical driver of current policy. With the currency nearing 40-year lows, the BOJ is facing a "conundrum" where rate hikes are needed to combat imported inflation, yet market volatility persists. Government intervention talks are swirling, adding a layer of complexity to the BOJ's timing.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
