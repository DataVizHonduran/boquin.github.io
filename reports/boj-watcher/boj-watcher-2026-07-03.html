Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a historic pivot for the Bank of Japan. On June 16, the BOJ raised its key interest rate to 1.0%, the highest level in 31 years, citing heightened inflation risks. Governor Ueda has shifted into an "inflation-fighting mode," signaling a path for steadier hikes. While the move was a milestone, internal divisions are widening: Hawkish members (Tamura, Takata) are pushing for a neutral rate of ~2% and hikes every few months, while Deputy Governor Uchida continues to emphasize caution. The Board also signaled the end of JGB tapering by next year. The transition of the Board continues with Junko Nakagawa's term ending June 29, 2026, to be replaced by Ayano Sato.

The BOJ has officially pivoted to an "inflation-fighting mode." The June 16 hike was explicitly cited as a response to "heightened inflation risks" and the risk of an inflation overshoot.

2. Growth Outlook
Not explicitly detailed in recent statements, though the focus has shifted heavily from stimulating growth to managing price stability.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The Yen remains a primary driver. Reports indicate that "yen and inflation worries" took hold, leading to the 1% hike. External members (specifically the Takaichi appointee/Takata) are urging extreme vigilance regarding the Yen's impact on imported inflation.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The BOJ is managing a delicate balance. While rates are rising, the Board is mindful of market stability. The move to 1% is a 31-year high, increasing pressure on JGB yields.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
The BOJ has provided a timeline for the end of its quantitative tightening, stating it will "end tapering next year."

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has shifted from "cautionary/neutral" to "steadier hikes." The phrase "flags more to come" in the June statement suggests a programmatic approach to normalization rather than sporadic adjustments.
