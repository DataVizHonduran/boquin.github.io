The BOJ has moved from "observing" inflation to "fighting" it. Governor Ueda and Deputy Governor Himino both highlighted the risk of inflation overshooting the 2% target. The focus has shifted from whether the 2% target is sustainable to how to prevent it from becoming excessive.

**2. Growth Outlook**
While not the primary focus of recent communications, the decision to hike to 1.0% suggests the BOJ believes the economy can withstand higher borrowing costs without triggering a recession.

**3. Yen / FX Considerations**
The yen was a primary catalyst for the June 16 hike. Reports explicitly link the move to the yen "languishing at historic lows," indicating that FX volatility and imported inflation have become intolerable for the Board.

**4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market**
The move to 1.0% is a 31-year high. The BOJ is now managing the transition to a higher-rate environment, with a focus on preventing market dysfunction while continuing the upward trajectory.

**5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)**
The BOJ has signaled that the current tapering of JGB purchases will conclude by 2027, indicating a definitive timeline for the end of large-scale asset purchases.

**6. Forward Guidance Evolution**
Guidance has evolved from "cautionary/data-dependent" to "steadier hikes." The Governor's pivot to "inflation-fighting mode" suggests a more predictable, upward path for rates rather than sporadic, event-driven hikes.
