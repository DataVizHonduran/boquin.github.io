Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a decisive acceleration in the Bank of Japan's normalization path. On June 16, the BOJ raised its key interest rate to 1%, a 31-year high, citing persistent inflation and yen volatility. Despite Governor Ueda's hospitalization on June 10, which caused him to miss the June MPM, the Board pivoted toward a more aggressive inflation-fighting stance. Governor Ueda and Deputy Governor Himino have both explicitly flagged risks of inflation overshooting the 2% target. Most notably, External Member Naoki Tamura has signaled a desire for a faster pace, suggesting rate hikes "every few months." The Board is now transitioning from cautious "data-dependency" to a more systematic tightening cycle to prevent a wage-price spiral.

Reports indicate the BOJ raised rates partly to address "yen and inflation worries," suggesting that the currency's weakness was contributing to imported inflation, thereby necessitating a higher nominal rate to stabilize the exchange rate.
