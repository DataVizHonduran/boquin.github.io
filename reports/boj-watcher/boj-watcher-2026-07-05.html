Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a historic pivot for the Bank of Japan. On June 16, the BOJ raised its policy interest rate to 1.0%, the highest level since 1995. This move was driven by heightened inflation risks and a severely depreciated yen (hitting 40-year lows). While the hike signals a commitment to normalization, internal divisions are widening. Hawkish members, specifically Naoki Tamura, are now openly calling for a neutral rate of approximately 2% and suggesting hikes every few months. Conversely, the Board continues to manage market stability, with Deputy Governor Uchida emphasizing persisting uncertainties. The transition of board members (Nakagawa exiting, Sato incoming) adds a layer of structural change to the voting bloc.
