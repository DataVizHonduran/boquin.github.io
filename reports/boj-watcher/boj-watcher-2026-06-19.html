Executive Summary

The Bank of Japan (BOJ) has entered a decisive "inflation-fighting mode." On June 15/16, the BOJ raised the policy interest rate to 1.0%, the highest level since 1995. This move was driven by persistent inflation worries and a languishing yen. Governor Ueda has pivoted toward a steadier hiking path to contain inflation, which he expects to rise significantly in 2026. While the hike was a significant hawkish signal, Deputy Governor Uchida continues to emphasize balance sheet stability, suggesting that annual JGB purchases of 2 trillion yen are sufficient. The board remains divided, but the momentum has shifted toward faster normalization to ward off "war inflation" and currency depreciation.

1. Inflation Assessment (CPI, Core, Wages)
The BOJ has shifted its assessment, with Governor Ueda explicitly stating the need to "contain inflation" and predicting a significant rise in 2026. The BOJ's new trend gauge confirms that inflation is now exceeding the target, moving the conversation from "achieving" the target to "managing" it.

2. Growth Outlook
Data suggests a mixed environment; while the BOJ is hiking, reports indicate retail sales are sluggish due to falling real wages and factory output is being squeezed by Middle East/Iran-related shocks.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The yen's "historic lows" were a primary catalyst for the June hike. Despite the move to 1.0%, market reaction has been muted, with the yen struggling to capitalize fully on the hawkish decision.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The BOJ is balancing rate hikes with market stability. The move to 1.0% is the most aggressive since 1995, increasing the pressure on JGB yields.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
Deputy Governor Uchida has signaled a pragmatic approach to the balance sheet, stating that 2 trillion yen in annual JGB purchases is sufficient for adjustment, suggesting the BOJ is not seeking a chaotic unwind.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The guidance has evolved from "cautionary" to "inflation-fighting." Governor Ueda's remarks clear the path for "steadier hikes" rather than sporadic adjustments.
