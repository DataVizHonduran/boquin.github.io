Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a historic pivot for the Bank of Japan. On June 15/16, the BOJ raised its policy interest rate to 1.0%, the highest level since 1995, citing heightened inflation risks and the need to ward off "war inflation." While the Board signaled further tightening intent and a risk of inflation overshoot, internal divisions are widening. Hawkish members (Tamura, Takata) are pushing for a neutral rate of ~2% and hikes every few months, while new member Toichiro Asada has emerged as a clear dissenter, demanding demand-driven inflation before supporting further hikes. Market focus now shifts to the July meeting, where the BOJ is expected to maintain the 1% rate while potentially raising growth forecasts.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment (CPI, Core, Wages)
The BOJ is now explicitly concerned with "inflation overshoot" and "war inflation." While real wages have risen for five straight months, the pace is slowing, suggesting a tension between cost-push inflation and the desired virtuous wage-price cycle.

2. Growth Outlook
Reports indicate the BOJ may raise its growth forecasts in the upcoming July meeting, which would provide the macroeconomic justification for maintaining a tighter policy stance.

3. Yen / FX Considerations
The hike to 1% was driven in part by "yen and inflation worries." However, market analysts continue to warn of a "slow motion currency crisis," suggesting the 1% rate may not yet be sufficient to stabilize the yen against global volatility.

4. Financial Conditions & JGB Market
The move to a 31-year high rate is a significant shock to the JGB market. The BOJ is balancing the need for normalization with the risk of market instability, as evidenced by Deputy Governor Uchida's continued emphasis on uncertainties.

5. Balance Sheet (JGB purchase taper)
The BOJ has signaled a definitive timeline for the end of its tapering process, with a target to complete the wind-down of JGB purchases by next year.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
Guidance has shifted from "cautionary" to "active tightening." The BOJ is now flagging the risk of inflation overshooting its 2% target, providing the narrative cover for "accelerated hikes" if risks grow.
