Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a historic pivot in the Bank of Japan's normalization cycle. On September 18, the BOJ raised interest rates to 1.25%, a 31-year high, signaling a shift toward a "preemptive" fight against inflation. This move follows intense pressure from the Board's hawkish wing, led by Hajime Takata, who had been calling for "nimble" and "flexible" hikes. While Governor Ueda maintains a cautious tone regarding the outlook, the consensus has shifted toward the necessity of further hikes to cap the price trend at 2%. Market volatility and U.S. economic pressure were noted as complicating factors, but the overarching trajectory is now firmly geared toward faster normalization.
