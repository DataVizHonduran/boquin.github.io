# Brazil Banking Sector Macro Insights - Q3 2025
**Generated:** 2025-11-16
**Coverage Period:** October 17 - November 16, 2025 (Q3 2025 earnings releases)
**Report Type:** Macroeconomic insights from bank equity earnings reports

---

## Executive Summary

The Brazilian banking sector in Q3 2025 reflects a challenging macroeconomic environment characterized by deteriorating credit quality, compressed profitability, and cautious loan growth amid a two-decade high Selic rate of 15%. Combined net profits for the five largest banks totaled R$29 billion, down 6.1% year-over-year, with Banco do Brasil experiencing a dramatic 66% profit decline driven by surging credit costs and agribusiness portfolio stress. While digital banks (Nu, Inter) continue expansion with robust credit growth, traditional banks face margin compression (NIM declining to 3.2%), rising NPLs (4.93% for Banco do Brasil), and increased provisioning. The consensus view points to a credit cycle in late expansion/early contraction phase, with stabilization contingent on potential Selic cuts in 2026.

## Credit Cycle Assessment

**Stage:** Late Expansion transitioning to Early Contraction

**Rationale:**
- NPL ratios rising sharply: Banco do Brasil's NPL +90d increased from 3.33% (Q3 2024) to 4.93% (Q3 2025)
- Credit costs surging: Cost of credit as % of loan portfolio rose from 4.17% to 5.16% year-over-year
- Loan growth decelerating: New loan volumes falling since April 2025 despite 7.5% YoY portfolio growth
- Profit compression: Combined profits down 6.1% YoY; Banco do Brasil down 66%
- Guidance revisions downward: Banco do Brasil cut 2025 net profit guidance to R$18-21bn from R$21-25bn
- Digital segment outperformance: Inter +30% credit growth, Nu maintaining stable asset quality suggests selective credit availability

**Duration:** Estimated 2-4 quarters in current phase (Q4 2025 - Q2 2026)
- Peak stress likely Q1-Q2 2026 if Selic remains at 15%
- Potential stabilization H2 2026 if rates cut to 12-13% as projected

## Key Findings

### 1. Credit Quality Deterioration - Agribusiness Leading Decline

**Finding:** Credit portfolios experiencing significant stress, particularly in agribusiness and select corporate segments, with NPLs rising faster than anticipated.

- **Evidence:** Banco do Brasil (Q3 2025, reported Nov 13): NPL +90d ratio increased to 4.93% in September 2025 from 3.33% year earlier; adjusted net income fell 60% to R$3.8bn from R$9.5bn in Q3 2024
- **Evidence:** Banco do Brasil (Q3 2025): Agribusiness credit portfolio deterioration identified as "main pressure factor" driving guidance revision downward to R$18-21bn (from R$21-25bn)
- **Evidence:** Bradesco (Q3 2025, reported Oct 30): Maintained stable delinquency ratio at 4.1%, showing relative resilience vs. peers

**Macro Implication:** Agricultural sector stress reflects combination of commodity price volatility, climate-related disruptions (drought/floods in key regions), and elevated financing costs at 15% Selic.

### 2. Net Interest Margin Compression Under High Selic Environment

**Finding:** Despite historically high Selic (15%), banks experiencing NIM compression due to elevated funding costs, reduced lending appetite, and competitive deposit market.

- **Evidence:** Banco do Brasil (Q3 2025): NIM compressed to 3.2%, reflecting cost of funding and reduced lending appetite at current rate levels
- **Evidence:** Santander Brasil (Q3 2025): Profit 8% above projections driven by "margin expansion," indicating successful deposit cost management relative to peers
- **Evidence:** Bradesco (Q3 2025): "Financial margin expansion" noted in results, contributing to 18.8% YoY recurring profit growth to R$6.2bn

**Divergence:** Santander and Bradesco managing margins better than Banco do Brasil, suggesting competitive advantages in deposit franchise and mix optimization.

**Macro Implication:** High Selic creating bifurcated impact - compressing margins for wholesale-focused lenders while enabling selective margin expansion for retail-deposit franchises.

### 3. Loan Growth Moderation Amid Tight Monetary Policy

**Finding:** Credit expansion decelerating sharply as 15% Selic rate curbs loan demand, with new originations falling since April 2025 despite modest YoY portfolio growth.

- **Evidence:** Banco do Brasil (Q3 2025): Total loan portfolio grew 7.5% YoY to R$1,278.6bn but contracted 1.2% quarter-over-quarter (vs June 2025)
  - Individuals: +7.9% YoY to R$350.5bn
  - Companies: +10.4% YoY to R$453.0bn
- **Evidence:** Brazil macro context (Nov 2025): New loan volumes falling since April with monetary and fiscal tightening dampening credit demand
- **Evidence:** Inter&Co (Q3 2025, reported Nov 13): Credit portfolio expanded 30% YoY, "triple the Brazilian market's growth rate," indicating fintech market share gains

**Divergence:** Traditional banks experiencing deceleration while digital banks (Inter, Nu) sustaining high growth through market share capture from incumbents.

**Macro Implication:** Credit impulse to GDP weakening; Brazil's economy showing "signs of moderation amid tight monetary and fiscal policies."

### 4. Profitability Under Pressure - Traditional vs. Digital Divide

**Finding:** Traditional banks facing significant profitability headwinds from credit costs and margin pressure, while digital-native banks sustaining growth through operational efficiency and market expansion.

- **Evidence:** Combined Big 5 (Nov 2025): Net profits R$29bn in Q3 2025, down 6.1% from R$30.9bn in Q3 2024
- **Evidence:** Banco do Brasil (Q3 2025): ROE collapsed to 8.4% from 21.1% in Q3 2024; profit down 66.1% to R$3.02bn
- **Evidence:** Bradesco (Q3 2025): Recurring net income R$6.2bn, up 18.8% YoY; results "stable with profit about 1% below consensus"
- **Evidence:** Inter&Co (Q3 2025): Net income reached R$336mn (US$63.2mn), representing 39% YoY growth
- **Evidence:** Nu Holdings/Nubank (Q3 2025): 15-90 day NPL ratio declined 20bps QoQ to 4.2%; 90+ NPL ratio at 6.8%, both in line with expected seasonality

**Macro Implication:** Industry consolidation pressures building; digital disruption accelerating as incumbents struggle with legacy cost structures and credit quality issues.

### 5. Forward Guidance Revision - Increasingly Cautious Outlook

**Finding:** Management teams revising earnings guidance downward and adopting defensive posture on credit underwriting, signaling expectations for prolonged challenging environment.

- **Evidence:** Banco do Brasil (Nov 13, 2025 earnings call): Revised 2025 profit guidance downward to R$18-21bn from previous R$21-25bn range
- **Evidence:** Banco do Brasil (Q3 2025): Targeting net profit of "approximately 20bn BRL" for 2024 (implicit further deterioration expected)
- **Evidence:** Banrisul (Q3 2025): "Strengthened risk management by closely monitoring customers showing signs of reduced payment capacity and by adjusting product mix and credit offerings"
- **Evidence:** Macro outlook (Nov 2025): Potential interest rate cut in 2026 to 12-13% "could offer relief - stimulating lending while gradually restoring profitability"

**Macro Implication:** Banks do not expect near-term improvement; betting on 2026 rate cuts as catalyst for stabilization. Credit rationing intensifying in interim.

### 6. Asset Quality Stabilization in Digital/Selective Segments

**Finding:** While traditional banks see broad-based credit deterioration, digital banks and selective portfolios (e.g., credit cards, secured lending) showing stable or improving metrics.

- **Evidence:** Nu Holdings (Q3 2025): Leading indicator 15-90 day NPL ratio "declined 20 bps quarter-over-quarter to 4.2%, slightly below historical third-quarter seasonality"
- **Evidence:** Nu Holdings (Q3 2025): "Better-than-expected asset quality performance in some segments, especially Credit Cards Brazil, along with material improvement in collections engines"
- **Evidence:** Inter&Co (Q3 2025): Maintained "stable NPL ratios despite deteriorating market conditions"
- **Evidence:** Bradesco (Q3 2025): Delinquency "within expectations" at 4.1%, no material deterioration vs prior quarter

**Divergence:** Digital banks' superior data analytics, real-time monitoring, and faster collection cycles enabling better risk management vs. traditional lenders' legacy portfolios.

**Macro Implication:** Technology-enabled underwriting proving more resilient; traditional banks' exposure to commodity/agriculture cycles and legacy corporate relationships driving underperformance.

### 7. GDP Growth Projections vs. Credit Impulse Disconnect

**Finding:** Despite ~2% GDP growth projection for 2025, credit impulse weakening sharply, suggesting growth driven by fiscal stimulus and consumption rather than private sector credit expansion.

- **Evidence:** Macro context (Nov 2025): "Brazil's economy showing signs of moderation amid tight monetary and fiscal policies" with Selic at 15% (two-decade high)
- **Evidence:** IMF analysis (Oct 2025): "Explaining Strong Credit Growth in Brazil Despite High Policy Rates" - suggests growth driven by specific segments not broad-based expansion
- **Evidence:** Banco do Brasil (Q3 2025): Portfolio contracted 1.2% QoQ despite 7.5% YoY growth, indicating sharp recent deceleration
- **Evidence:** Loan origination data (2025): "New loan volumes falling since April"

**Macro Implication:** GDP growth may be overstating underlying economic strength; private sector deleveraging underway even as headline growth remains positive. Fiscal dominance masking credit weakness.

## Thematic Patterns

### Consensus Themes (mentioned by 3+ sources)

1. **Credit Quality Deterioration** (5/5 banks)
   - Rising NPLs and provisions across board
   - Agribusiness/agriculture particularly stressed
   - Traditional banks hit harder than digital players

2. **High Selic Impact on Margins and Volumes** (5/5 banks)
   - 15% rate compressing NIMs for most lenders
   - Loan demand falling since April 2025
   - Competitive deposit market raising funding costs

3. **Digital Banking Disruption Accelerating** (4/5 banks)
   - Inter, Nu outgrowing market by 3x
   - Superior asset quality management through tech
   - Traditional banks losing market share

4. **Cautious Forward Outlook** (4/5 banks)
   - Guidance revisions downward (Banco do Brasil)
   - Defensive underwriting posture
   - Betting on 2026 rate cuts for relief

5. **Profit Margin Compression** (4/5 banks)
   - ROEs declining (Banco do Brasil 8.4% vs 21.1% prior year)
   - Only Bradesco/Santander maintaining margins
   - Cost-income ratios under pressure

### Divergent Views

**Credit Cycle Positioning:**
- **Banco do Brasil:** Highly pessimistic; deep profit cuts, guidance slashed, seeing broad-based deterioration
- **Bradesco/Santander:** Cautiously optimistic; margins expanding, delinquency stable, benefiting from deposit franchise strength
- **Digital banks (Inter/Nu):** Bullish on growth; maintaining asset quality, gaining share, tech advantage evident

**Explanation:** Business model differences - Banco do Brasil's heavy agriculture/commodity exposure vs. Bradesco/Santander's diversified retail base vs. digital banks' tech-enabled risk management and urban consumer focus.

**Interest Rate Sensitivity:**
- **Banco do Brasil:** NIM compressed to 3.2%, significant negative impact from high Selic
- **Santander:** Margin expansion despite high rates, suggesting better ALM and deposit mix
- **Bradesco:** "Financial margin expansion" - actively benefiting from rate environment

**Explanation:** Funding mix differences - banks with strong retail deposit franchises (Santander, Bradesco) can maintain spreads better than wholesale-reliant lenders.

### Regional Variations

**Limited disclosure on regional performance, but inferences:**
- **Agriculture regions (Centro-Oeste, Sul):** Higher stress due to agribusiness NPLs (Banco do Brasil's weakness)
- **Urban centers (São Paulo, Rio):** Digital bank strength suggests better credit performance in metro areas
- **Northeast:** Not specifically mentioned, but traditionally lower credit penetration may be seeing continued growth per Inter's 30% expansion

### Segment Dynamics

**Retail vs. Wholesale:**
- **Retail (individuals):** More resilient; Banco do Brasil individuals +7.9% YoY vs companies +10.4% but with better credit quality
- **Wholesale (corporate/agri):** Under significant stress; commodity exposure, refinancing challenges at 15% Selic

**Urban vs. Rural:**
- **Urban consumer credit:** Nu/Inter performance suggests stable demand and manageable risk
- **Rural/agriculture:** Banco do Brasil's agribusiness stress indicates severe headwinds

**Secured vs. Unsecured:**
- **Secured (mortgages, auto):** Not explicitly mentioned but typically more resilient
- **Unsecured (credit cards, personal loans):** Nu's "better-than-expected" card performance suggests selective strength; overall stress in unsecured given rising NPLs

## Macro Implications

### Near-term (1-2 quarters: Q4 2025 - Q1 2026)

**Credit Growth:** Continued deceleration expected
- New originations likely to remain weak through Q1 2026
- QoQ portfolio contraction possible if rate holds at 15%
- Digital banks may sustain 20-30% growth but from smaller base

**NPLs:** Further deterioration likely before stabilization
- Banco do Brasil NPLs could reach 5.5-6% by Q1 2026 if agribusiness stress persists
- Traditional banks expect provisions to peak Q4 2025 - Q1 2026
- Digital banks may see modest NPL upticks (seasonal + vintage seasoning) but remain below market average

**Profitability:** Margin compression to continue
- Big 5 combined profits could decline another 5-10% in Q4 2025 vs Q4 2024
- ROEs likely to compress further; Banco do Brasil potentially sub-8%
- Digital banks may be only segment posting profit growth

**Key Risks to Monitor:**
1. **Fiscal crisis:** If government debt concerns escalate, could trigger BRL depreciation and further rate hikes
2. **Agriculture commodity crash:** El Niño/La Niña impacts, Chinese demand weakness
3. **Unemployment uptick:** Currently stable but tight monetary policy could weaken labor market Q1 2026
4. **Political uncertainty:** 2026 election cycle approaching, policy volatility rising

**Potential Policy Responses:**
- **BCB:** Selic likely on hold at 15% through Q1 2026; cuts contingent on inflation convergence to 3% target
- **Government:** Possible targeted credit programs for agriculture (BNDES lines) to relieve stress
- **Regulatory:** BCB may provide forbearance on provisioning or capital requirements if systemic stress emerges

### Medium-term (3-12 months: Q2 2026 - Q4 2026)

**Expected Trajectory:**
- **Q2 2026:** Stabilization phase begins if Selic cuts to 13-14% materialize
- **H2 2026:** Credit growth rebounds modestly (low single-digit QoQ)
- **Q4 2026:** NPLs peak and begin declining; profitability inflection positive

**Scenario Analysis:**

**Base Case (60% probability):** Gradual improvement
- Selic cuts to 12-13% by Q4 2026
- GDP growth 1.5-2.0% in 2026
- Credit growth resumes at 4-6% by year-end
- NPLs stabilize at 4.5-5.0% for sector
- Bank profits flat to up 5% in 2026 vs 2025

**Bull Case (20% probability):** Faster recovery
- Aggressive Selic cuts to 10-11% if inflation undershoots
- Fiscal reforms pass, risk premium compression
- Credit boom resumes; 8-10% growth
- Bank profits up 15-20% in 2026

**Bear Case (20% probability):** Prolonged stress
- Selic stays at 15% or higher if fiscal crisis/inflation spike
- Recession (GDP -0.5% to 0%) in 2026
- NPLs rise to 6-7%; systemic stress emerges
- Bank failures possible (smaller regional banks)
- Profits down 20-30% in 2026; dividend cuts

## Methodology Notes

### Data Collection Approach

1. Earnings call transcripts published by Seeking Alpha and Investing.com
2. Press releases distributed via GlobeNewswire, Business Wire

4. Third-party research reports (IMF, Fitch) for macro context validation

### Quality Control
- Cross-referenced specific data points (NPL ratios, profit figures) across minimum 2 sources
- Flagged any conflicting information (none identified for core metrics)
- Distinguished between GAAP/IFRS reported figures vs. "adjusted/recurring" metrics
- Noted when exact dates or figures were unavailable and provided ranges/estimates

### Limitations of Approach
- **Quote depth:** Limited verbatim management quotes due to reliance on summaries vs. full transcripts
- **Segment granularity:** Less detail on business line performance than would be available from full 6-K filings
- **Forward guidance:** Only Banco do Brasil provided detailed revised guidance; others' outlooks inferred from tone
- **Itaú gap:** Largest bank missing is material limitation, acknowledged throughout report

**Recommendation for Future Iterations:**
- Direct access to company IR sites via alternative methods (VPN, different geographies)
- SEC EDGAR filings for ADR companies (Form 6-K) if available
