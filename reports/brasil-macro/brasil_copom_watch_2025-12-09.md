## Executive Summary

During the past 30 days, Brazil's Central Bank (BCB) and COPOM members maintained a firmly **hawkish stance**, keeping the Selic rate at 15% (the highest level in nearly 20 years) for the third consecutive meeting on November 5, 2025. President Gabriel Galípolo, who assumed leadership on January 1, 2025, emphasized that interest rates must remain "elevated for an extended period" to address persistent inflation pressures. Director Diogo Guillen delivered a farewell speech on November 27 highlighting the de-anchoring of inflation expectations as a key challenge facing the committee. The period was marked by ongoing fiscal policy tensions as markets remained skeptical about the government's ability to meet fiscal targets, with congressional debates over the 2026 budget highlighting continued concerns about the sustainability of Brazil's fiscal framework implemented in 2024.

## Thematic Analysis

### 1. Inflation Outlook

**Current IPCA Trajectory:**
- **October 2025 (most recent):** Annual inflation 4.68% (down from 5.17% in September)
- Monthly: 0.1% (deceleration from 0.48% in September)
- **Boletim Focus projection (December 2025):** 2025 IPCA at 4.43-4.46%, approaching tolerance band ceiling of 4.5%

**Core Inflation Trends:**
- **Services sector inflation** remains persistently elevated, a key concern for COPOM
- July IPCA-15 showed acceleration in underlying services, indicating domestic demand pressures
- Services inflation stickiness suggests wage-price dynamics remain robust despite monetary tightening

**Inflation Expectations Monitoring:**
- Focus survey shows inflation expectations for 2025 declined from 4.70% four weeks prior to 4.43-4.46% in early December
- **2026 expectations:** 4.2% - still above 3% target center
- **De-anchoring concerns:** Guillen explicitly cited "de-anchoring of expectations" as major 2025 challenge
- BCB emphasizes need to target 3% center, not 4.5% upper band

**Member Consensus:**
- **Unanimous hawkish consensus:** All COPOM members voted to maintain 15% Selic in November
- No public dissent; board appears fully aligned on need for prolonged restrictive policy
- Galípolo's November Senate testimony reinforced collective commitment to inflation target center

### 2. Growth Assessment

**GDP Growth Views:**
- BCB reduced 2025 growth projection from 2.1% to 2%
- IMF and market analysts note "moderating growth" while labor market "still shows dynamism"
- Growth slowdown acknowledged but not deemed sufficient to warrant policy easing

**Output Gap Estimates:**
- COPOM statement references "resilient economic activity" and "positive output gap"
- Labor market strength cited as source of demand-side inflation pressure
- September COPOM minutes noted concerns about economy operating above potential

**Labor Market Conditions:**
- Characterized as showing continued "dynamism" despite monetary tightening
- Wage pressures contributing to services sector inflation persistence
- Employment growth slowing but remains positive

**Member Views on Economic Activity:**
- Consensus view: Growth deceleration necessary to bring inflation to target
- No expressions of concern about recession risk
- Policy explicitly prioritizes inflation control over growth support

### 3. External Environment

**Global Monetary Policy Outlook:**
- **US Economic Uncertainty:** COPOM November statement cited "complex" external environment due to US economic conditions
- Federal Reserve policy trajectory remains source of volatility for emerging markets
- Global financial volatility highlighted as ongoing external risk factor

**Exchange Rate Considerations (USD/BRL):**
- **November-December 2025:** Real trading around 5.44 per USD, with volatility linked to fiscal and political concerns
- November 12, 2025 marked strongest point: 1 USD = 5.2722 BRL
- **2024-2025 trajectory:** Real recovered from 6.19 (Dec 31, 2024) low following Nov 2024 fiscal crisis to 5.78 (Feb 13, 2025), then fluctuated in 5.27-5.44 range through November-December 2025
- **Political factors:** 2026 election uncertainty (Bolsonaro family dynamics) affecting currency in late 2025
- **BCB Intervention:** Intervened in early 2025 following late-2024 selloff; no reported intervention in Nov-Dec 2025 period
- Exchange rate weakness cited as **upside inflation risk** in COPOM statement

**International Capital Flows:**
- Political uncertainty (2026 election outlook, Bolsonaro family dynamics) affecting risk premium
- Fiscal concerns causing sovereign spreads to widen
- Foreign investor sentiment impacted by ongoing doubts about fiscal framework sustainability

**External Risks Mentioned:**
- COPOM identified downside risks: sharper domestic slowdown, falling commodity prices
- Upside risks: Persistent services inflation, currency depreciation
- Risk balance characterized as "broadly balanced" but with inflation bias

### 4. Fiscal Policy Interaction

**Concerns About Fiscal Sustainability:**
- **Senate Independent Fiscal Institution (November 2025):** Concluded spending commitments make spending cap unsustainable in medium term
- COPOM September minutes warned "lax fiscal policy could exacerbate inflationary pressures"
- Market perception: Fiscal dominance risk increasing

**Fiscal-Monetary Coordination:**
- **Background (November-December 2024):** Finance Minister Haddad announced spending cut package on Nov 27, 2024
  - Target: BRL 330 billion savings (2025-2030); BRL 72 billion in 2025-2026
  - Congress approved majority of measures in December 2024
  - Market reaction was negative - real depreciated sharply to near 6.0 per USD
- **Current status (Nov-Dec 2025):**
  - Implementation of 2024 fiscal package ongoing throughout 2025
  - 2026 budget debate in Congress: targeting 0.25% of GDP primary surplus
  - Market skepticism persists about government's ability to achieve fiscal targets
  - Congressional committee backed 2026 targets in early December 2025, but delays continue
- **BCB response:** Maintained hawkish stance throughout 2025, implicitly requiring fiscal discipline for future easing

**Impact of Government Spending on Inflation:**
- Fiscal expansion cited as structural inflation pressure
- Public debt over 75% of GDP constrains fiscal space
- Constitutional spending caps and mandatory expenditures limit adjustment capacity

**Credibility/Anchoring Issues:**
- **Political pressure on BCB:** Government and PT party criticized Galípolo for maintaining 15% rate
- Finance Minister Haddad stated "not possible to sustain real interest rate of 10% with inflation at 4.5%"
- **BCB independence:** Galípolo resisted political pressure, maintaining institutional credibility
- Fiscal uncertainty contributing to inflation expectation de-anchoring

### 5. Forward Guidance Evolution

**Changes in Language/Tone:**
- **May 2025:** "Elevated rates for extended period"
- **November 2025:** "Restrictive for quite prolonged period" + "future policy adjustments may be necessary"
- Shift from generic "extended" to more specific "prolonged" suggests longer pause than markets anticipated
- Introduction of conditionality ("may be necessary") maintains policy flexibility

**Rate Path Signaling:**
- **Current consensus:** Selic remains at 15% through end of 2025
- **Market expectations:** Easing cycle beginning December 2025 with 25bp cut (per former deputy governor Werlang)
- **Alternative forecasts:** Some analysts expect 15% maintained into early 2026
- **Peak rate debate:** Most forecasts converged on 15% as terminal rate; previous projections of 14.25% or 14.75% abandoned

**Conditionality of Future Moves:**
- November COPOM statement: "Future policy adjustments may be necessary to maintain price stability"
- Conditions for easing implicitly include:
  - Sustained inflation convergence toward 3%
  - Re-anchoring of inflation expectations
  - Fiscal policy credibility restoration
  - External environment stabilization

**Unanimity vs Dissent:**
- **November 2025 decision:** Unanimous vote to maintain 15%
- No recorded dissents in recent COPOM meetings under Galípolo presidency
- Contrast with some prior COPOM cycles featuring split votes
- Unanimity strengthens forward guidance credibility but reduces transparency about internal debate

### Key Shifts Identified

**Gabriel Galípolo:**
- **Trajectory:** Maintained hawkish consistency throughout first year as president
- **Notable resistance:** November 2025 Senate testimony explicitly rejected government pressure to cut rates, reaffirming 3% target focus
- **Language evolution:** Shifted from "extended period" (May) to "quite prolonged period" (Nov), signaling longer pause
- **Political dimension:** Successfully maintained institutional independence despite criticism from appointing government

**Diogo Guillen:**
- **Trajectory:** Consistent hawkish stance as mandate concludes
- **Final messaging (Nov 27):** Used farewell speech to institutionalize inflation vigilance, emphasizing COPOM robustness against individual preferences
- **Legacy emphasis:** Highlighted de-anchoring as defining challenge, signaling to successors need for sustained restrictive policy

**Collective COPOM:**
- **Board cohesion:** Unanimous hawkish votes contrast with some emerging market central banks experiencing internal division
- **Post-Campos Neto transition:** Lula-appointed board maintained/strengthened hawkish stance despite political preferences for looser policy
- **Credibility defense:** Board collectively prioritized inflation target over political pressure, supporting BCB autonomy

**No identified shifts toward dovishness:** Unlike some past cycles where individual members signaled readiness for easing, current board shows unified restrictive stance with no public dovish dissent.

## Risk Assessment

### Upside Risks to Inflation (requiring sustained restrictive policy)
1. **Services sector inflation persistence** - Sticky wage-price dynamics
2. **Exchange rate depreciation** - Fiscal concerns weakening real
3. **Fiscal dominance** - Spending cap sustainability doubts
4. **De-anchored expectations** - 2025-2026 forecasts above 4%
5. **External shocks** - US policy uncertainty, commodity price volatility

### Downside Risks to Inflation (could enable earlier easing)
1. **Sharper growth slowdown** - Monetary policy transmission intensifying
2. **Commodity price declines** - Global demand weakness
3. **Fiscal consolidation success** - If spending cuts credibly implemented
4. **External disinflation** - Global inflation falling faster than expected
5. **Demand destruction** - Real interest rates of 10% eventually constraining activity

### Policy Stance Assessment
**Current balance:** COPOM characterizes risks as "broadly balanced" but communication bias is hawkish. Committee prioritizing inflation convergence over growth support, accepting real interest rates well above neutral (~4-5%) to ensure expectations re-anchor at 3% target center.

## Market Implications & Outlook

### Short-term (Dec 2025 - Q1 2026)
- **Rate path:** Selic expected to remain at 15% through December meeting
- **Earliest cut timing:** Divergent views - some forecast December 2025 (-25bp), others Q1 2026
- **Currency:** Real likely to remain under pressure absent fiscal credibility restoration
- **Fixed income:** Long-end yields elevated due to fiscal premium; curve inversion persists

### Medium-term (2026)
- **Easing cycle:** If initiated, likely to be gradual (25bp increments) and data-dependent
- **Terminal rate:** Market divided on whether easing reaches 12-13% or remains higher
- **Inflation trajectory:** Focus projections show 2026 IPCA at 4.2%, requiring sustained restrictivity
- **Political cycle:** 2026 elections may complicate BCB independence; Galípolo facing political pressures

### Key Uncertainties
1. **Fiscal framework sustainability** - Will Haddad's cuts restore credibility or require further measures?
2. **US economic policy** - How will potential US tariff/trade policy changes affect Brazil?
3. **BCB board transitions** - Post-Guillen board composition and internal dynamics
4. **Inflation expectations** - Can BCB re-anchor expectations at 3% or will 4%+ become new norm?
