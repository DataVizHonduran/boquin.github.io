## Executive Summary

Mexico's macro landscape at the start of 2026 is characterized by sluggish growth, sticky inflation, and mounting fiscal pressures, yet investor sentiment remains cautiously optimistic on structural tailwinds from nearshoring. The economy contracted 0.3% in Q3 2025—its first decline since late 2021—driven by manufacturing weakness (-2.9% YoY) amid USMCA uncertainty and Trump tariff threats. Despite this, equity markets surged over 50% YTD through December 2025, validating contrarian positioning on attractive valuations and the long-term reshoring thesis.

Banxico completed its 2025 easing cycle with a final 25bp cut to 7.0% on December 18, but pivoted to a more cautious stance for early 2026, signaling a likely pause at its February meeting as core inflation remains stubbornly elevated at 4.34% (well above the 3% target). Services inflation at 4.49% reflects persistent wage pressures, amplified by the 12.1% minimum wage increase effective January 1, 2026. While headline inflation returned to the 3% ±1% target range at 3.72%, Banxico emphasizes that convergence to 3% requires sustained core deceleration—a goal now pushed to Q3 2026.

The fiscal picture is deeply concerning. PEMEX received MX$380 billion ($20.6 billion) in government support through September 2025—nearly triple the budgeted amount—while contributing only MX$204 billion to federal revenues, creating a negative fiscal balance of MX$182 billion. The 2026 federal budget of MX$10.19 trillion (+5.9% vs 2025) allocates $13.5 billion in additional PEMEX support and MX$263.5 billion for debt amortization, yet the government maintains the unrealistic goal of PEMEX "financial self-sufficiency" by 2027. Credit rating agencies upgraded PEMEX to B1 (Moody's) and BB+ (Fitch) based on sovereign support expectations, but stand-alone ratings remain deeply distressed at CCC.

The banking sector navigated Q3 2025 with mixed results: profitability remained robust (Banorte ROE 27%, Santander net profit +7.4% YoY) but signs of credit cycle deceleration emerged. Real loan growth slowed to 4.5% YoY, consumer credit showed resilience while corporate lending stagnated amid manufacturing recession. Asset quality remains controlled (Santander NPL 2.38%), but proactive provisioning increased (Banorte cost of risk 2.69%). Fitch rated Mexico as the only LatAm market with a "deteriorated" banking outlook for 2026, citing weak corporate credit demand and margin compression from Banxico's 400bp rate cuts since early 2025.

Politically, MORENA's dominance appears unshakeable heading into the 2027 mid-term elections. Polls show the ruling coalition at 46-51%, maintaining a 30+ percentage point lead over fragmented opposition (PAN 11-16%, MC 8-11%, PRI 7-8%). President Sheinbaum's approval stands at 69%—down from 85% in February 2025 but still strong by historical standards—with weakness concentrated in security (18% approve) and corruption (16% approve). The administration advanced landmark legislation including a new water law (ending private concession transfers), vaping prohibition, and extortion penalties up to 42 years.

Hedge funds maintained constructive Mexico positioning despite macro headwinds, with BlackRock identifying Mexico as its "biggest overweight" in Latin American portfolios. However, ETF flows diverged: Mexico-dedicated EWW saw $309M outflows over three months (profit-taking after 50% rally), while diversified ILF attracted $449M inflows, suggesting preference for regional rather than single-country exposure. Mexican domestic asset managers rotated defensively toward dividend-paying stocks with pricing power and reduced bond duration ahead of anticipated rate cuts.

**Key Themes:**
- **Growth Stagnation:** Q3 GDP contraction, manufacturing down 2.9%, credit demand weakening faster than asset quality deterioration
- **Fiscal Sustainability Crisis:** PEMEX burden ballooning, deficit targets revised from 3.2% to 4.1% for 2026, MX$284 billion revenue shortfall
- **Inflation Stickiness:** Core at 4.34% (target 3%), services at 4.49% amplified by 12.1% minimum wage hike, IEPS tax increases January 1

- **Nearshoring vs. Tariff Risk:** Long-term investment thesis intact but Trump 25% tariff threat and 2026 USMCA review create near-term volatility

## Fiscal & Political Developments

### Budget and Deficit Trajectory

Mexico's 2026 federal budget of **MX$10.19 trillion** (US$549 billion) took effect January 1, representing a 5.9% nominal increase over 2025. The government revised fiscal deficit projections **upward** from initial targets of 3.2% (2026) to **4.1%**, reflecting increasingly rigid spending commitments. Total budgetary revenues are estimated at 8.721 trillion pesos, including 1.204 trillion from oil and 5.839 trillion from taxes—a **5.7% increase** projected for 2026 tax collection based on greater efficiency and base expansion.

The **PEMEX fiscal burden** dominates the 2026 budget picture. Through September 2025, PEMEX received **MX$380.1 billion** ($20.6 billion) in capital injections—**279% of the budgeted amount**—while contributing only MX$204 billion to federal revenues, generating a **negative net fiscal balance of MX$182 billion**. This gap drove a **MX$284 billion federal revenue shortfall** through October 2025. For 2026, the budget allocates:
- MX$517.4 billion total PEMEX budget (+7.7% vs 2025)
- MX$263.5 billion for debt amortization
- $13.5 billion in government financing (up from $6.7B in 2025)
- 17.7% increase in physical investment

Despite unprecedented support, the government maintains PEMEX will achieve **"financial self-sufficiency" by 2027**—a timeline analysts universally view as unrealistic given the company's $100.3 billion debt burden and structural production constraints.

### Tax Reform Implementation

The **Economic Package for 2026**, approved by Mexico's Senate on October 29, 2025, officially took effect January 1, 2026, with key provisions:
- **IEPS excise tax on tobacco** increased from 160% to 200%
- **New 50% levy** on digital service providers engaged in gambling and raffles
- **Capital repatriation program** allowing 15% final income tax on resources held abroad as of September 8, 2025
- **Digital platform data sharing** requirement with SAT (real-time operational data access effective April 1, 2026)
- **Banking fee limitation**: Elimination of three-quarters of banking fees to IPAB as deductible
- **Online betting tax increase** from 30% to 50%

The **gasoline IEPS** was adjusted for inflation (Magna +MX$0.24/liter, Premium +MX$0.21/liter, Diesel +MX$0.27/liter), but President Sheinbaum secured a voluntary agreement with gas station operators to **absorb the increase**, preventing pass-through to consumers. Magna regular gasoline remains below MX$24/liter.

### Plan Mexico Initiative

President Claudia Sheinbaum presented **"Plan Mexico"** in early January 2026, outlining a national development vision with 13 goals aimed at reducing poverty and inequality. The plan entails **$277 billion in national and foreign investments**, though specific fiscal implementation mechanisms remain under development. The initiative represents Sheinbaum's framework for sustainable growth, though skepticism abounds given fiscal constraints and lack of detailed project pipeline.

### Legislative Tracker - Key Developments

**Recently Enacted Laws (November-December 2025):**

1. **Ley General de Aguas (December 11, 2025)** - Regulates human right to water access and sanitation. **Prohibits water concession transfers between private parties**, requiring Conagua to redistribute them. Replaces 1992-2025 water market regime with state stewardship model.

2. **Ley de Desarrollo Sustentable de la Cafeticultura (December 19, 2025)** - Regulates coffee production, distribution, and commercialization. Creates National Commission for Coffee Development and information system. Enters force 180 days after publication (June 2026).

3. **Health Law Reform - Vaping Ban (December 9, 2025)** - **Prohibits electronic cigarettes and vaping devices nationwide**. Imposes fines of 100-2,000 times UMA (11,314-226,280 pesos) and 1-8 years prison for commercial activities. Approved 324-129 votes.

4. **Ley General para Prevenir, Investigar y Sancionar los Delitos en Materia de Extorsión (November 29, 2025)** - Establishes **15-25 year prison sentences** for extortion, with penalties **reaching 42 years** under aggravating circumstances. Makes extortion prosecutable ex officio without victim complaint.

5. **Ley de Ingresos de la Federación 2026 (November 7, 2025)** - Federal revenue law projecting 8.721 trillion pesos (+519B vs 2025), tax revenue target 5.8 trillion (15.1% GDP).

**Under Committee Review (Expected 2026 Action):**

- **Electoral Reform** - Proposes reduction/elimination of proportional representation legislators, district redefinition, restructuring of local tribunals. Expected mid-January 2026 extraordinary session.
- **40-Hour Workweek** - Constitutional reform reducing workweek from 48 to 40 hours without salary reduction. Under analysis for 2026 approval.
- **Public Procurement Reform (Medicines)** - Improves transparency and efficiency in pharmaceutical purchasing to address shortages and corruption.
- **Organized Crime Cover-Up** - Expands criminal liability for harboring organized crime members.

**Fiscal Impact Assessment:** Water law restructuring eliminates market for concessions, potentially reducing government revenue from transfers but increasing state control. Vaping ban cuts informal economy activity but creates enforcement costs. Extortion law escalates prison population burden. No major fiscal stimulus measures under consideration.

## Monetary Policy & Central Bank

### December Policy Decision

Banxico completed its 2025 easing cycle on **December 18** with a **25bp cut to 7.00%**, the 12th consecutive reduction from the peak of 11.25% in early 2023. The decision passed **4-1**, with Deputy Governor **Jonathan Heath dissenting for the fifth consecutive meeting**, arguing for a hold at 7.25% given "stubborn core inflation" and questioning the feasibility of reaching 3% by Q3 2026.

**Forward Guidance Shift:** The December statement marked a notable **hawkish pivot**:
- **Previous language (through November):** Implied continued gradual easing
- **December 18 language:** "The Board will evaluate the timing for additional reference rate adjustments"
- **Interpretation:** Removed commitment to further cuts; introduced conditionality and data-dependency

Market consensus now expects a **pause at the February 5, 2026 meeting**, with only **two 25bp cuts in 2026** (bringing the rate to 6.50% by year-end), significantly fewer than the 4-5 cuts anticipated in mid-2025.

### Inflation Assessment

**Current Status (December 2025 First Half):**
- **Headline INPC:** 3.72% YoY (within 3% ±1% target range)
- **Core (Subyacente):** 4.34% YoY (well above 3% target)
- **Services:** 4.49% YoY (sticky, persistent)
- **Food, Beverages, Tobacco:** 5.07% YoY (elevated)
- **Agricultural Products:** -1.54% quincenal (volatile, seasonal)
- **Energy & Gov. Tariffs:** +2.4% YoY (subdued by subsidies)

**Banxico's Dilemma:** Headline inflation returned to the target range, but core and services inflation remain stubbornly elevated. The central bank has emphasized that **convergence to 3% requires sustained core deceleration**, particularly in services. Governor Victoria Rodríguez Ceja cited **economic slack** and **peso appreciation** as disinflationary forces justifying December's cut, but acknowledged **upside risks** from:
- 12.1% minimum wage increase (January 1, 2026)
- IEPS tax increases (January 1, 2026)
- Potential U.S. tariffs
- Peso depreciation (USD/MXN at 20.0-20.7)

**Inflation Forecasts:**
- **Banxico:** 3.0% by Q3 2026 (later than previous timeline)
- **Private Sector Survey:** 3.88% end-2026, 3.75% end-2025
- **Deputy Governor Heath:** Publicly stated forecasts face "credibility crisis" given difficulty of reaching 3% target

### Hawk-Dove Spectrum

**Hawkish:**
- **Jonathan Heath (Deputy Governor):** Dissented 5 consecutive meetings since mid-2025. Argues core inflation stubborn, forecasts lack credibility, premature easing. Rare public critique of institutional forecasting.

**Neutral/Data-Dependent:**
- **Victoria Rodríguez Ceja (Governor):** Supported December cut but shifted to more cautious forward guidance. Emphasizes economic slack, peso appreciation, but "evaluate timing" for future moves.
- **Galia Borja Gómez, Omar Mejía Castelazo, José Gabriel Cuadra García (Deputy Governors):** Aligned with majority; no dissents in recent period. Cohesive bloc supporting gradual easing.

**Evolution:** Governor Rodríguez Ceja moved from **neutral-dovish (Oct-Nov)** to **neutral-cautious (Dec)**. Heath maintained **hawkish stance** but intensified public criticism. Majority coalition remained stable with 4-1 votes throughout 2025.

### External Environment Considerations

**Fed Policy:** Banxico noted U.S. monetary policy "could contribute to cutting rates in the foreseeable future," though December guidance suggests less confidence in parallel Fed cuts.

**Exchange Rate:** MXN appreciation cited as key disinflationary factor, helping mitigate merchandise price pressures. Peso strength in Q4 2025 despite fiscal concerns reflected carry trade appeal and nearshoring optimism, though December 2025-January 2026 saw depreciation to 20.0-20.7 range.

**Trump Tariff Threats:** Late December 2025 threats of 25% tariffs on Mexican imports emerged but not explicitly addressed in December 18 announcement. Governor Rodríguez Ceja made subsequent comments noting MXN gains from "evolving US tariff views."
