## Executive Summary

Mexican banks reported a challenging Q3 2025 earnings season, with sector-wide profits declining 2.1% in real terms to MXN 226.1 billion through September as Banxico's rate-cutting cycle compressed net interest margins after a three-year boom. The macro picture reveals divergent signals: robust consumer lending growth (+12% YoY with auto loans surging +31%) contrasts with slowing overall credit expansion (+5.1% real), while asset quality remains solid with NPL ratios stable-to-improving (BBVA 2.8%, Banorte 1.4%) despite isolated commercial loan provisions. Forward guidance indicates banks expect GDP growth near 1% for 2025 (down from earlier forecasts), further 50bps of Banxico rate cuts to 7.0%, and MXN at 18.80 by year-end, with ROE targets holding at 22%+ despite NIM compression headwinds.

- **Evidence:** Mexican Banking Sector (CNBV Data, Oct 2025): Sector profits fell 2.1% in real terms through September after 3.5-year boom driven by rate-hike cycle that peaked at 11.25% (April 2023-March 2024). Rate cuts from 11.25% → 7.25% (Nov 2025) compressed interest income.

### 2. **Consumer Credit Surge Masks Commercial Loan Caution**

- **Evidence:** Banorte (Q3 2025): Isolated commercial loan provision recognized; NPL ratio rose to 1.4% from lower levels, though cost of risk guidance maintained at 1.8-2.0% for full year.
- **Evidence:** BBVA México (Q3 2025): Lending up 9.8%, deposits +13.7%, but modest 1.0% profit growth signals margin squeeze.

### 3. **Asset Quality Solid but Early Warning Signs Emerging**
- **Evidence:** BBVA Group (Q3 2025): NPL ratio improved to 2.8% (from 3.3% prior year), coverage ratio strengthened to 84% (from 75%). Cost of risk 1.35% for 9M25, better than expected.
- **Evidence:** Banorte (Q3 2025): NPL 1.4%, cost of risk 2.7% in Q3 (above full-year 1.8-2% guidance due to Bineo MXN 1.3B impairment + isolated commercial case). Management maintains guidance confidence for full year.
- **Evidence:** Santander Global (Q3 2025): NPL ratio stable at 2.92%, cost of risk improved to 1.13% YoY and QoQ. CEO stated Mexico cost of risk "well below 3%."

### 4. **Economic Growth Downgrades Temper 2026 Outlook**
- **Evidence:** BBVA Research (Oct 2025): Revised Mexico GDP growth forecast upward to +0.7% (from -0.4% contraction), but still materially below historical trends.
- **Evidence:** Banorte (Q3 2025 Guidance): Expects MXN at 18.80/USD by year-end, two additional 25bp Banxico cuts in Q4 2025 (implying 7.0% terminal rate).
- **Evidence:** Sector Data (CNBV, Oct 2025): Current loans growth to non-financial private sector slowed to 5.1% in real terms annually; deposit growth 3.9% (1.4pp below 1H25 average).

### 5. **Digital Banking and Nearshoring Drive Strategic Positioning**
- **Evidence:** Santander (Q3 2025): "Mexico Gravity" digital platform implemented in October; part of broader digital transformation to capture nearshoring-related commercial banking opportunities.
- **Evidence:** Banorte (Q3 2025): Proposed extraordinary dividend of MXN 6.99 per share, signaling confidence despite margin headwinds; emphasis on consumer digital channels for volume growth.

## Macro Implications

### Near-term (1-2 quarters)

**Expected Trajectory:**
- **Credit Growth**: Deceleration to 4-5% real as consumer leverage peaks and commercial caution persists
- **NPLs**: Gradual uptick to 1.5-1.8% sector average (from ~1.4%) as isolated commercial stress broadens
- **Profitability**: Continued compression; sector profit growth flat-to-negative in nominal terms through 1H26

**Key Risks to Monitor:**
1. **Consumer Over-Leverage**: Auto/credit card surge may lead to delinquency uptick if employment weakens
2. **Commercial Real Estate**: Construction slowdown + government spending cuts could trigger provisions
3. **Sovereign-Bank Nexus**: PEMEX credit stress ($100B debt) spilling over to banks with exposure
4. **FX Volatility**: MXN weakness beyond 20/USD would hurt dollarized corporate borrowers

**Potential Policy Responses:**
- **Banxico**: Pause rate cuts at 7.0% if NPLs accelerate or inflation re-anchors above 4%
- **CNBV**: Increase capital buffers or loan-loss reserve requirements if systemic stress emerges
- **Fiscal**: Government credit guarantee programs for SMEs to offset lending contraction

### Investment Implications

**Sectors Likely to Benefit:**
- **Consumer Durables/Auto**: Continued loan growth supports vehicle sales, appliances, electronics
- **Fintech/Digital Payments**: Banks investing in digital channels (Santander Gravity) → partner opportunities
- **Logistics/Industrial RE**: Nearshoring-driven credit flowing to warehousing, manufacturing facilities

**Sectors Likely to Suffer:**
- **Commercial Real Estate**: Lending pullback amid oversupply, government spending cuts
- **Retail (Non-Essential)**: Consumer credit tightening if delinquencies rise → discretionary spending pressure
- **Energy (Contractors)**: PEMEX credit stress reducing project finance availability

**Mexican Equity Market Sentiment Indicators:**

- **Dividend Appeal**: Banorte extraordinary dividend (MXN 6.99) signals capital return priority → defensive investor positioning

**Relative Attractiveness vs Other LatAm:**
- **Mexico**: Superior asset quality (NPL ~1.4-2.8%) vs Brazil (~3-4%), Chile (~2-3%)
- **ROE Competitiveness**: Mexico 18-22% vs Brazil 15-18%, Chile 12-15%
- **Growth Handicap**: Mexico 1% GDP vs Brazil 2-3%, Chile 1.5-2% → lower loan growth upside
- **FX Stability**: MXN more stable than BRL, CLP → lower mark-to-market volatility
- **Verdict**: Mexico banks offer **defensive quality** over **growth**; prefer BBVA/Banorte over regional peers for capital preservation
