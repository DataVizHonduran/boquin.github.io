## Executive Summary

PEMEX remains in chronic financial distress despite massive government support totaling 380.1 billion pesos in 9M 2025 (279% of budgeted support). Q3 2025 net loss of MXN 61.2 billion ($3.3 billion) represents improvement from MXN 161.3 billion loss in Q3 2024, driven by cost cuts and lighter taxes. Total debt stabilized at $100.3 billion but remains unsustainable without continued government backstops. Oil production continues multi-year decline at 1.374 million bpd (9M 2025 avg, down 9.1% YoY), while the flagship Dos Bocas refinery operates at ~57% capacity despite $20 billion investment. Credit rating upgrades from Fitch (BB+) and Moody's (B1) reflect government support commitment, not operational improvement. Mexico's 2025-2039 energy plan targets 25 GW renewable capacity by 2030 but prioritizes CFE's state monopoly (54% minimum grid share) over private sector dynamism.

## Fiscal Impact Analysis

### Government Support Provided (2025)

**Direct Capital Contributions (9M 2025):**
- MXN 380.1 billion ($20.6 billion) injected in first 9 months
- **279.1% of budgeted support** (FY2025 budget allocated MXN 136.2 billion)
- Represents escalating government commitment beyond planned levels

**2025 Budget Support:**
- MXN 136 billion ($6.7 billion) allocated for 2025 debt payments
- Additional ~MXN 250 billion ($13 billion) planned via new government-backed investment vehicle through 2027

**Total Government Support (2019-2024):**
- Tax reductions, capital injections, early redemption of notes receivable
- Cumulative support estimated at $40+ billion since 2019

**Financing Mechanisms:**
- Direct capital contributions from federal budget
- Tax relief (reduced IEPS fuel taxes, favorable treatment)
- Government-guaranteed debt issuance
- Debt assumption/tender offers (July 2025: $9.9 billion)

### PEMEX Tax Burden

PEMEX benefits from **lighter tax treatment** in 2025 compared to historical norms:
- Fuel excise tax (IEPS) relief during low oil price periods
- Special fiscal regime distinct from private energy companies
- Tax burden reduced as part of government support strategy

**Note:** Specific 2025 tax figures not disclosed in Q3 earnings. Historical tax burden has been ~65-70% of revenues under old regime; current regime likely lower but undisclosed.

### Net Fiscal Position

**PEMEX is a net drain on federal budget:**

| Item | 9M 2025 Amount | Impact |
|------|----------------|--------|
| Government capital contributions | +MXN 380.1 bn | **Outflow from budget** |
| PEMEX tax payments | Not disclosed | Inflow to budget (reduced) |
| PEMEX dividends | MXN 0 (no profits) | No inflow |
| **Net Fiscal Impact** | **~MXN 380 bn drain** | **Significant budget pressure** |

**Context:**
- In profitable years (pre-2015), PEMEX contributed ~30% of federal revenues
- Since 2019, PEMEX has become **structural fiscal liability** requiring hundreds of billions in annual support
- 2025 support (MXN 380 bn in 9M) represents ~2% of Mexico's GDP

### Contingent Liabilities

**Government Guarantees:**
- Explicit guarantee on PEMEX debt obligations (credit rating linkage)
- $100.3 billion total debt effectively sovereign-backed
- Fitch and Moody's rate PEMEX 1-2 notches below sovereign, implying government will prevent default

**Maturity Profile Risk:**
- $9 billion maturing in 2025 (addressed via July tender offer)
- $13 billion maturing in 2026
- Without government support, PEMEX cannot refinance independently

**Fiscal Rule Implications:**
- PEMEX support crowds out other budget priorities
- Rising debt sustainability concerns for sovereign if PEMEX continues requiring bailouts
- IMF and rating agencies monitor PEMEX as contingent sovereign liability

## Policy Environment

### Recent Energy Policy Changes

**Power Sector Development Plan 2025-2039 (PLADESE):**
Published by SENER in October 2025, representing comprehensive energy sector roadmap:

**Key Provisions:**
1. **CFE Grid Dominance:** Mandates CFE maintain minimum 54% of power generation injected into grid annually
2. **Renewable Targets:** 25 GW new capacity by 2030 (19,954 MW renewables + 5,000 MW storage)
   - Solar PV: 58% of new clean capacity
   - Wind: 22%
   - Battery storage: 20%
3. **State-Led Development:** CFE to develop 69.2% of total new renewable capacity (private sector 30.8%)
4. **Binding Planning:** SENER granted authority to issue binding planning instruments, subordinating private sector to state priorities
5. **Clean Energy Goal:** Minimum 35% clean energy share by 2030

**Analysis:**
- Represents **hybrid model**: renewable ambitions but state-monopoly structure
- Private sector participation allowed but secondary to CFE
- Contrasts with 2013-2018 energy reform which prioritized competitive markets

### Refinery Projects

**Dos Bocas (Olmeca) Refinery:**
- **Investment:** ~$20 billion (final cost TBD; originally budgeted $8 billion)
- **Design Capacity:** 340,000 bpd crude processing
- **Current Output:** ~195,000 bpd (September 2025) = **57% utilization**
- **Target Capacity:** PEMEX aims to reach 320,000 bpd "soon"
- **Status:** Operational but plagued by technical issues, downtime, and cost overruns
- **Strategic Rationale:** Reduce gasoline/diesel imports, achieve "energy sovereignty"

**Performance Issues:**
- Frequent shutdowns and restarts (restarted after shutdown in mid-2025)

- National Refining System averaged 1.009 million bpd in 9M 2025 (up 4.8% YoY)

### Private Sector Participation

**Hydrocarbon Upstream:**
- 2013-2018 energy reform opened oil/gas exploration to private firms
- Current administration (2018-2025) de-emphasized private E&P partnerships
- CNH (Comisión Nacional de Hidrocarburos) continues regulating but with reduced auction activity
- PEMEX retains dominant position in upstream; private sector limited to marginal fields

**Power Sector:**
- 2025-2039 plan allows private renewable development but subordinated to CFE
- Private sector confined to ~30% of new renewable capacity
- Legacy private power purchase agreements (PPAs) from reform era remain valid but politically contested

**Outlook:**
- Sheinbaum administration (2024-2030) signals continuity with AMLO's statist approach
- Limited appetite for expanded private sector role
- Private investment contingent on regulatory clarity and CFE cooperation

### Renewable Energy vs. Fossil Fuels

**Policy Balance:**
Mexico's 2025-2039 energy plan presents **contradictory signals**:

**Renewable Ambitions:**
- 25 GW new renewables + storage by 2030
- 35% clean energy target by 2030
- Solar and wind prioritized in capacity additions

**Fossil Fuel Reality:**
- Continued heavy investment in PEMEX oil production and refining (Dos Bocas, rehabilitation)
- CFE reliance on natural gas and fuel oil for baseload generation
- Energy sovereignty narrative prioritizes hydrocarbons over renewables

**Net Assessment:**
- **Rhetoric:** Pro-renewable, climate-conscious
- **Actions:** Fossil fuel-centric, state monopoly prioritized
- **Outcome:** Renewables will grow but fossil fuels remain dominant through 2030+

## Market Context

### Oil Price Environment

**Relevant Benchmarks (Approx. Nov 2025):**
- **Brent Crude:** ~$72-75/bbl (range in late 2025)
- **WTI Crude:** ~$68-71/bbl
- **Mexican Crude Basket:** ~$65-68/bbl (discount to Brent due to heavy crude quality)

**Impact on PEMEX:**
- Q3 2025 revenue decline (-11.1%) partially driven by **lower international prices** vs Q3 2024
- Export volumes also down due to government strategy to prioritize domestic refining
- PEMEX breakeven estimated at ~$55-60/bbl; current prices marginally profitable on production but insufficient to cover debt service

**Price Sensitivity:**
- Every $1/bbl change in Mexican basket = ~$500 million annual revenue impact
- PEMEX highly vulnerable to oil price volatility given fixed cost structure and debt burden

### Currency Effects (USD/MXN)

**PEMEX Exposure:**
- **Dollar-denominated debt:** $100.3 billion (100% of total debt)
- **Peso-denominated revenues:** ~80% of revenues (domestic sales in pesos)
- **FX Mismatch:** Creates balance sheet vulnerability to peso depreciation

**Recent USD/MXN Dynamics:**
- Peso depreciated slightly in late 2025 (approx. MXN 18-19 per USD range)
- Each 1 peso depreciation increases peso-denominated debt by ~5%
- Government support partially mitigates FX risk but doesn't eliminate it

**Hedging:**
- Mexico's sovereign oil hedge (annual program) provides revenue floor
- PEMEX benefits indirectly but hedge is sovereign instrument, not PEMEX-specific

### Regional Competitive Dynamics

**Gulf of Mexico Competitors:**
- **US Producers:** Chevron, ExxonMobil, Shell active in US GoM; outcompete PEMEX on technology and efficiency
- **Deepwater Economics:** PEMEX lacks technical capability and capital for deepwater; production concentrated in mature shallow-water fields

**PEMEX Competitive Position:**
- **Production Decline:** Multi-year trend (-9.1% in 9M 2025) vs stable/growing US GoM output
- **Reserve Replacement:** PEMEX struggling to replace reserves; R/P ratio declining
- **Technology Gap:** Lacks EOR, deepwater, and shale capabilities of international majors

**Refining Competition:**
- **US Gulf Coast Refiners:** Export gasoline/diesel to Mexico at competitive prices
- **Dos Bocas Economics:** $20 billion investment for 340k bpd capacity = ~$58,800/bpd capital cost (2-3x industry norm)
- **Import Dependency:** Despite Dos Bocas, Mexico still imports ~40% of gasoline/diesel

**Net Assessment:**
PEMEX is **structurally uncompetitive** vs international peers due to:
1. Technical/operational inefficiencies
2. Legacy cost structure and labor agreements
3. Political mandates (employment, domestic content) that increase costs
4. Chronic underinvestment in upstream (diverted to debt service)

## Forward Outlook

**2025 Government Target:**
- **1.76 million bpd** crude oil production (full year)

**Actual 9M 2025 Performance:**
- **1.374 million bpd** average
- **Gap:** -386,000 bpd (-22% below target)

**Feasibility Assessment:**
- **Unlikely to meet 2025 target** given:
  - 9M production 22% below goal
  - Multi-year declining trend (field depletion)
  - Insufficient capex for major new production (funds diverted to debt/operations)

**Medium-Term Outlook (2026-2027):**
- PEMEX guidance: Production stabilization at ~1.4-1.5 million bpd
- No major new field developments announced
- Dos Bocas refining ramp-up may reduce export volumes further, masking production declines

**Upside Scenario:**
- Successful exploitation of Zama field (shared with US operator Talos Energy)
- Enhanced recovery in existing fields (Cantarell, KMZ)
- **Probability:** Low without sustained capex increase

**Downside Scenario:**
- Continued decline toward 1.2-1.3 million bpd by 2027
- Mature field depletion accelerates
- **Probability:** Moderate to high given historical trends

### Debt Sustainability Assessment

**Current Debt Metrics:**
- **Total Debt:** $100.3 billion
- **Debt/EBITDA:** Estimated ~15x+ (EBITDA severely depressed by operational losses)
- **Interest Coverage:** Negative (net losses mean no EBIT to cover interest)

**Debt Maturity Profile:**
- **2025:** $9 billion (addressed via July tender offer)
- **2026:** $13 billion
- **2027+:** Balance of ~$78 billion (long-term bonds)

**Sustainability Without Government:**
- **Unsustainable** - PEMEX cannot service debt from operating cash flow
- Q3 2025 net loss of MXN 61 billion + interest expense = deep cash burn
- Free cash flow negative; relies on government capital injections

**Sustainability With Government:**
- **Conditionally Sustainable** if:
  1. Government continues annual support of MXN 200-400 billion
  2. Oil prices remain above $60/bbl
  3. Production stabilizes (doesn't decline further)
  4. Refining margins improve (Dos Bocas ramps up)

**Rating Agency View:**
- Fitch (BB+) and Moody's (B1) upgrades reflect **government support assumption**, not standalone credit quality
- Fitch notes standalone rating remains CCC (deep junk)
- One notch below sovereign (BBB-) implies government will prevent default

**Fiscal Sustainability for Mexico:**
- PEMEX support at MXN 380 billion (9M 2025) = ~2% of GDP
- Sustainable in near term given Mexico's fiscal position (debt/GDP ~50%)
- **Risk:** Crowding out of other priorities, political backlash, rating agency concerns if support escalates further

**Verdict:** PEMEX debt is **sovereign contingent liability**—sustainable only via continued government bailouts.

### Government Support Expectations

**2026-2027 Baseline:**
- Expect **MXN 150-200 billion annual** direct support (lower than 2025 but still substantial)
- Additional support via tax relief, debt guarantees, and refinancing assistance

**Sheinbaum Administration Commitment:**
- President Claudia Sheinbaum (Oct 2024-Sep 2030) has pledged PEMEX sustainability
- Target: "PEMEX will no longer need finance ministry support by 2027" (per Sheinbaum)
- **Assessment:** Highly optimistic; would require dramatic production turnaround and/or oil price surge

**Mechanisms:**
- New government-backed investment vehicle: MXN 250 billion ($13 billion) through 2027
- Continued budget allocations for debt service
- Debt tender offers to smooth maturity profile

**Political Economy:**
- PEMEX is **politically untouchable**—national symbol, major employer
- No appetite for restructuring, privatization, or bankruptcy
- Support will continue regardless of economic cost

### Energy Security Implications

**Mexico's Energy Self-Sufficiency:**
- **Oil:** Net exporter but volumes declining
- **Natural Gas:** Net importer (~60% from US via pipeline)
- **Refined Products:** Net importer of gasoline/diesel (~40% despite Dos Bocas)
- **Electricity:** Self-sufficient but dependent on natural gas imports

**PEMEX Role in Energy Security:**
- Critical for domestic crude oil supply (100% production via PEMEX)
- Refining capacity expansion (Dos Bocas) aims to reduce import dependency
- **Reality:** Declining production undermines energy security narrative

**Risks:**
1. **Production Decline:** Accelerating decline could push Mexico toward net oil import status by 2030+
2. **Natural Gas Dependency:** Reliance on US gas creates vulnerability to supply disruptions
3. **Refining Inefficiency:** Dos Bocas underperformance perpetuates import dependency

**Strategic Alternatives:**
- **Private Sector Upstream:** Open deepwater, shale to private investment (politically unlikely)
- **Renewable Acceleration:** Reduce fossil fuel dependency via faster renewable deployment
- **Regional Integration:** Deepen energy ties with US/Canada (USMCA framework)

**Likely Path:** Continued state-led model with gradual erosion of energy self-sufficiency.

### Sustainability and Energy Transition

**Mexico's Climate Commitments:**
- Paris Agreement: Reduce GHG emissions 22% by 2030 (vs BAU)
- Long-term: Net-zero by 2050 (aspirational)

**PEMEX's Role in Transition:**
- **Obstacle:** PEMEX prioritizes fossil fuel production, refining
- **Methane Emissions:** PEMEX is major methane emitter; limited flaring/leakage controls
- **Capex Allocation:** Funds directed to oil/gas, not renewables

**2025-2039 Energy Plan:**
- **Progress:** 25 GW renewable capacity by 2030 is meaningful
- **Limitations:** Fossil fuels (oil, gas, coal) remain dominant in energy mix
- **CFE Role:** State utility will develop renewables but also relies on fossil baseload

**Just Transition Concerns:**
- PEMEX employs ~125,000 directly + hundreds of thousands in supply chain
- Energy transition risks job losses in oil-dependent regions (Tabasco, Campeche, Veracruz)
- Government prioritizes employment over efficiency/transition speed

**Investment Community View:**
- **ESG Concerns:** PEMEX is poor ESG investment (governance failures, environmental issues, social risks)
- **Stranded Assets:** Risk of oil reserves becoming stranded if transition accelerates
- **Transition Bonds:** Mexico has issued green bonds but PEMEX excluded from green finance

**Outlook:**
- **Slow Transition:** Mexico will lag global energy transition due to PEMEX/CFE inertia
- **2030 Scenario:** Fossil fuels still 60-70% of energy mix despite renewable growth
- **Risk:** Climate-related liabilities, carbon pricing could further strain PEMEX finances

## Key Risks and Catalysts

### Downside Risks

1. **Oil Price Collapse:**
   - Sustained <$55/bbl crude would devastate PEMEX finances
   - Government support requirements could double
   - Fiscal crisis for Mexico if oil revenues collapse

2. **Accelerated Production Decline:**
   - Production falling below 1.2 million bpd by 2027
   - Major fields (Cantarell, KMZ) depleting faster than expected
   - Insufficient capex to develop new reserves

3. **Dos Bocas Failure:**
   - Refinery unable to reach >70% capacity utilization
   - Technical failures force extended shutdown
   - $20 billion investment stranded

4. **Peso Devaluation:**
   - USD/MXN moves to 22-25 range (crisis scenario)
   - $100 billion USD debt balloons in peso terms
   - Debt sustainability crisis

5. **Sovereign Rating Downgrade:**
   - Mexico downgraded below BBB- (investment grade) due to PEMEX contingent liability
   - PEMEX automatically downgraded (linkage to sovereign)
   - Borrowing costs spike

6. **Political Transition Risk:**
   - Post-2030 administration reduces PEMEX support
   - Fiscal retrenchment forces PEMEX restructuring
   - Default/bankruptcy scenario

### Upside Catalysts

1. **Oil Price Rally:**
   - Brent sustained above $90/bbl due to geopolitical shocks
   - PEMEX profitability improves, reduces gov't support need
   - Debt reduction accelerates

2. **Production Stabilization:**
   - Zama field (Talos/PEMEX partnership) comes online with 100k+ bpd
   - Enhanced recovery in existing fields succeeds
   - Production stabilizes at 1.5 million bpd

3. **Dos Bocas Success:**
   - Refinery reaches 90%+ capacity utilization by mid-2026
   - Refining margins improve due to heavy crude advantage
   - Import substitution reduces forex drain

4. **Debt Restructuring:**
   - Successful liability management reduces debt to $80 billion
   - Maturity profile extended beyond 2030
   - Interest expense declines

5. **Private Sector Partnership:**
   - PEMEX opens upstream/midstream to minority private stakes
   - Technology transfer and capex sharing improve efficiency
   - Production decline reversed

6. **Energy Transition Pivot:**
   - PEMEX pivots to renewable/low-carbon business lines
   - Carbon capture projects generate revenue
   - "Green PEMEX" attracts ESG investment

**Probability Assessment:**
- **Downside Risks:** Moderate to high (40-60% probability of one or more materializing by 2027)
- **Upside Catalysts:** Low to moderate (20-30% probability of meaningful upside by 2027)

**Base Case:** Continued muddling through with high government support, declining production, and marginal debt reduction.
