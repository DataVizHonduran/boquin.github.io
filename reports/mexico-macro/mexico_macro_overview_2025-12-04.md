## Executive Summary

Mexico's macroeconomic picture as of late November 2025 presents a study in contrasts: strong political capital (President Sheinbaum's 70-73% approval rating, MORENA's electoral dominance ahead of 2027 mid-terms) coexists with economic headwinds (1% GDP growth forecast, PEMEX's $100 billion debt burden requiring MXN 380 billion in 9-month government support). The Sheinbaum administration's fiscal consolidation targets (5.9% to 3.9% deficit reduction) face skepticism from analysts given structural spending commitments and PEMEX's chronic losses. Banxico navigates this environment with a measured easing cycle (11 consecutive cuts from 11.25% peak to 7.25% in November), though internal dissent from Deputy Governor Jonathan Heath signals hawk-dove divisions on inflation convergence timelines. International investors maintain cautious optimism on nearshoring potential (BlackRock, Ashmore overweights) while hedging against political risks from constitutional reforms and potential US trade tensions under Trump. The energy sector remains a fiscal albatross, with Dos Bocas refinery operating at 57% capacity despite $20 billion investment and oil production declining 9.1% YoY. Inflation moderated to 3.57% (October) but sticky core at 4.28% and services inflation at 4%+ reflect structural rigidities that constrain monetary policy flexibility.

**Key Themes:**
- **Political Strength, Economic Softness:** Sheinbaum's popularity insulates MORENA from weak growth and fiscal strains; 2027 mid-term polling shows MORENA-led coalition retaining 50-55% Chamber support
- **PEMEX as Structural Liability:** Government support at 279% of budget (MXN 380B in 9M25) crowds out priorities; debt sustainability depends entirely on continued bailouts, not operational turnaround
- **Monetary-Fiscal Divergence:** Banxico easing into economic weakness while fiscal policy tightens (nominally), creating policy contradictions and questioning inflation convergence to 3% target by Q3 2026

## Fiscal & Political Developments

### Budget and Deficit Targets

The Sheinbaum administration submitted a **MXN 9.3 trillion budget for 2025** targeting deficit reduction from 5.9% to 3.9% of GDP, with a primary fiscal surplus of 0.6% (vs 1.4% deficit in 2024). While nominal spending increases 2.6%, this represents a **3.6% real-terms cut**, with defense slashed 44% and reductions in health, education, and environment. The government prioritizes social programs (MXN 835.7 billion allocated) and infrastructure, particularly rail projects (MXN 189 billion).

**Skepticism from Markets:**
Analysts doubt the 3.9% target, citing optimistic macroeconomic assumptions (2-3% GDP growth forecast vs actual ~1%), consumption slowdown, lower remittances, and reduced investment. The fiscal consolidation effort is undermined by:
- **PEMEX Burden:** MXN 380.1 billion government support in 9M 2025 alone (279% of budgeted MXN 136.2 billion), representing ~2% of GDP
- **State Enterprise Losses:** CFE reported USD 2.3 million Q2 2025 loss, with energy sales barely covering fuel/labor costs
- **Social Pension Pressure:** Set to consume 22% of federal budget in 2024, increased pension stipends create structural spending rigidity

### Credit Ratings and External Assessment

All major agencies maintain investment-grade ratings but with divergent outlooks:
- **Moody's:** Baa2 with **negative outlook** - expects deficit to decline from 5%+ (2024) to ~4% (2025) but warns federal debt burden will continue growing; PEMEX remains structural challenge
- **S&P, Fitch, DBRS:** BBB ratings with **stable outlooks**

The **IMF Article IV Consultation (October 23, 2025)** emphasized that "strengthening financial health and profitability of state-owned enterprises, especially PEMEX, is essential" for fiscal sustainability.

### Congressional Actions and Constitutional Reforms

MORENA's legislative supermajority (elected June 2024) has aggressively reshaped institutions:
- **Regulatory Agency Elimination (Nov 29, 2024):** Senate dissolved seven independent oversight agencies, transferring functions to government ministries; critics warn of weakened transparency
- **17 Constitutional Reforms (through Jan 2025):** Including judicial system overhaul (popular election of judges implemented June 2025), energy sector restructuring (PEMEX/CFE redesignated from "productive" to "public" state companies)
- **Energy Sector Laws (January 2025):** Six new laws presented (PEMEX Law, CFE Law, Energy Planning and Transition Law, Electricity Sector Law, Hydrocarbons Law, National Energy Commission Law), mandating CFE maintain minimum 54% grid share

**Recent Legislative Activity (as of Nov 17, 2025):**
1. **National Development Plan 2025-2030** (Approved Nov 5): Establishes strategic framework across four general axes and three transversal axes
2. **Constitutional Reform - Articles 39 and 40 (Sovereignty)** (Introduced Feb 20): Prohibits foreign intervention in investigations without Mexican authorization; applies severe penalties to foreigners involved in gun smuggling (response to US anti-cartel measures)
3. **General Law on Artificial Intelligence** (Introduced Feb 19): Grants Congress authority to regulate AI, mandates passage within 180 days
4. **2026 Economic Package Customs/Tariff Reforms** (Introduced Sep 9): Stricter customs compliance, increased import duties on 1,463 product classifications

### Tax Policy: "No New Taxes" Rhetoric vs. Reality

President Sheinbaum has repeatedly stated intention to avoid tax rate increases, relying on improved collection efficiency and digitization. However, the **2026 budget blueprint** (approved by Senate in October 2025) includes multiple stealth tax hikes:
- **Excise Tax Increases:** Betting/sweepstakes tax raised from 30% to 50%; tobacco, flavored beverages, gambling rates increased
- **Financial Withholding:** Rate on capital in financial system increased from 0.5% to 0.9%
- **Repatriation Incentive:** New preferential 15% corporate income tax rate for repatriating legally sourced resources for productive activities
- **Revenue Target:** 5.7% increase in tax revenues for 2026

**PEMEX Fiscal Regime Reform (March 18, 2025):** Repealed previous hydrocarbon duties, replaced with unified "Petroleum Welfare Duty" at 30% rate on extracted hydrocarbon value (11.63% for non-associated gas). This **lightens PEMEX's tax burden** as part of government support strategy.

## Monetary Policy & Central Bank

### Rate Cuts and Board Dynamics

Banxico has executed **11 consecutive rate cuts** from the peak of 11.25% (held April 2023-March 2024) to **7.25%** (November 6, 2025). The November decision was a **4-1 split vote**, with Deputy Governor Jonathan Heath dissenting for the **fourth consecutive meeting** since September.

**Governor Victoria Rodríguez Ceja** signaled on November 10 that "another rate cut is highly probable and already discounted by the market... we would continue the cycle of cuts." Market consensus expects a December cut to 7.00%, with terminal rate near this level by year-end 2025.

### Hawk-Dove Spectrum

The Board shows clear ideological split:

**HAWKISH:**
- **Jonathan Heath Constable:** Voted against all cuts since Sep 2025 (4 consecutive dissents). Rationale: "Premature monetary easing would lead to reaching neutrality levels in an environment of still elevated inflation with regressive effects on the general population... the objective is not to maintain inflation within the variability range, but to sustainably reach the specific target of three percent." Emphasizes core inflation at 4.24% is well above the 3% target, not merely within the 2-4% tolerance band.

**NEUTRAL/DATA-DEPENDENT:**
- **Galia Borja Gómez:** Voted for Nov cut but emphasizes external risks and trade uncertainty (HSBC IMF Roundtable October speech noted "uncertainty has increased due to new trade policies")
- **José Gabriel Cuadra García** (appointed Feb 2025): Limited public statements; voting record suggests moderate dovish tilt

**DOVISH:**
- **Victoria Rodríguez Ceja (Governor):** Shifted from neutral/cautious (early 2025) to explicitly dovish (Nov 2025), emphasizing economic slack (Q3 GDP contracted 0.3% vs Q2) and weak activity
- **Omar Mejía Castelazo:** Most dovish member; reportedly believes Banxico can cut even with policy at neutral, willing to ease into accommodative territory

**Key Shift Identified:** Forward guidance language softened from "assessing further adjustments" (September) to "evaluate reducing the reference rate" (November), suggesting Board majority still dovish but introducing more conditionality, potentially setting up for pause in early 2026 if inflation stalls.

### Inflation Outlook and Forecasts

**Current Inflation (October 2025):**
- Headline INPC: 3.63% (early Oct) → 3.57% (end Oct), within Banxico's 3% ± 1% target
- Core inflation: 4.24%-4.26%, **sticky and well above target**
- Non-core: 1.18% (agricultural deflation offsetting energy spike)

**Banxico Quarterly Report Forecasts:**

- **Convergence to 3% target:** Expected Q3 2026 (delayed from earlier forecasts)

**Private Sector Expectations (Nov 20, 2025 Survey):**

- Year-end 2025 core: 4.23% (up from 4.20%)
- Year-end 2026 headline: 3.91% (up from 3.90%)

**Concerning Trend:** Inflation expectations for 2026 are **drifting upward**, suggesting some de-anchoring risk that validates Heath's hawkish caution.

### Growth Assessment

- **Q3 2025 GDP:** Contracted 0.3% vs Q2 (sequential quarterly decline)
- **Banxico 2025 forecast:** Raised to 0.6% from 0.1% (Quarterly Report)
- **Market consensus:** 0.5% for 2025, 1.4% for 2026

The Board majority cites "slack economic conditions" and "weakness of economic activity" as justification for easing. Heath counters that monetary easing cannot resolve structural/fiscal issues and risks inflation.

### External Environment and FX

- **USD/MXN:** Peso depreciated slightly between Sep-Nov meetings; recent range ~18.45 per USD
- **Fed Policy:** Not explicitly discussed in recent statements, but Banxico easing occurs amid global central bank divergence
- **Trade Policy Uncertainty:** Galia Borja noted "uncertainty has increased due to new trade policies" in October speech; November announcement cited "environment of uncertainty and trade tensions"

**Fiscal Policy Interaction:** Unlike Brazil's BCB which actively discusses fiscal risks, Banxico has **not emphasized fiscal-monetary coordination issues** in recent communications, despite PEMEX burden and deficit concerns. This may reflect deliberate separation of policy domains or relatively stable fiscal outlook compared to peers.

## Energy Sector - PEMEX & Production

### PEMEX: Chronic Financial Distress Despite Massive Support

**Q3 2025 Financial Snapshot:**
- **Net Loss:** MXN 61.2 billion ($3.3 billion) - improvement from MXN 161.3 billion loss in Q3 2024 (+62% improvement)
- **Total Debt:** $100.3 billion (stable from $106.9 billion Q3 2024, -6.8%)
- **Revenue:** MXN 378.9 billion (down 11.1% from MXN 426.1 billion Q3 2024)
- **Government Support (9M 2025):** MXN 380.1 billion - **279% of budgeted support** (FY2025 budget allocated MXN 136.2 billion)

**Production Metrics:**
- **Crude Oil:** 1.374 million bpd (9M 2025 avg) - **down 9.1% YoY**, 22% below government target of 1.76 million bpd
- **Refining Throughput:** 1.009 million bpd (up 4.8% YoY)
- **Dos Bocas Refinery:** ~195,000 bpd (September) = **57% utilization** of 340,000 bpd design capacity

**Debt Sustainability Assessment:**
- **Without Government:** Unsustainable - PEMEX cannot service debt from operating cash flow (Q3 net loss + interest expense = deep cash burn)
- **With Government:** Conditionally sustainable if: (1) Annual support of MXN 200-400 billion continues, (2) Oil prices remain above $60/bbl, (3) Production stabilizes, (4) Refining margins improve
- **Rating Agency View:** Fitch (BB+, Aug 1 2025 upgrade) and Moody's (B1, Sep 8 2025 upgrade) reflect **government support assumption**, not standalone credit quality (standalone: CCC)

### Fiscal Impact Analysis

**Government Support Mechanisms (2025):**
- Direct capital contributions: MXN 380.1 billion (9M 2025) = ~2% of Mexico's GDP
- Debt assumption/tender offers: $9.9 billion (July 2025)
- Tax relief: Lighter IEPS fuel taxes, favorable treatment under March 2025 fiscal regime reform
- Government-guaranteed debt issuance

**Net Fiscal Position:**
PEMEX is a **net drain on federal budget** of ~MXN 380 billion (9M 2025). In profitable years (pre-2015), PEMEX contributed ~30% of federal revenues; since 2019, it has become **structural fiscal liability**.

**Contingent Liabilities:**
- Explicit guarantee on $100.3 billion debt (Fitch/Moody's rate PEMEX 1-2 notches below sovereign, implying government will prevent default)
- Maturity profile: $13 billion maturing in 2026, balance ~$78 billion long-term
- **Fiscal Rule Implications:** PEMEX support crowds out other budget priorities; IMF and rating agencies monitor as contingent sovereign liability

### Dos Bocas Refinery: $20 Billion Investment, 57% Utilization

**Policy Contradictions:**
