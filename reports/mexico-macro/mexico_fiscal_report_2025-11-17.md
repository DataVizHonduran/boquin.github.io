## Executive Summary

The Sheinbaum administration's fiscal policy over the past month has centered on deficit reduction targets while navigating substantial headwinds from PEMEX's deteriorating finances and declining growth projections. The government submitted a MXN 9.3 trillion budget proposal for 2025 targeting a deficit reduction from 5.9% to 3.9% of GDP, though analysts express skepticism about the optimistic assumptions. Congressional supermajority power enabled passage of energy sector reforms and controversial elimination of independent regulatory bodies, while PEMEX's Q3 2025 results showed continued debt burden of USD 100.3 billion. Rating agencies maintain investment-grade status but Moody's negative outlook reflects concerns over fiscal sustainability and state enterprise liabilities.

## Fiscal Developments

### 2025 Budget Proposal (November 15, 2025)
- **Total Budget:** MXN 9.3 trillion (USD 460 billion) submitted to Congress by Finance Minister Rogelio Ramírez de la O
- **Deficit Target:** Reduction from 5.9% of GDP (2024) to 3.9% of GDP (2025), with primary fiscal surplus projected at 0.6% of GDP versus 1.4% deficit in 2024
- **Real Spending Cut:** Overall spending up 2.6% nominally but represents 3.6% reduction in real terms
- **Public Debt Projection:** 51.4% of GDP, considered sustainable level
- **Economic Growth Forecast:** 2-3% GDP growth for 2025
- **Revenue Projections:** MXN 8,056 billion total revenues (MXN 1,142 billion oil revenues, MXN 6,914 billion non-oil revenues)
- **Priority Social Programs:** MXN 835.7 billion allocated; infrastructure projects (mainly rail lines) receive MXN 189 billion
- **Sector Cuts:** Defense spending cut 44%; reductions in health, education, environmental areas

**Sources:** Mexico Business News, November 15, 2025; The Yucatan Times, November 2024; Scotiabank Latam Insights, November 21, 2024

### 2026 Economic Package Tax Changes (September-October 2025)
- **Excise Tax Increases:** Betting/sweepstakes tax raised from 30% to 50%; tobacco, flavored beverages, and gambling rates increased
- **Financial System Withholding:** Rate on capital invested in financial system increased from 0.5% to 0.9%
- **Repatriation Incentive:** New preferential 15% corporate income tax rate for individuals and companies repatriating legally sourced resources into Mexico for productive activities
- **Revenue Target:** 5.7% increase in tax revenues for 2026 without introducing new general taxes

**Sources:** KPMG Mexico Tax Alert, November 2024; Alvarez & Marsal, 2025; PwC Mexico, October 2025

### PEMEX Fiscal Regime Reform (March 18, 2025)
- **New Tax Structure:** Repealed Shared Utility Duty, Hydrocarbon Extraction Duty, and Hydrocarbon Exploration Duty; replaced with "Petroleum Welfare Duty"
- **Rate Structure:** Unified 30% rate on extracted hydrocarbon value (no deductions allowed); differentiated 11.63% rate for non-associated gas
- **Q3 2025 Financial Results (October 27 announcement):**
  - Total debt: USD 100.3 billion (approximately MXN 1,843 billion)
  - Capital expenditure: MXN 182.6 billion (USD 9.3 billion) executed through September 30, representing 81.6% of MXN 223.7 billion approved budget
- **Fiscal Impact:** Government issuance and guarantees continue to shift PEMEX risk to sovereign balance sheet, potentially lifting Mexico's risk premium and crowding out other public investment

**Sources:** PEMEX Q3 2025 Earnings Report, October 27, 2025; Natural Resource Governance Institute, 2025; IMF Mexico Article IV Consultation, October 23, 2025

### Plan México Tax Incentives (January 21, 2025)
- **Immediate Deduction Decree:** Allows immediate deduction of investments in new fixed assets acquired through September 30, 2030
- **Depreciation Rates:** For 2025-2026, depreciation percentages range from 41% to 91%, designed to stimulate nearshoring-related investment

**Sources:** Covington & Burling Analysis, January 2025; Alvarez & Marsal, January 2025

## Political Developments

### Congressional Supermajority Actions (October-November 2025)
- **Regulatory Agency Elimination:** Senate approved (November 29, 2024) dissolution of seven independent regulatory and oversight agencies, transferring functions to government ministries; critics warn of weakened transparency and executive oversight
- **Constitutional Reforms:** Morena-led coalition leveraged supermajority to pass 17 constitutional reforms through January 2025, including judicial system overhaul and energy regulatory restructuring
- **Legislative Leadership:** Ricardo Monreal, as Morena coalition leader in lower house, driving reform agenda with minimal opposition

**Sources:** Americas Quarterly, 2025; Freedom House Mexico Report, 2025; Wilson Center Analysis, 2025

### Energy Sector Restructuring (October 2024 - January 2025)
- **Constitutional Reform:** President Sheinbaum signed reforms redesignating PEMEX and CFE (Federal Electricity Commission) from "productive" to "public" state companies
- **Secondary Legislation:** Six new laws presented in January 2025 (PEMEX Law, CFE Law, Energy Planning and Transition Law, Electricity Sector Law, Hydrocarbons Law, National Energy Commission Law)
- **Private Participation:** CFE must maintain minimum 54% participation in mixed investment schemes; six defined schemes for private electricity market participation
- **Cost Savings Target:** MXN 50 billion targeted through elimination of redundant expenses (2024-2030 period)

**Sources:** DWF Group Energy Alert, February 2025; Ritch Mueller Client Alert, 2025; Enerdata, January 2025

### Fiscal Policy Communication
- **"No New Taxes" Rhetoric:** President Sheinbaum repeatedly stated intention to avoid tax rate increases, relying instead on improved collection efficiency
- **Reality vs. Rhetoric:** 2026 budget blueprint includes multiple tax hikes ("hiding in plain sight") despite public statements, approved by Senate in October 2025
- **Efficiency Focus:** Digitization of procedures and streamlining bureaucracy highlighted as revenue enhancement strategies without tax burden increases

**Sources:** Bloomberg, October 24-29, 2025; Wilson Center Fiscal Policy Analysis, 2025

## Market & External Context

### Credit Ratings & Agency Commentary
- **Moody's:** Baa2 rating with **negative outlook**; expects deficit decline from over 5% of GDP (2024) to about 4% (2025), but warns federal debt burden will continue growing; PEMEX remains structural challenge
- **Standard & Poor's:** BBB rating with **stable outlook**
- **Fitch:** BBB- rating with **stable outlook** (affirmed April 16, 2025)
- **DBRS:** BBB rating with **stable outlook**
- **Consensus:** All agencies maintain investment-grade status, but Moody's negative outlook reflects fiscal sustainability concerns

**Sources:** Trading Economics, Country Economy, Fitch Ratings, Moody's Reports, 2025

### IMF Assessment (October 23, 2025)
- **Article IV Consultation:** IMF Executive Board completed review, emphasizing that strengthening financial health and profitability of state-owned enterprises, especially PEMEX, is essential
- **Fiscal Concerns:** Highlighted need for fiscal consolidation amid declining revenues and persistent structural deficits

**Sources:** IMF Country Report No. 25/286, October 2025

### Analyst Skepticism
- **Deficit Target Doubts:** Analysts believe 3.9% deficit target unlikely given consumption slowdown, lower remittances, and reduced investment
- **Growth Assumptions:** Optimistic macroeconomic assumptions underpinning budget questioned; concerns about economic stagnation persist
- **PEMEX Drag:** Higher refined product imports increase pressure on PEMEX finances, with subsidies impacting fiscal balance and expanding public deficit

**Sources:** Scotiabank Latam Insights, November 2024; Natural Resource Governance Institute, 2025

### Public Spending Pressures
- **Social Pensions:** Set to consume 22% of federal budget in 2024; increased pension stipends create substantial structural spending pressure
- **State Enterprise Losses:** CFE reported USD 2.3 million loss in Q2 2025, with energy sales revenue (USD 10.3 billion) barely covering fuel and labor costs (USD 10 billion)

**Sources:** Wilson Center Analysis, 2025; Natural Gas Intel, 2025
