**Report Date:** November 17, 2025

## Executive Summary

Mexico's inflation continued its deceleration trajectory in October 2025, with headline INPC falling to 3.57% annually from 3.76% in September, now comfortably within Banxico's 3% ± 1% target range. The moderation was driven primarily by a 0.90% monthly decline in agricultural products (eggs, chicken, vegetables), which offset a 1.89% surge in energy prices due to the end of summer electricity subsidies in 18 cities. Core inflation remained sticky at 4.28% annually, with services inflation (0.39% monthly) showing persistent pressures from housing, education, and transportation. Looking ahead, analyst expectations for 2025 year-end inflation range from 3.5% to 4.4%, with consensus around 3.8-3.9%, suggesting Banxico has room to continue gradual monetary easing while monitoring service sector stickiness and potential energy price volatility.

## Key Inflation Drivers (Last 30 days)

### Accelerating Pressures

**1. Electricity Tariffs (+17.65% monthly)**
- **Driver:** Conclusion of summer electricity subsidy program in 18 cities where INPC is measured
- **Context:** Seasonal subsidy removal is typical annually; creates temporary spike in October
- **Fiscal cost saved:** Subsidies are expensive; government reduces budget burden in cooler months
- **Outlook:** Likely to moderate in November-December absent further tariff adjustments

**2. Air Transport (+11.29% monthly)**
- **Driver:** Seasonal demand patterns; end of summer travel season typically sees price adjustments
- **Context:** Tourism services packages also rose 8.04%
- **Impact:** Limited weight in overall INPC; affects travel-heavy consumers

**3. Services Inflation (0.39% monthly, sticky at elevated levels)**
- **Components:** Housing (rent), education, healthcare, restaurant/hotel services
- **Driver:** Labor cost pressures, sticky wage inflation, capacity constraints
- **Significance:** Core services inflation is most persistent component; difficult for Banxico to influence directly
- **Outlook:** Likely to remain elevated through 2026 given minimum wage increases and tight labor markets

### Moderating Pressures

**1. Agricultural Products (-0.90% monthly, +0.1% annually)**
- **Eggs:** -2.23% monthly (had spiked earlier in 2025 due to avian flu concerns)
- **Chicken:** -1.32% monthly
- **Potatoes and tubers:** -7.29% monthly
- **Green tomatoes:** Decline reported
- **Driver:** Improved harvests, resolution of avian flu supply disruptions, seasonal abundance
- **Outlook:** Volatile component; vulnerable to weather shocks and disease outbreaks

**2. Fruits & Vegetables (-10.27% annually)**
- **Driver:** Seasonal production cycles, improved supply chains
- **Context:** This is the most volatile INPC subcomponent
- **Outlook:** Will fluctuate with seasonal patterns; December-January could see increases

**3. Merchandise Inflation (4.12% annually, moderated from recent peaks)**
- **Significance:** Interrupted 10-month streak of acceleration
- **Driver:** Peso strength (MXN has been relatively stable in 18-20 range), moderating global commodity prices
- **Outlook:** Vulnerable to peso depreciation or renewed global inflation

## Policy Context

### Government Measures

**Energy Subsidies:**
- **Electricity:** Summer subsidy program (typically June-September) reduces tariffs in hot regions; removal in October creates predictable seasonal spike
- **Fiscal cost:** Subsidies cost billions of pesos annually; government balances affordability with fiscal discipline
- **Gasoline:** Implicit price cap at MXN 24/liter for Magna (regular) agreed by Sheinbaum administration and business sector
  - Current prices (Nov 17): National avg MXN 23.37 (Magna), MXN 25.83 (Premium)
  - CDMX: MXN 23.46 (Magna), MXN 26.04 (Premium)
  - Policy limits pass-through of global oil price increases; fiscally costly if Brent rises

**Price Controls:**
- Gasoline price cap (MXN 24 Magna) is key inflation management tool
- Tortilla prices historically subject to informal monitoring
- Electricity tariffs set administratively by government

**Minimum Wage Impact:**
- 2025 minimum wage increases (specific amount not in search results but historically 10-20% annual increases)
- Timing: Typically takes effect January 1
- Impact: Puts upward pressure on services inflation with 6-12 month lag
- Outlook: 2026 increase will be announced in December 2025; likely to sustain services inflation in 4-5% range

### External Factors

**Exchange Rate (USD/MXN):**
- **Current level:** ~18-20 range in Q4 2025 (relatively stable)
- **Impact on inflation:**
  - Peso strength (MXN strengthening toward 18) reduces imported inflation (merchandise, energy)
  - Peso weakness (MXN weakening toward 21-22) increases imported inflation
- **Recent trend:** Peso has been volatile in 2025 due to U.S. policy uncertainty, Banxico rate cut cycle, and fiscal concerns
- **Outlook:** Vulnerability to U.S. election cycle (2026), Fed policy, and Mexico fiscal/PEMEX risks

**Global Commodity Prices:**
- **Oil (Brent):** $70-85/barrel range in Oct-Nov 2025
  - Lower prices reduce energy inflation and gasoline subsidy costs
  - PEMEX revenues suffer, increasing fiscal pressure
- **Food commodities:** Generally stable in H2 2025
  - Wheat, corn, soy prices moderate from 2024 peaks
  - Benefits Mexico's imported food inflation
- **Metals/industrial:** Less direct impact on consumer inflation

**U.S. Inflation Spillover:**
- **Mechanism:** 54% of Mexico's electricity from U.S. gas; extensive trade integration (USMCA)
- **U.S. inflation (2025):** Moderating toward 2-3% range (specific recent data not in search results)
- **Impact:** Lower U.S. inflation reduces imported price pressures on Mexico
- **Outlook:** Mexico's inflation increasingly decoupled from U.S. in 2025; domestic factors (services, energy policy) more important

**Trend Analysis:**

- **Core inflation:** Remains stubbornly elevated at 4.28%, well above 3% target
- **Divergence:** Core (4.28%) vs Non-Core (1.18%) shows 3.1 percentage point gap
  - Core stickiness driven by services
  - Non-core subdued by agricultural deflation and moderate energy prices (ex-October electricity spike)

### Persistent vs. Transitory Components

**Persistent (Structural) Inflation:**
- **Services (Core):** 0.39% monthly, elevated annual rate
  - Housing (rent): Sticky due to housing shortages in major cities
  - Education: Annual tuition increases typically in August-September
  - Healthcare: Capacity constraints and wage pressures
  - Restaurants/hotels: Labor-intensive, wage-sensitive
- **Outlook:** Will remain elevated (4-5% range) through 2026 unless demand significantly weakens

**Transitory (Volatile) Inflation:**
- **Agricultural products:** -0.90% monthly swings common
  - Weather-dependent (droughts, floods)
  - Disease outbreaks (avian flu for eggs/chicken)
  - Seasonal patterns
- **Energy:** 1.89% monthly in October due to policy (subsidy removal)
  - Electricity spikes in April (summer tariff starts) and October (subsidy ends)
  - Gasoline: Capped administratively, can spike if cap is lifted
- **Outlook:** Will continue to create monthly volatility but limited sustained impact

### Seasonal Patterns and Base Effects

**Seasonal Patterns:**
- **October:** Electricity spike (end of summer subsidies) is recurring annual pattern
- **January:** Minimum wage increase takes effect; often followed by price adjustments in services
- **August-September:** Education costs spike (school year begins)
- **December-January:** Holiday demand can elevate food prices (traditional foods)

**Base Effects:**
- October 2025 (3.57%) benefits from comparison to October 2024 (4.76%), a high base
- Going forward (Nov-Dec 2025), base effects will be less favorable if 2024 comparisons were already moderate

## Banxico Assessment

### Current Inflation vs. Target Range

- **Headline INPC (3.57%):** Within target range of 3% ± 1% (i.e., 2-4%)
- **Core inflation (4.28%):** Above the 3% target, indicating underlying price pressures
- **Banxico target:** 3% with tolerance band of ± 1 percentage point

**Significance:**
- Headline inflation within target allows Banxico flexibility to cut rates
- Core inflation above target suggests caution needed; premature easing could re-accelerate inflation
- Banxico typically focuses on core inflation and inflation expectations for policy decisions

### Banxico Commentary on Component Drivers

(Based on general Banxico communication patterns; specific November 2025 minutes not in search results)

- **Services inflation:** Likely acknowledged as key concern; sticky and difficult to moderate without demand cooling
- **Energy volatility:** Typically "looked through" as transitory; seasonal electricity spikes not seen as requiring policy response
- **Agricultural deflation:** Welcome but not relied upon for sustained disinflation (too volatile)
- **Exchange rate:** Peso stability (18-20 MXN/USD) has been helpful anchor; depreciation would be concerning
- **Forward guidance:** Likely emphasizes gradual approach to rate cuts given core inflation stickiness

### Inflation Expectations from Banxico Survey

**Private Sector Analyst Survey (October/November 2025):**
- **2025 year-end inflation:** 3.80% (median), down from 3.85% previous month
  - Range: 3.5% (optimistic) to 4.4% (pessimistic)
  - Reflects 4th consecutive month of downward revisions
- **Alternative forecasts:**
  - IMEF (November): 3.8% for 2025
  - Citibanamex: 3.90% for 2025 year-end
  - Monex: 4.05% for 2025 year-end

**2026 Outlook (limited data in search results):**
- Citi survey: Inflation expected to "cling" to upper bound of Banxico's target range (near 4%) through 2026
- Implies core inflation stickiness will persist

**Longer-term expectations:**
- Banxico closely monitors 12-month and 24-month ahead expectations
- Well-anchored expectations (near 3%) are critical for credibility
- Any drift above 4% in longer-term expectations would be concerning

### Implications for Monetary Policy Stance

**Current Policy Rate:**
- Specific November 2025 rate not in search results, but Banxico has been in easing cycle
- Market expectations: Continued gradual cuts given inflation within target range

**Forward Outlook:**
- **Case for continued easing:**
  - Headline inflation at 3.57%, comfortably in target
  - Economic growth sluggish (Banxico cut 2025 growth forecast to 0.6%)
  - Real rates likely still restrictive
- **Case for caution:**
  - Core inflation at 4.28%, well above target
  - Services inflation sticky and labor-market driven
  - Peso vulnerability to external shocks could re-ignite inflation
  - Energy price risks (gasoline cap unsustainable if oil prices surge)

**Likely Path:**
- 25 basis point cuts at quarterly or every-other-meeting pace
- Data-dependent approach: Pausing if inflation re-accelerates or peso weakens significantly
- Terminal rate likely above pre-pandemic neutral (historically ~5-6%) given elevated core inflation

## Forward Outlook (Next 3-6 Months)

### Expected Trends

**Headline Inflation (Nov 2025 - Mar 2026):**
- **November-December 2025:** Likely to moderate further toward 3.4-3.6% range
  - Favorable base effects (comparing to higher late-2024 levels)
  - No major energy policy changes expected
  - Seasonal food price patterns (some increase in holiday demand)
- **January 2026:** Risk of uptick due to:
  - Minimum wage increase (effective Jan 1)
  - Post-holiday price adjustments
  - Potential gasoline price adjustments if cap is relaxed
- **Q1 2026:** Likely to settle in 3.5-3.9% range, consistent with analyst forecasts

**Core Inflation:**
- **Services:** Expected to remain sticky at 4-5% annual rate
  - January 2026 minimum wage increase will sustain pressure
  - Housing, education, healthcare show no signs of moderating
- **Merchandise:** Vulnerable to peso depreciation
  - Stable around 4% if MXN stays in 18-20 range
  - Could rise to 5%+ if peso weakens to 21-22

**Non-Core Inflation:**
- **Agricultural products:** Will fluctuate with weather and disease patterns
  - Winter months (Dec-Feb): Possible uptick in vegetable prices
  - Eggs: Vulnerable to avian flu outbreaks (recurring risk)
- **Energy:** Depends entirely on government policy
  - Electricity: No major subsidies in Nov-Mar; relatively stable
  - Gasoline: MXN 24 cap under pressure if Brent crude exceeds $90-95/barrel

### Key Risks

**Upside Risks (Higher Inflation):**

1. **Peso Depreciation:**
   - Trigger: U.S. policy uncertainty, Mexico fiscal concerns, PEMEX crisis, Fed policy shifts
   - Impact: MXN weakening to 22-24 would add 0.5-1.0 ppt to headline inflation
   - Probability: Moderate (30-40%) given multiple risk factors

2. **Energy Price Shock:**
   - Trigger: Geopolitical event pushing Brent to $100+/barrel
   - Impact: Gasoline cap becomes unsustainable; removal could add 1-2 ppt to inflation
   - Probability: Low (10-20%) but high-impact

3. **Agricultural Supply Shock:**
   - Trigger: Drought, avian flu outbreak, extreme weather
   - Impact: Eggs, chicken, vegetables could spike 15-30%
   - Probability: Moderate (25-35%) given climate volatility
   - Duration: Typically 2-4 months

4. **Excessive Minimum Wage Increase:**
   - Trigger: Political pressure for 25-30% increase (vs typical 10-15%)
   - Impact: Services inflation accelerates to 5-6% with 6-12 month lag
   - Probability: Moderate (20-30%) in 2026 given Sheinbaum's leftist orientation

5. **U.S. Inflation Re-acceleration:**
   - Trigger: U.S. policy changes, trade tensions
   - Impact: Imported inflation rises 0.3-0.5 ppt
   - Probability: Low-Moderate (20-25%)

**Downside Risks (Lower Inflation):**

1. **Economic Slowdown:**
   - Trigger: Mexico growth disappointing (Banxico forecast only 0.6% for 2025)
   - Impact: Demand-driven disinflation, especially in services; could bring core to 3.5-3.8%
   - Probability: Moderate (30-40%)

2. **Peso Strength:**
   - Trigger: U.S. Fed cuts, Mexico fiscal discipline, positive USMCA developments
   - Impact: MXN strengthening to 17-18 reduces imported inflation by 0.3-0.5 ppt
   - Probability: Low-Moderate (20-30%)

3. **Sustained Agricultural Deflation:**
   - Trigger: Excellent weather, bumper crops, absence of disease
   - Impact: Food deflation extends through Q1 2026
   - Probability: Moderate (25-35%)

4. **Energy Subsidy Expansion:**
   - Trigger: Political pressure, populist fiscal policy
   - Impact: Could reduce headline inflation by 0.2-0.4 ppt but fiscally costly
   - Probability: Low (15-20%) given fiscal constraints

### Upcoming Events and Impact Dates

**December 2025:**
- **Mid-month:** INEGI releases November 2025 INPC (expect 3.4-3.6% annual)
- **Late month:** Government announces 2026 minimum wage increase
  - Watch for: 10-15% is baseline; 20%+ would be concerning for 2026 inflation
- **Banxico meeting (likely):** Potential 25 bp rate cut depending on November inflation data

**January 2026:**
- **January 1:** Minimum wage increase takes effect
- **Gasoline price review:** Possible adjustment to MXN 24 cap if fiscally unsustainable
- **Mid-month:** December 2025 INPC release (expect 3.5-3.8% annual)

**February 2026:**
- **Mid-month:** January 2026 INPC release - will show first impact of wage increase
- **Banxico meeting (likely):** Policy decision influenced by wage/price dynamics

**March 2026:**
- **Electricity tariff changes:** Possible adjustments ahead of summer subsidy season
- **Q1 2026 GDP:** Economic data will inform Banxico's inflation vs. growth trade-off
