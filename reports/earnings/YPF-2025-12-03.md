## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

* **Vaca Muerta transforms operations, but conventional decline drags results:** YPF delivered spectacular 35% YoY growth in Vaca Muerta shale oil production to 170 Mbbl/d (October hit 190 Mbbl/d, exceeding Dec 2025 target early), but total hydrocarbon output fell 6.4% to 523 Mboe/d as company executes strategic exit from mature conventional fields. Revenue missed consensus by 8% at $4.64B (down 12% YoY), driven by lower Brent prices (-13% YoY) and reduced export volumes. However, EPS of $0.84 beat estimates despite 78% decline from prior year's $3.75, reflecting aggressive cost cutting: lifting costs down 28% QoQ and 45% YoY, operating expenses down 31% YoY.

* **Profitability improving sequentially despite top-line headwinds:** Adjusted EBITDA flat YoY at $1.40B but surged 21% QoQ, with margin expanding 370 bps to ~30%. Upstream EBITDA jumped 33% YoY to $1.04B on lower lifting costs and higher natural gas sales, while Downstream EBITDA fell 26% to $354M on compressed local fuel prices. Net loss of $198M (vs. profit in prior year) reflects production decline and export weakness, but sequential margin improvement demonstrates operational leverage emerging as Vaca Muerta scales and conventional portfolio shrinks.

* **Cash flow deeply negative; debt rising to fund shale acceleration:** Free cash flow negative $759M in Q3, driven by shale asset acquisitions, mature field exit costs, and $360M negative working capital from seasonal gas sales and inventory restocking. Net debt increased to $9.6B (net leverage 2.1x), though company successfully returned to international bond market with $500M issuance at 8.25% yield—signal of improved investor confidence in Argentina macro under Milei. Management expects Q4 working capital normalization to turn FCF positive, but sustained negative cash generation raises questions about funding $20-25B LNG project and aggressive 2026-27 production targets without equity dilution or government support.

* **Stock rises despite miss; market pricing Milei reform upside:** Shares rose ~4% after-hours despite revenue miss and net loss, with investors focusing on operational improvements (shale growth, cost cuts, EBITDA inflection) and regulatory tailwinds. Argentina's RIGI (investment incentive regime) provides 30-year stability, 25% income tax rate, and FX flexibility for large projects—YPF's $50B LNG venture with Petronas approved under RIGI. Milei's deregulation of oil/fuel markets, elimination of export taxes/quotas, and commitment to lift currency controls in 2025 create structural tailwinds. However, stock trades at premium to LatAm peers (8-10x EV/EBITDA vs. PBR at 3-4x) despite binary political risk if Milei reforms stall or Argentina macro deteriorates.

## GUIDANCE & OUTLOOK

**Full Year 2025 (Implied from Q3 Commentary):**
* **Vaca Muerta Shale Oil Production:** 165 Mbbl/d average for FY2025 (on track)
  * Exit rate (December 2025): ~190 Mbbl/d (target slightly exceeded, October already at 190 Mbbl/d)
* **Total Production:** No specific guidance, but Q3 run rate of 523 Mboe/d implies ~191 MMboe for full year
* **Lifting Costs:** Expect continued decline driven by Vaca Muerta mix shift and efficiency gains
* **Free Cash Flow:** Q4 expected to turn positive as working capital normalizes (reversal of $360M seasonal drain)

**2026-2027 Outlook (Preliminary Guidance):**
* **Vaca Muerta Shale Oil Production:**
  * 2026: ~215 Mbbl/d (up 27% from 2025 exit rate)
  * 2027: ~290 Mbbl/d (up 35% from 2026, representing 70% total growth from Nov 2023 baseline)
* **Capital Allocation:** Continuing shift from conventional (5% of upstream capex in last 12 months vs. 35% in 2023) to unconventional (Vaca Muerta represents 70% of Q3 investment)
* **LNG Project Timeline:**
  * 2030: Phase 1 operational with two floating liquefaction terminals exporting 40 MMcm/d
  * 2031: Phase 2 onshore terminal adds 80 MMcm/d capacity
  * Total capex estimate: $20-25B (funded via RIGI benefits, strategic partners, and project finance)

**No Formal Guidance Provided On:**
* FY2025 or FY2026 revenue, EBITDA, or EPS targets
* Downstream refining throughput or fuel margin outlook
* Natural gas production trajectory (Q3 down 4.8% YoY)
* Capex levels for 2026-27 (beyond LNG project commentary)

**Macro & Strategic Commentary:**

* **Milei Reforms as Structural Catalyst:** CEO Horacio Marín stated "the big reform for our industry was done," highlighting RIGI framework providing 30-year stability, 25% corporate tax (vs. standard 35%), reduced dividend tax (7% declining to 3.5% after 7 years), and FX flexibility. Deregulation of domestic oil/fuel pricing (previously controlled by Kirchner governments) allows YPF to pass through international Brent movements, reducing margin volatility and political interference. Elimination of export quotas/taxes enables full monetization of Vaca Muerta production via LNG exports.

* **Vaca Muerta as Core Growth Engine:** Argentina's Vaca Muerta shale formation ranks among top 5 globally in recoverable resources (16 billion barrels oil, 308 TCF gas per EIA). YPF controls ~12,000 hectares in core Loma Campana and other blocks, representing ~25-30% of basin potential. Q3 shale oil growth of 35% YoY (to 170 Mbbl/d) demonstrates execution capability, with October spike to 190 Mbbl/d validating 2026-27 targets. Break-even costs estimated at $35-40/bbl WTI (vs. current ~$70), providing robust margins even in downturn scenarios. Key risks: infrastructure bottlenecks (pipeline/export capacity), sand/water logistics, and ability to sustain 30-40% annual growth rates at scale.

* **Conventional Portfolio Exit Strategy:** YPF divesting mature fields with declining production and high lifting costs. In 2023, conventional assets represented 35% of upstream capex; in last 12 months only 5%. While this creates near-term production headwinds (total output down 6.4% YoY), it accelerates capital redeployment to higher-return Vaca Muerta and improves unit economics (lifting costs down 45% YoY). Strategic risk: timing mismatch if Vaca Muerta ramp slower than conventional decline, creating production gap and revenue shortfall.

* **Downstream Margin Compression Ongoing:** Refinery utilization hit record 97% (processing 326 Mbbl/d), but downstream EBITDA fell 26% YoY to $354M on compressed local fuel prices. Argentina domestic fuel demand weak amid economic recession (GDP contracted in 2024, inflation still >200% annually despite Milei stabilization efforts). La Plata refinery won "Refinery of Year" for LatAm and ranked first quartile in Solomon benchmarking, demonstrating operational excellence, but margin outlook uncertain until domestic demand recovers. Potential upside: Milei's elimination of fuel subsidies could allow pricing normalization and margin expansion if competitive dynamics support.

* **LNG Mega-Project as Long-Term Value Driver:** $50B LNG venture with Petronas (pending final FID) targets 120 MMcm/d export capacity by 2031, positioning Argentina as top 10 global LNG exporter. Project economics supported by low-cost Vaca Muerta gas (~$2-3/MMBtu production cost) and proximity to Atlantic markets (Brazil, Europe). RIGI framework critical: provides FX stability (can hold revenues offshore), tax benefits (25% corporate rate vs. 35% standard), and political risk mitigation (30-year legal stability). Key risks: execution on $20-25B capex, securing offtake agreements pre-FID, and avoiding cost overruns (common in mega-projects). If successful, could transform YPF from regional player to global LNG exporter by 2030s.

* **Argentina Macro as Binary Risk/Opportunity:** Milei's shock therapy (budget surplus achieved in 2025 after decades of deficits, inflation down from 26% monthly to 2.2%, FX reserves rebuilding) creates bullish backdrop. However, political sustainability uncertain: Milei's coalition controls <50% of Congress, requires negotiation with Peronist opposition. Risks include: (1) Policy reversal if Milei loses 2027 election; (2) Social unrest if austerity deepens recession; (3) Renewed currency crisis if reserves drain or capital controls persist beyond 2025. Bulls argue RIGI's 30-year legal protections insulate YPF from policy shifts; bears counter that Argentina's history of contract renegotiation (2001 default, 2012 YPF nationalization) undermines legal certainty.

## SEGMENT PERFORMANCE

**Upstream (Oil & Gas Production) - 42% of Revenue, 74% of EBITDA:**
* **Revenue:** $1.97B (vs. consensus $1.92B, beat by 3%)
* **Adjusted EBITDA:** $1.04B (+33% YoY)
* **EBITDA Margin:** ~53% (up from ~40% prior year)

**Production Breakdown:**
* **Total Hydrocarbons:** 523 Mboe/d (down 6.4% YoY from 559 Mboe/d)
  * **Crude Oil:** 239.8 Mbbl/d (down 6.3% from 255.8 Mbbl/d)
    * **Vaca Muerta Shale Oil:** 170 Mbbl/d (up 35% YoY)—represents 71% of total oil production
    * **Conventional Oil:** ~70 Mbbl/d (down ~40% YoY, estimated)—strategic exit in progress
  * **Natural Gas:** 38.4 MMcm/d (down 4.8% YoY from 40.3 MMcm/d)
    * Vaca Muerta shale gas contribution not separately disclosed but growing

**Key Drivers of Upstream Performance:**
* **Positive:** Vaca Muerta shale oil surged 35% YoY; lifting costs down 45% YoY (from $18-20/bbl estimated to $10-11/bbl) driven by shale economies of scale and conventional exit; seasonal natural gas sales boost (winter demand in Argentina peaks Q3)
* **Negative:** Total production down 6.4% as conventional field decline outpaced shale growth; natural gas output fell 4.8% on mature field depletion; export volumes constrained by pipeline/terminal capacity

**Outlook:** Management targeting 215 Mbbl/d shale oil in 2026 (+27% from 2025 exit rate) and 290 Mbbl/d in 2027 (+35%). Conventional output will continue declining, but shale acceleration should offset by mid-2026, returning total production to growth. Upstream EBITDA margin expected to sustain 50-55% as low-cost shale displaces high-cost conventional.

Utilization likely to remain 90-95% given integrated supply from YPF's upstream, but margin upside limited until domestic demand rebounds.

## ANALYST TAKEAWAY

Thesis bifurcated; high-risk, high-reward Argentina reform play. YPF's operational momentum is undeniable—Vaca Muerta shale oil surging 35% YoY (October hit 190 Mbbl/d vs. 170 Mbbl/d Q3 average), lifting costs down 45%, and EBITDA up 21% QoQ despite revenue headwinds. Milei's RIGI framework and energy deregulation create structural tailwinds absent since YPF's 2012 nationalization, with $50B LNG project offering pathway to transform Argentina into top 10 global exporter by 2031. However, Q3 revealed three critical vulnerabilities: (1) Negative $759M FCF and rising debt ($9.6B net debt, up $1.2B YoY) despite EBITDA growth, questioning ability to self-fund $20-25B LNG capex without dilutive equity raise or government backstop; (2) Revenue miss of 8% and total production decline of 6.4% as conventional field exit outpaces Vaca Muerta ramp, creating near-term top-line pressure; (3) Binary political risk—YPF trading at 8-10x EV/EBITDA (premium to PBR at 3-4x) assumes Milei reforms succeed and RIGI protections hold, but Argentina's history of policy reversals (2001 default, 2012 YPF seizure) undermines legal certainty. Execution on 2026-27 production targets (215-290 Mbbl/d) critical to justify valuation.

**Valuation Impact:** Multiple expansion likely if Argentina macro stabilizes, but downside severe if Milei reforms stall. YPF trades at ~8-10x NTM EV/EBITDA ($15-18B market cap, $24-28B EV) vs. LatAm peers: Petrobras (PBR) at 3-4x, Ecopetrol (EC) at 4-5x. Premium justified by Vaca Muerta growth (30-40% annual shale production CAGR vs. peers at 0-5%) and RIGI regulatory certainty, but vulnerable to multiple compression if: (1) Q4 2025/Q1 2026 FCF remains deeply negative (forces equity dilution or debt restructuring); (2) Downstream margins deteriorate further (Argentina recession deepens); (3) Argentina political risk reprices (Milei coalition weakens, 2027 election uncertainty).

Fair value estimate: $25-30/share (7-8x 2026E EV/EBITDA, assuming $1.8-2.0B EBITDA and net debt of $10-11B). Downside scenario: $12-15/share (4-5x on Argentina crisis, RIGI protections challenged). Upside scenario: $40-50/share (10-12x on LNG FID, production acceleration to 250 Mbbl/d by 2027, Milei reforms sustained). Current price ~$26-28 implies balanced risk-reward for risk-tolerant investors with Argentina conviction.

**Positioning Recommendation:** Neutral with bullish bias for long-term holders; tactical entry $20-24. Operational execution impressive (shale growth, cost cuts, EBITDA inflection), and Milei reforms are most credible Argentina stabilization attempt in decades. However, negative FCF, rising leverage, and binary political risk argue for waiting for: (1) Q4 2025 earnings (Feb 2026) demonstrating FCF positive as management guided; (2) LNG project FID with Petronas confirming funding structure and first offtake agreements; (3) Vaca Muerta Q1 2026 production sustaining 190+ Mbbl/d exit rate (validating 215 Mbbl/d 2026 target). Preferred entry: $20-24/share (5-6x EV/EBITDA), offering 25-40% upside to $28-32 fair value if execution delivers and 40-100% upside to $35-50 bull case if LNG project succeeds. Trim above $35 (10x+, pricing in perfect execution with minimal political risk discount).

**For growth/emerging market investors:** YPF offers asymmetric upside if Milei's Argentina turnaround succeeds—comparable to Brazil's Lula-era reforms (2003-2010) that drove PBR from $8 to $70 (+775%). Risks include policy reversal, social instability, or currency crisis, but RIGI's 30-year protections and YPF's operational improvements (cost structure, shale scale) provide downside buffer absent in prior cycles.

**For value investors:** Avoid until FCF positive and political clarity improves. Negative cash generation and 2.1x leverage with $20-25B LNG capex looming creates balance sheet risk. Argentina's history of nationalizations and contract breaches justifies deep discount to NAV (~$50-60/share assuming $10/bbl NPV on Vaca Muerta reserves).

**Key Catalysts:**
* **Positive:**
  * Q4 2025 earnings (Feb 2026) showing FCF positive (working capital normalization)
  * LNG project FID with Petronas (expected H1 2026)
  * Vaca Muerta production sustaining 190+ Mbbl/d in Q1 2026 (validating 215 Mbbl/d 2026 target)
  * Milei's lift of currency controls in 2025 (improves FX liquidity and investor confidence)
  * Downstream margin recovery (fuel deregulation, domestic demand rebound)
  * Debt refinancing at <7% yield (improving vs. 8.25% Q3 issuance)

* **Negative:**
  * Q4 2025 FCF miss (remains negative despite management guidance)
  * LNG project delayed or restructured (partner exits, financing gaps)
  * Vaca Muerta production disappoints (sub-180 Mbbl/d in Q1 2026 signals 2026 target at risk)
  * Argentina political shock (Milei coalition fractures, 2027 election risk emerges early)
  * Brent oil collapse below $60/bbl (upstream margins compress, shale break-evens tested)
  * Currency crisis or RIGI legal challenges (undermines FX stability and investor protections)

**Sector Positioning:** Within LatAm energy, prefer Petrobras (PBR) at 3-4x EV/EBITDA for lower risk/return profile—PBR offers 8-10% dividend yield, investment-grade credit, and Brazil's institutional stability. YPF offers higher growth (shale 30-40% CAGR vs. PBR's 0-5%) but substantially higher risk (Argentina political/macro uncertainty, negative FCF, execution on LNG mega-project). For pure-play Argentina exposure, YPF is the only liquid option (Ecopetrol is Colombia, Petronas is Malaysia). Tactical overweight YPF vs. PBR for investors with Argentina conviction and risk tolerance; otherwise neutral or underweight.
