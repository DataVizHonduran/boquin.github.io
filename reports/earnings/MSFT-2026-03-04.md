## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **Beat driven by Cloud and AI monetization.** Revenue exceeded consensus by ~$1.0B and non-GAAP EPS beat by $0.17, with all three segments beating StreetAccount estimates. The Microsoft Cloud crossing $50B in a single quarter ($51.5B, +26% YoY) was a landmark milestone.
- **Azure growth deceleration is the core bear thesis.** Azure grew 39% in Q2 vs. 40% in Q1 FY26, triggering the "deceleration" narrative. Though still elevated by any absolute standard, marginal slowdown in the flagship KPI drove a ~7% after-hours stock selloff.
- **CapEx is the other catalyst for the decline.** Quarterly CapEx surged to $37.5B — well above Wall Street's $34.3B estimate — reflecting aggressive AI infrastructure build-out. The OpenAI $250B cloud commitment inflated commercial RPO by 110% to $625B, but also raised concerns about capex intensity sustainability.
- **Q3 margin guidance below expectations.** Operating margin guided at ~45.1% for Q3, below the 45.5% consensus, with operating margins expected to be down slightly YoY in Q3 before recovering for a modest full-year FY2026 improvement.

## GUIDANCE & OUTLOOK

| Period | Revenue Guide | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 FY2026 | $80.65B–$81.75B | +15–17% YoY; in-line with consensus |
| Q3 Operating Margin | ~45.1% | Below 45.5% consensus; down YoY |
| FY2026 Full Year | Operating margins up slightly YoY | H1 outperformance expected to offset Q3 dip |

**CFO Amy Hood commentary:**
- Acknowledged continued pressure on margins from AI infrastructure depreciation and build-out costs, but noted efficiency improvements as assets mature: *"Where you've sold the entirety of an asset's life, the margins actually improve with time."*
- Supply constraints on AI infrastructure expected to persist through at least June 2026 (consistent with Q1 messaging).
- Flagged higher mix of Windows OEM and on-prem commercial revenues as a favorable margin tailwind in H1.
- No specific macroeconomic headwinds cited; demand environment characterized as robust across cloud and AI workloads.

## SEGMENT PERFORMANCE

### Intelligent Cloud — $32.9B (+29% YoY) ✅ Beat ($32.4B est.)
- **Azure & Other Cloud Services: +39% YoY (+38% CC)** — sequential deceleration from +40% in Q1; this was the primary stock-negative catalyst
- Server products and cloud services revenue +21% YoY
- Supply constraints remain a binding factor on Azure capacity ramp

### Productivity & Business Processes — $34.1B (+16% YoY) ✅ Beat ($33.5B est.)
- **Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud: +17% YoY (+14% CC)** — Copilot now at 15 million commercial seats
- Microsoft 365 Consumer cloud: +29% YoY (+27% CC)
- LinkedIn: +11% YoY (+10% CC)
- Dynamics 365: +19% YoY (+17% CC)
- Office Commercial products and cloud services: +13% YoY

### More Personal Computing — $14.3B (-3% YoY) ⚠️
- Windows OEM and Devices: +1% YoY (roughly flat in CC)
- Xbox content and services: -5% YoY (-6% CC); gaming softness persists
- Search and news advertising: modest growth; Bing Copilot integration ongoing

## ANALYST TAKEAWAY

Thesis largely intact — Azure deceleration is modest and AI monetization (Copilot seats, commercial RPO) is accelerating — but the $37.5B capex print and below-consensus Q3 margin guide are legitimate near-term multiple compression catalysts; the re-rating will depend on whether Azure reaccelerates in Q3 FY2026 (April 2026 print).
