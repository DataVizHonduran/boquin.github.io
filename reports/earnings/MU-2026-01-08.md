- **Thesis-Changing Beat:** Micron delivered a blowout quarter, crushing estimates on both revenue (+6.3% vs. consensus) and EPS (+21% vs. consensus), driven by explosive AI demand for HBM3E chips and tight memory supply across DRAM/NAND. The company has **sold out its entire 2026 HBM capacity**, with pricing and volume locked for calendar year 2026—a remarkable visibility indicator rarely seen in the cyclical memory sector.

- **Primary Beat Driver:** Cloud Memory Business Unit (CMBU) revenue doubled (+100% YoY) to $5.3B, fueled by HBM shipments to NVIDIA, AMD, and hyperscalers building AI infrastructure. CMBU alone accounted for 39% of total revenue. Data center DRAM bit shipments surged due to GenAI-driven server upgrades requiring high-capacity DIMMs (256GB+) and HBM3E for GPU clusters.

- **After-Hours Reaction:** Stock rose +15% immediately post-earnings, closing at $104.25 on December 18, 2025. YTD 2026 stock performance: +12%.

- **Structural Shift, Not Cyclical Peak:** Management emphasized this is not a traditional memory upcycle but a **secular AI-driven demand shift**. HBM TAM now projected to hit $100B by 2028 (vs. prior $50B estimate)—equivalent to the entire DRAM market in 2024. Micron increased FY2026 capex from $18B to $20B to address supply-demand tightness extending **beyond 2026**.

## SEGMENT PERFORMANCE

### Cloud Memory Business Unit (CMBU) — **Star Performer**
- **Revenue:** $5.3B, +100% YoY, +34% QoQ
- **Key Products:** HBM3E (24GB and 36GB stacks), 128GB/256GB DDR5 RDIMMs, CXL memory modules
- **Performance Drivers:**
  - HBM revenue tripled YoY; Micron now holds ~20% HBM market share (vs. SK Hynix ~50%, Samsung ~30%)
  - **All calendar 2026 HBM capacity sold out**—pricing and volume locked with NVIDIA, AMD, hyperscalers
  - DDR5 RDIMM ASPs up 40% YoY due to mix shift to 256GB modules for AI servers
- **Outlook:** HBM TAM growing at 40% CAGR through 2028, reaching $100B (vs. $35B in 2025)—Micron expects to maintain 20%+ share, implying $20B+ HBM revenue by 2028

### Core Data Center & Enterprise — **Solid, but Overshadowed**
- **Revenue:** $2.4B, +4% YoY, flat QoQ
- **Performance:** Traditional enterprise DRAM (non-AI servers, storage, networking) growing modestly as IT budgets remain constrained. NAND data center revenue exceeded $1B for first time, driven by SSD demand for AI training datasets (hyperscalers need PB-scale storage for model checkpointing).
- **Outlook:** Low-to-mid single-digit growth expected in FY2026; AI servers cannibalizing traditional server budgets

### Mobile and Client (Smartphones, PCs, Tablets)
- **Revenue:** $4.3B, +63% YoY, +18% QoQ
- **Key Drivers:**
  - Flagship smartphone DRAM content rising (12GB mix hit 59% in CY Q3 2025 vs. 28% a year ago)
  - LPDDR5X ASPs up 30% YoY due to AI-enabled smartphone features (on-device LLMs)
  - PC market stabilizing (+2% unit growth in CY 2025) with Windows refresh cycle and AI PC adoption (Copilot+ devices)
- **New Products:** Began sampling 1-gamma 16Gb LPDDR6 in Q1—50% faster than LPDDR5X, targeting flagship phones in H2 2026
- **Outlook:** Mid-teens revenue growth in FY2026; smartphone units growing low-single-digits but DRAM content per device up 30%+

### Embedded (Automotive, Industrial, IoT)
- **Revenue:** $1.7B, +49% YoY, +15% QoQ
- **Key Drivers:**
  - Automotive DRAM content per vehicle rising (electric vehicles averaging 8GB vs. 2GB for ICE vehicles)
  - LPDDR5X and UFS 4.1 NAND seeing strong adoption in ADAS systems and robotics
  - Industrial automation and edge AI applications driving demand for automotive-grade memory
- **Design Wins:** Secured "billions of dollars" in automotive design wins for LPDDR5X and UFS products
- **Outlook:** High-teens to 20% growth in FY2026; long-term TAM expanding as EVs scale and humanoid robotics emerge

### Business Unit Summary
**Segment Rankings by Growth:**
1. Cloud Memory (+100% YoY) — **AI supercycle**

3. Embedded (+49% YoY) — **EV and industrial IoT**

**Thesis Intact; Multiple Expansion Justified Despite 60% YTD Rally.** Micron is no longer a commodity memory supplier—it has repositioned as a critical AI infrastructure enabler, with HBM and high-capacity DRAM now representing 40%+ of revenue. The company's ability to sell out 2026 HBM supply 12 months in advance provides **unprecedented visibility** in a historically volatile sector. At 15x NTM P/E (vs. 8x historical average), the stock appears fully valued on traditional metrics, but **AI-driven margin expansion (60%+ gross margins in H2 FY2026 vs. 40% historical) supports 18-20x P/E**, implying $130-$140 fair value (+15-20% upside from $115 current). Key risks: (1) AI capex slowdown if hyperscalers pause spending post-2026, (2) HBM market share loss to Samsung's HBM3E 12H (Micron trails on density), (3) China export control escalation impacting 15% of revenue.
