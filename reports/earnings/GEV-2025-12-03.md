## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

* **Revenue momentum accelerating, but profitability lags:** GE Vernova beat revenue expectations by 9% driven by robust Power (+15% organic) and Electrification (+32% organic) growth, offsetting continued Wind headwinds (-8%). However, EPS of $1.64 missed consensus $1.86 by 12%, reflecting margin pressure from mix (higher equipment sales at lower margins than services), elevated R&D spending, and Wind segment losses ($61M) despite substantial improvement from prior year ($317M loss).

* **Order book inflection underscores multi-year growth runway:** Q3 orders of $14.6B (up 55% organically) drove backlog to $135B—representing 3.4x trailing revenue, providing exceptional visibility into 2026-28 revenue. Power segment captured $7.8B orders (+50% organic), driven by AI data center demand for gas turbines and grid reliability investments. Electrification equipment orders more than doubled, reaching $26B cumulative backlog, positioning segment for sustained 20-25% growth through 2026-27.

* **2025 guidance reaffirmed despite EPS miss:** Management maintained full-year outlook of $36-37B revenue (trending to high-end at $37B), 8-9% EBITDA margin, and $3.0-3.5B free cash flow. This confidence reflects: (1) backlog conversion accelerating in Q4; (2) margin trajectory improving as Wind losses moderate and Electrification scales; (3) working capital tailwinds from advance payments on large orders. However, guidance implies Q4 EPS must be $2.35-2.50 to hit full-year Street estimates of ~$7.50—a 40-50% sequential increase requiring flawless execution.

* **Stock reaction negative despite operational strength:** Shares declined ~4% in after-hours trading, with investors focusing on EPS miss and demanding proof of margin improvement after two consecutive quarters of misses (Q2 also underwhelmed on profitability). Market appears skeptical that Wind turnaround and Power margin expansion can offset Electrification ramp costs and elevated corporate expenses. Valuation at 25-28x NTM EPS leaves little room for disappointment.

## SEGMENT PERFORMANCE

**Power ($4.8B, +15% YoY Organic):** Outperformance driven by gas turbine equipment sales (up 20%+ as utilities and industrial customers ordered capacity for AI data centers, replacing coal retirements, and enhancing grid reliability) and services revenue (up 10-12% from installed base growth and higher utilization rates). Orders of $7.8B (+50% organic) reflect HA gas turbine demand and multi-year service agreements. EBITDA margin expanded to 13.3% from 11.9%, with further improvement to 14-15% expected as mix shifts toward higher-margin services and new equipment pricing improves. Backlog now 62 GW (gas turbine capacity), up from 55 GW in Q2, representing 2.5-3 years of equipment revenue visibility.

**Electrification ($2.6B est., +32% YoY Organic):** Strongest growth segment, with equipment orders more than doubling driven by transmission/distribution grid investments (utilities upgrading infrastructure to handle renewable integration and electrification loads), data center electrical systems, and industrial electrification. Revenue growth of 32% reflects backlog conversion accelerating. Equipment backlog reached $26B (up $6.5B YTD), providing 2.5-3x revenue coverage for next 2-3 years. Margin expanding toward 14-15% target (from low-teens currently) as scale benefits materialize and Prolec GE acquisition integrates (expected to be accretive immediately given strong margins on transformers). Challenge: Supply chain constraints on transformers and switchgear creating 12-18 month lead times, potentially limiting growth if capacity doesn't expand (hence Prolec acquisition strategic).

**Wind ($2.6B, -8% YoY Organic):** Continued headwinds but tangible improvement trajectory. Revenue decline reflects: (1) Onshore wind market oversupply driving pricing pressure; (2) Haliade-X offshore wind ramp slower than expected due to project delays; (3) Selective bidding discipline (turning away low-margin projects). Orders of $1.8B (+4% organic) stabilizing, suggesting demand floor. EBITDA losses dramatically improved to $61M from $317M (down 81%), driven by: cost restructuring ($200M annualized savings captured YTD), warranty reserve releases as legacy projects roll off, and profitable project mix. Management guided to $400M full-year loss (implying Q4 ~$100M loss), with breakeven targeted for 2026. Offshore wind remains strategic priority given higher margins (15-20% at scale vs. 5-10% onshore), but requires operational excellence execution.

* Americas: ~$4.5B (+18% YoY) - U.S. AI data center power demand, grid modernization
* Europe: ~$3.0B (+8% YoY) - Energy transition investments, wind installations
* Asia: ~$1.8B (+10% YoY) - China/India power infrastructure

## ANALYST TAKEAWAY

Thesis intact but execution risk elevated near-term. GE Vernova's positioning in secular growth themes (AI power, grid modernization, energy transition) remains compelling, with $135B backlog providing unprecedented revenue visibility through 2027-28. However, profitability delivery has disappointed two consecutive quarters (Q2 and Q3 EPS misses), raising questions about margin trajectory and cost control. Wind segment, while improving dramatically, remains loss-making and subject to competitive/project execution risks. Electrification growth is real but requires continued capacity expansion (capex, M&A) and supply chain execution to capture demand.

**Valuation Impact:** Multiple compression likely despite operational beat. Stock trades at ~25-28x NTM EPS vs. industrial peers at 18-22x, reflecting growth premium, but EPS misses erode confidence in near-term earnings power. Fair value estimate of $400-420 (24-25x 2026E EPS of ~$17) implies ~5-10% upside from current levels ($380-400 range), insufficient risk-reward given execution uncertainty. Peak bull case of $500-550 (management's intrinsic value view, 28-30x 2027E EPS of $18-20) requires flawless execution: Wind breakeven achieved, Power margins sustaining 14-15%, Electrification scaling without margin dilution.

**Positioning Recommendation:** Downgrade to Neutral from Overweight. Revenue/order momentum and backlog visibility are unquestionable positives, but profitability execution risks and valuation premium (25-28x) argue for awaiting Q4 results before adding. Long-term structural story (multi-decade AI/grid super-cycle) remains investable, but near-term risk-reward skewed to downside given: (1) Q4 EPS must hit $2.35-2.50 (50% above Q3) to meet full-year—challenging bar; (2) Wind breakeven not yet proven; (3) Electrification ramp costs may pressure margins through mid-2026. Preferred entry $320-350 range (20-22x 2026E EPS), offering 20-25% upside to $420 fair value and 50%+ to bull case. Current holders trim on rallies above $420, reload on weakness below $340.

**Key Catalysts:**
* **Positive:** Q4 earnings (Jan 28, 2026) demonstrating $2.40+ EPS and margin inflection; Wind segment achieving breakeven in Q1/Q2 2026; Prolec GE integration accretive to margins; additional large AI data center power orders ($5-10B magnitude); share buyback acceleration at <$350 supporting stock
* **Negative:** Q4 EPS miss (<$2.20, implying full-year $7.20-7.30); Wind losses persist beyond 2026; Electrification supply chain constraints capping growth; major warranty/project charge ($200M+ magnitude); valuation de-rating to 20-22x on execution fears

**Sector Positioning:** Within power/industrial equipment sector, prefer Eaton (ETN) at 30x for pure Electrification exposure with better margin profile (18-20% EBITDA), or General Electric (GE) aerospace at 28x for higher-quality earnings (defense/aviation vs. commodity power). GE Vernova offers higher growth (12-15% revenue CAGR 2025-28E) but lower quality (Wind drag, margin volatility). Tactical underweight GEV, overweight ETN/GE for risk-adjusted returns.
