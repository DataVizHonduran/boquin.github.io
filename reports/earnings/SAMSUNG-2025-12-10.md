# Samsung Electronics Q4 2024 Review: Record Memory Sales Offset by HBM Delays; Profit Misses Despite 30% YoY Growth

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

* **Record memory marred by HBM stumble**: Samsung posted record Q4 memory revenue driven by HBM3E and DDR5 server DRAM, but operating profit of KRW 6.5T missed consensus by 15.7% due to elevated R&D costs for HBM qualification and ramp-up expenses. Memory business hit all-time sales high, yet profit declined QoQ on competitive pressures and overheating issues delaying HBM shipments to key customers.

* **AI chip qualification struggle**: Samsung's 8-Hi and 12-Hi HBM3E memory facing overheating issues, leading to delays in securing qualifications from NVIDIA and other hyperscalers. While SK Hynix dominates 50%+ HBM market share, Samsung playing catch-up with inferior yields—critical weakness as AI accelerator demand explodes.

* **Mobile plateau continues**: Mobile eXperience business posted KRW 25.8T revenue (+3.2% YoY), but profit of KRW 2.1T declined QoQ as flagship Galaxy S24 momentum faded. Smartphone market saturating despite Galaxy AI features; tablet/wearable growth insufficient to offset smartphone ASP pressure.

* **2025 guidance cautious**: Samsung projects KRW 47.4T capex (down 11% YoY), prioritizing process transitions over capacity expansion. Management signaled shift toward "long-term profitability over rapid expansion"—acknowledgment that memory oversupply risks persist and HBM qualification delays force strategic reset.

## GUIDANCE & OUTLOOK

**FY 2025 Outlook (Qualitative):**
* Capex: **KRW 47.4T** ($32.6B), down 11% vs. 2024
* Memory strategy: **Reduce short-term capacity adds, focus on process transitions to advanced nodes**
* HBM timeline: **HBM3E 8-Hi/12-Hi ramping through 2025; HBM4 mass production 2H 2025**
* Mobile strategy: **Galaxy S25 launch to drive Q1; AI smartphone focus**
* Foundry: **3nm yield improvements; 2nm risk production**

**Management Commentary:**
* Memory Business: "Will continue to strengthen business competitiveness and optimize portfolio by increasing high value-added products such as HBM, DDR5, LPDDR5x, GDDR7 and server SSDs"
* HBM Strategy: "Despite delays, Samsung expects to significantly increase HBM supply in 2025 as qualification processes progress"
* Capex Philosophy: "Prioritize long-term profitability over rapid capacity expansion; minimize oversupply risks through capex approach focused on balancing customer demand with pricing"
* Foundry: "Continue to enhance competitiveness of advanced nodes and expand customers for 3nm process node"
* Mobile: "Plans to drive sales growth based on flagship models, particularly Galaxy S25 series, and lead AI smartphone market"

**Analyst Takeaway on Guidance:**
Capex cut of 11% signals Samsung acknowledging memory cycle peak and HBM competitive disadvantage. While management frames this as "disciplined" allocation, reality is Samsung unable to justify aggressive expansion when SK Hynix controls HBM leadership and TSMC dominates foundry. HBM4 timeline (2H 2025) trails SK Hynix, who ships HBM3E 12-Hi at scale today. Mobile guidance relies on Galaxy S25 refresh, but smartphone market structurally challenged—AI features insufficient to drive replacement cycle acceleration.

## SEGMENT PERFORMANCE

**Memory Business** (Device Solutions - DS Division):
* Q4 revenue: **Record high** (implied ~KRW 30T based on DS segment)
* Q4 operating profit: **KRW 2.9T** (DS segment total)
* Technology mix: HBM3E (8-Hi/12-Hi), DDR5 server DRAM, LPDDR5x mobile, NAND server SSDs
* Performance: Record quarterly sales driven by blended DRAM ASP increases (+higher HBM/DDR5 mix). However, operating profit declined QoQ due to elevated R&D expenses for HBM qualification and initial ramp costs. **Critical issue**: HBM3E overheating problems delaying NVIDIA qualification, allowing SK Hynix to capture 50%+ market share. NAND market contracted 8.5% in Q4, signaling oversupply risks.
* Full year 2024: Memory revenue surged 91% to KRW 84.5T on HBM/DDR5 demand boom

**Mobile eXperience (MX) & Networks**:
* Q4 revenue: **KRW 25.8T** (+3.2% YoY, -15.4% QoQ)
* Q4 operating profit: **KRW 2.1T**
* Performance: QoQ decline in sales/profit as Galaxy S24 launch effects faded. Full-year 2024 saw double-digit growth for S24 series featuring Galaxy AI, with strong tablet/wearable performance. However, smartphone market maturity limiting growth despite AI features. Galaxy S25 launch (Q1 2025) critical to regain momentum, but replacement cycles remain extended.

**Display (SDC)**:
* Q4 revenue: **KRW 8.1T**
* Q4 operating profit: **KRW 0.9T**
* Performance: Stable contribution from OLED panels for smartphones and tablets

**Foundry & System LSI** (part of DS Division):
* Performance: Foundry struggling with 3nm yield issues and customer wins vs. TSMC. Samsung's gate-all-around (GAA) technology at 3nm facing adoption challenges. 2nm risk production ongoing but timeline lags TSMC's 2nm ramp.

**Segment-Level Analysis:**
Portfolio bifurcation clear: Memory remains cash cow (84.5T revenue in 2024, +91% YoY) but competitive positioning weakening on HBM delays. Mobile business mature with limited growth (25.8T revenue, +3% YoY) despite AI smartphone positioning. Foundry remains distant third behind TSMC/Samsung with persistent yield/customer issues. Samsung lacks a dominant growth driver—memory threatened by SK Hynix, mobile saturating, foundry subscale. Display provides stability but not growth.

However, capex cut to KRW 47.4T (vs. TSMC's $40-42B) highlights Samsung's strategic dilemma—unable to match TSMC/SK Hynix investment intensity while defending market positions. Balance sheet strength provides cushion through memory cycles but doesn't solve HBM competitive gap.

## ANALYST TAKEAWAY

**Structural headwinds mounting despite cyclical memory recovery**. Q4 results superficially strong (revenue +12% YoY, operating profit +30% YoY), but miss vs. consensus and QoQ profit decline signal deeper issues. Samsung trapped in uncomfortable middle ground: strong enough to generate cash, but losing competitive position in two critical battlegrounds—HBM memory (SK Hynix dominance) and foundry (TSMC unassailable). Mobile business mature with limited AI-driven upside despite Galaxy S25 launch.

**Valuation: 14x forward P/E looks optically cheap** but reflects structural concerns rather than opportunity. Memory business facing (1) HBM qualification delays allowing SK Hynix to entrench 50%+ share, (2) potential 2026 DRAM oversupply as all suppliers add capacity, (3) NAND market already contracting (-8.5% Q4). Foundry subscale at <5% market share vs. TSMC's 60%+, with persistent 3nm yield issues. Mobile at 3% YoY growth insufficient to offset memory volatility.

**Risks**: (1) HBM qualification—if Samsung fails to win NVIDIA/hyperscaler approvals in 2025, structural HBM share loss to SK Hynix; (2) Memory oversupply—2026 could see DRAM glut as capex decisions made in 2023-2024 come online; (3) Foundry—continued 3nm/2nm yield issues could force strategic exit; (4) Geopolitical—Korea-China tensions and US export controls on advanced chips; (5) Mobile saturation—smartphone replacement cycles structurally extending despite AI features.

**Recommendation: Hold** pending HBM qualification clarity. Samsung's 50% FCF return (KRW 9.8T dividend + KRW 10T buyback) provides downside support, but upside limited until HBM competitive position improves. If HBM3E 12-Hi wins NVIDIA qualification in Q1-Q2 2025, upgrade to Buy with $65 price target (17x 2026E EPS). If qualification delays extend beyond Q2, risk of downgrade as SK Hynix cements HBM leadership. Memory cycle peak likely in 2025; position for defensiveness rather than growth.
