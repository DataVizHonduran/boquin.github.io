## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

- **Double miss on deliveries and EPS; revenue in-line with depressed expectations.** Q1 2026 was defined by the same macro trifecta pressuring the sector for three-plus years: elevated mortgage rates, constrained affordability, and cautious consumer sentiment — now compounded by geopolitical uncertainty including the Iran conflict flagged by CEO Stuart Miller. Homes delivered of 16,863 missed prior-quarter guidance of 17,698 by ~835 units (-4.7%), while EPS of $0.93 missed the $1.42 consensus by 34.5%.

- **Margin compression driven by incentives (~14%) and land cost pressure.** Homebuilding gross margin contracted 350 bps YoY to 15.2% (vs. 18.7% in Q1 2025), as the company maintained incentive levels of ~14% of sale price alongside base price adjustments to sustain volume. Construction costs improved ~2.5% QoQ and are down 12% over two years, providing a partial offset. SG&A deleveraged 130 bps to 9.8% of homebuilding revenue on lower revenue base.

- **Financial Services deteriorated sharply.** Operating earnings declined 36.4% YoY to $91M from $143M, driven by lower lock volume and reduced profit per locked loan — a reflection of the mortgage rate environment pressuring originations. The segment is the second most significant margin headwind after homebuilding gross margin.

- **Stock initially declined post-earnings, then recovered.** Shares fell in after-hours trading on March 12 before rebounding on March 13 as management's sequential improvement narrative (Q2 delivery guide of 20,000–21,000) offered a more constructive forward read. As of March 17, LEN trades at $97.18 (+1.3%), approximately 33% below its 52-week high of $144.24. The Q1 average buyback price of $118.54 implies management sees current levels as undervalued.

No FY2026 full-year guide raised. FY2026 consensus at $33.56B revenue / $6.75 EPS implies further significant sequential step-ups in H2 that look increasingly at risk.

## ANALYST TAKEAWAY

Multiple compression likely persists near term given full-year consensus (FY2026 EPS: $6.75) now appears increasingly at risk — with Q1 running at an annualized rate of ~$3.72 and affordability headwinds, elevated incentives, and an uncertain mortgage rate path limiting near-term margin recovery. Shares trading at 15.4x forward P/E vs. historical 10–12x trough valuations leaves downside risk if macro does not improve. The land-light model, record cycle times, and improving construction costs remain structural positives for long-cycle value, but the thesis requires rates to normalize — a catalyst absent for over three years.
