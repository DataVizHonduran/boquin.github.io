Macro Summary

Last refreshed: 2026-03-27 10:38 UTC

As of March 27, 2026, the four Unlimited ETFs collectively project a risk-on posture, though with meaningful nuance embedded in duration, currency, and geographic bets that suggest the underlying hedge fund community is positioning for a reflationary, dollar-softening macro regime rather than a straightforward risk rally.

HFND, the flagship hedge fund replication vehicle, runs at 95% net exposure but carries a notable structural short in five-year US Treasuries (FVM6 at -18%), offset by a large long in iBoxx high-yield futures (IBYM6 at +21%) and a meaningful emerging market equity position via MSCI EM futures (+11%). The agency bond allocation (AGZ, +7%) provides ballast without adding duration risk in the belly of the curve. The message from HFND is clear: credit over rates, spread compression over duration, and EM equities as a levered play on a weaker dollar and resilient global growth.

HFGM, the Global Macro ETF and by far the largest fund in the suite at $108M AUM, runs the most aggressive book with gross exposure north of 247% and a net of 188%. The top positions tell a coherent macro story: long HY credit (IBYM6, +37%), long euro versus the dollar (ECM6, +31%), long S&P 500 (ESM6, +30%), and long gold (GCM6, +29%). The simultaneous presence of risk assets and gold is not contradictory — it reflects a stagflation hedge, or more precisely, a bet that nominal assets outperform in an environment of fiscal dominance and persistent inflation. The slight reduction in cash allocation (-1.9 percentage points) day-over-day signals a marginal increase in conviction.

HFMF, the Multi-Factor ETF, mirrors much of HFGM's directional positioning — long euro, long S&P, long gold — but adds a meaningful short in long US bonds (USM6, -21%), an explicit bearish view on duration. The uptick in FGXXX (money market, now 87%) reflects collateral mechanics rather than a risk-off posture; the underlying futures positions represent the true economic exposure. HFMF's combination of long equity, long gold, and short long-duration bonds is a classic reflation trade: the managers appear to expect above-trend nominal growth, sticky inflation, and continued steepening pressure on the long end of the US yield curve.

HFEQ, the equity-focused vehicle, stands out for its international and value orientation. Rather than concentrating in US large-cap growth, it allocates heavily to EM futures (MESM6, +23%), Euro Stoxx 50 (VGM6, +18%), and the financial sector (IXAM6, +18%), alongside Vanguard Value ETF (VTV, +14%). This tilt toward non-US equities, financials, and value factors is consistent with expectations of a weaker dollar, steeper yield curves — which benefit bank net interest margins — and a rotation away from stretched US tech multiples.

In aggregate, the suite is expressing a coherent macro view: dollar weakness, sticky inflation, spread tightening, EM outperformance, and duration risk concentrated at the long end. Gold serves as an asymmetric hedge across multiple funds. The positioning is not defensive — it is an active bet on a reflationary, dollar-softening cycle that rewards international diversification and credit risk over safe-haven Treasuries.
