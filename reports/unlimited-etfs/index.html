Macro Summary

Last refreshed: 2026-04-02 11:50 UTC

Key macro themes: The suite's dominant cross-fund expression is a sharp, coordinated pivot to short sterling and yen — the most decisive re-positioning since the prior snapshot — coupled with persistent longs in high-yield credit and emerging market equities that keep the overall stance tilted risk-on despite selective equity trimming. Underneath, the funds are quietly dismantling their commodity and reflation trades, with gold and copper cut materially across both macro mandates, suggesting a retreat from the China-recovery and fiscal-expansion narratives that supported those positions.

The most dramatic single move is in GBP. HFGM flipped from a 7.4% long to a 13.7% short (a 21-point swing), while HFMF went from a marginal 1.5% short to a 34.2% short — the largest gross position in that fund. This is not a housekeeping trim; it reads as a high-conviction macro call on sterling, likely reflecting deteriorating UK fiscal dynamics, stagflation risks, or renewed sensitivity to current-account pressures. Compounding the move, JPY shorts were simultaneously added or expanded across three of the four funds: HFND initiated a new JPY short, HFGM flipped from a 1.5% long to a 10% short, and HFMF added an 18.2% JPY short as a new position. The carry-trade unwind thesis for yen is being faded — these funds believe JPY weakness persists despite BOJ normalization rhetoric. EUR was also trimmed in both HFGM (31% → 22%) and HFMF (33% → 24%), but remains a meaningful long, implying the underlying bet is EUR strength against GBP and JPY rather than broad dollar weakness.

HFGM, the largest fund by AUM at $111M, is running a 123.6% net long with the heaviest allocation to HY credit futures (+33.9%), maintaining a clear credit risk-on stance even as it trims equities. The S&P 500 Emini position was cut from 30.2% to 22.9%, consistent with reduced conviction in US large-cap upside rather than outright bearishness. Gold was cut from 28.6% to 19.9% and copper from 4.8% to 0.8%, marking an exit from the metals-as-inflation-hedge trade. Duration shorts in 5-year Treasuries remain large (-24.2%), signaling continued conviction that US rates stay elevated.

HFMF, the multi-factor mandate, is the most aggressively positioned after the GBP move. Its 79.5% gross short is driven almost entirely by the sterling position. Long EUR and long S&P 500 (19.4%) were both trimmed but stay positive. The fund also closed its long-dated live cattle April contract and rolled to June while adding a small short, a minor tactical roll.

HFND is more muted — largely cash-heavy (49.7% in money market) with active bets in long HY credit (+19.4%), short 5-year Treasuries (-17%), and long MSCI EM (+10.7%). The new JPY short aligns it directionally with the broader suite.

HFEQ remains unambiguously long global equities at 153.5% net, with a pronounced emerging market overweight (MSCI EM at +22%) alongside Euro Stoxx 50 (+17.6%) and S&P 500 (+15%). The marginal increase in cash and Nikkei exposure suggests slight defensive rotation within the equity sleeve, but the overall posture is fully risk-on.
