Macro Summary

Last refreshed: 2026-03-26 20:16 UTC

The four Unlimited ETFs reflect a broadly risk-on posture with notable nuance across asset classes. The dominant theme running through HFGM and HFMF is a large allocation to gold — both funds carry roughly 27–29% in the June gold futures contract following a roll from the April expiry — signaling that the strategy sees precious metals as a core macro expression rather than a tactical hedge. This is complemented by meaningful long positions in HY credit futures (iBoxx iSh $HY) across HFND at 20.5% and HFGM at 36.3%, suggesting confidence that corporate credit spreads remain contained despite macro uncertainty. The simultaneous long in risk assets and gold is characteristic of a regime where managers are positioned for both a soft landing and a potential flight-to-safety scenario.

The rate positioning tells a clearer story on duration. HFND is meaningfully short 5-year Treasuries (-18.0%) and HFMF is short 30-year bonds (-20.9%), both implying an expectation that the long end of the curve will face continued pressure — either from sticky inflation, fiscal supply dynamics, or a Fed that keeps rates higher for longer. Notably, this short-duration stance coexists with the credit longs, a spread trade that effectively bets on tightening credit conditions outpacing sovereign rate moves.

Currency positioning is one of the more distinctive tilts. HFGM carries a 30.5% long in Euro FX futures and HFMF adds another 32.6%, making a long EUR/USD position the largest single non-cash trade across those two vehicles. This reflects a bearish USD view, likely tied to expectations of U.S. fiscal deterioration, a more dovish Fed path relative to the ECB, or relative growth dynamics favoring Europe.

Geographic equity exposure leans toward emerging markets and international developed. HFEQ's largest position is MSCI EM futures at 23.2%, and HFND adds another 11.2% in EM. HFEQ also holds Euro Stoxx 50 futures at 17.7% and XAF Financial sector futures at 17.9%, tilting the equity book toward financials and European names. The S&P 500 E-mini appears in HFGM (29.9%) and HFMF (26.9%), keeping some domestic U.S. exposure in the more macro-oriented funds, but the overall equity bias skews international. This positioning suggests the managers see better risk-adjusted returns outside the U.S., consistent with a weaker dollar view and relatively cheaper non-U.S. valuations.

The main position changes versus yesterday were purely mechanical — the roll of gold futures from the April contract (GCJ6) to the June contract (GCM6) across all four ETFs. There were no strategic additions or removals beyond this. The aggregate picture is one of a strategy that has conviction in gold as a store of value, believes credit spreads will hold, is positioned for dollar weakness via EUR longs, favors EM and international equities over U.S. large-cap growth, and is actively fading Treasury duration. It is a nuanced risk-on expression with built-in hedges rather than an outright directional bet.
