Macro Summary

As of March 25, 2026, the four Unlimited ETFs reflect a broadly risk-on posture with high-conviction macro overlays that go well beyond simple equity exposure. Net exposures range from 95% in HFND to 188% in HFGM, with leverage deployed primarily through futures — the large money market positions across all four funds are collateral, not defensive cash.

The sharpest cross-portfolio theme is a bearish dollar view. HFGM and HFMF both carry EUR/USD futures longs of 31–33% of AUM, a concentrated position that signals deliberate macro conviction rather than passive FX drift. No offsetting dollar-long exposure appears anywhere in the four books, making this one of the clearest and most consistent bets in the suite.

Gold reinforces the same macro narrative. HFGM and HFMF each hold 26–28% in gold futures, pairing well with the EUR long to form a classic reflationary trade — long hard assets, short the dollar, positioned for real yields to fall. The implication is that the funds see persistent inflation or at minimum a regime where the Fed is cutting into above-target inflation, compressing real rates and weakening the currency.

Duration is the counterweight. HFND is short US 5-year Treasuries at roughly 18% of AUM, while HFMF is short the US Long Bond at 21%. Both funds are therefore structurally bearish on fixed income even as they hold gold — a combination that makes sense if the thesis is inflationary rather than recessionary. The funds are not hedging against a slowdown; they are fading the idea that bonds will rally.

Credit sentiment is unambiguously constructive. HFND and HFGM both run large iBoxx High Yield futures longs (21% and 37% respectively), betting on spread compression or at minimum stable spreads in a growth-positive environment. These are among the largest non-cash positions in both funds.

Equity allocation leans toward emerging markets and Europe over the US. HFND leads with MSCI EM futures at 11%, while HFEQ stacks EM at 23%, Euro Stoxx 50 at 18%, and a Nikkei position alongside a Vanguard Value tilt — a clear preference for international and value exposure over US growth. HFGM is the exception with S&P 500 futures at 30%, though it pairs this with the EUR long, suggesting partial currency hedging intent. HFEQ's 18% weight in XAF Financial futures stands out as a concentrated sector bet on financials outperforming — consistent with a steeper yield curve or credit expansion narrative.

The aggregate picture is coherent: weaker dollar, higher gold, credit spreads holding, EM and European equities outperforming, and duration to avoid. This is the first snapshot in the 20-day rolling archive, so no shift in positioning can yet be tracked — future runs will reveal whether these macro bets are being added to, trimmed, or replaced.
