Key themes: The VIP basket is currently executing a high-conviction rotation from mega-cap "Magnificent Seven" proxies into a specialized "AI-Infrastructure" play, specifically targeting the intersection of semiconductor hardware and the energy requirements of data centers. Hedge fund managers are pivoting away from broad software and consumer platforms toward the physical and computational bottlenecks of the AI build-out.

The portfolio’s 34.8% technology tilt is highly concentrated in the semiconductor value chain, with Marvell (2.96%), Micron (2.37%), and AMD (2.19%) serving as core pillars. However, the most striking evolution is the aggressive expansion into the energy-compute nexus. The addition of Talen Energy (2.17%), Vistra (2.12%), and Core Scientific (2.12%) suggests that the smart money views power generation and crypto-adjacent infrastructure as essential components of the AI trade. This is a significant departure from traditional tech positioning, moving the bet from the "brain" of AI to the "nervous system" and "fuel" required to run it.

The recent removal of heavyweights like NVDA, AVGO, MSFT, and AMZN indicates a tactical de-risking of the most crowded, high-multiple names in favor of secondary beneficiaries. While the basket saw a 3.50% monthly drawdown, the 11.96% three-month surge suggests that the recent rotation into specialized hardware and utilities is yielding alpha as the market seeks the next leg of the infrastructure cycle. The reduction in weightings for SNDK (-2.5%) and AMD (-2.2%) further implies a move toward more granular, niche exposure rather than broad-based semiconductor beta.

Collectively, this positioning implies a macro view of "resilient growth with structural scarcity." By adding JPM (2.09%) and SPGI (2.09%), managers are maintaining a foothold in financial stability, but the heavy tilt toward industrials (11.0%) and utilities (6.3%) through new positions like TLN and VST suggests they expect a capital-intensive, supply-constrained environment. The basket is betting that earnings growth will be driven less by consumer software scaling and more by the massive, non-discretionary capital expenditures required to build out the next generation of global compute capacity.
