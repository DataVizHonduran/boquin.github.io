The recent performance profile, characterized by a sharp -10.64% three-month drawdown and a -6.12% monthly decline, indicates that the VIP cohort is currently navigating a period of significant de-risking or a tactical rotation that has yet to find its footing. Despite this recent weakness, the underlying conviction remains heavily skewed toward Technology (31.2%) and Financial Services (16.2%), creating a significant tilt toward growth and transaction-based economies compared to the broader market. The most telling signal lies in the recent turnover. This aggressive entry into high-beta tech is contrasted by the removal of Capital One (COF) and MercadoLibre (MELI), signaling a retreat from consumer credit sensitivity and emerging market volatility in favor of domestic, high-margin scalability. The inclusion of Talen Energy (2.32%) and Norfolk Southern (2.31%) further suggests that while the core is tech-centric, managers are hedging against structural shifts in energy demand and physical infrastructure. Collectively, this positioning implies a macro outlook that is cautiously defensive on the broad economy—evidenced by the YTD drag—but remains intensely bullish on the earnings power of dominant, scalable platforms that can thrive regardless of the wider interest rate or growth environment.
