Analyst Commentary

Last refreshed: 2026-06-15 18:42 UTC

Key themes suggest a concentrated bet on the physical and computational infrastructure required to sustain the artificial intelligence build-out, paired with a pivot toward the energy and financial pillars supporting that expansion. The hedge fund community is moving away from "Big Tech" software and consumer hardware ubiquity in favor of the high-conviction semiconductor equipment and power-generation plays that underpin the AI value chain.

The VIP basket’s 34.8% technology tilt is heavily concentrated in the semiconductor lifecycle, with Marvell (3.06%), Micron (2.62%), and Applied Materials (2.51%) leading the charge. This positioning reflects a granular shift from generalist mega-cap exposure toward the specialized hardware and lithography providers, such as ASML (2.40%) and Lam Research (2.49%), that are essential to the next phase of silicon scaling. Notably, the removal of massive incumbents like Microsoft, Apple, and Broadcom suggests that top-tier managers are rotating out of crowded, high-valuation software and platform plays to capture alpha in the more volatile, supply-constrained semiconductor equipment and memory sectors.

The basket also reveals a sophisticated macro play on the intersection of AI and energy. The inclusion of Hut 8 (2.24%), Core Scientific (2.20%), Talen Energy (2.09%), and Vistra (2.05%) indicates a collective conviction that the massive power requirements of data centers are a primary bottleneck, turning utility and crypto-infrastructure plays into proxy bets on compute demand. This is complemented by a significant rotation into financial stability, evidenced by the new 2.08% position in JPMorgan Chase and the addition of Berkshire Hathaway.

Overall, the 21.16% three-month return suggests this rotation is working effectively. By shedding consumer-facing names like Carvana and high-multiple software like AppLovin, the VIP cohort is expressing a view that earnings growth will be driven by the industrialization of AI rather than consumer discretionary spending or software SaaS cycles. The positioning implies a macro outlook where capital expenditure in infrastructure remains the dominant driver of equity returns, even as the broader market faces potential volatility in traditional big-tech valuations.

The GVIP portfolio exhibits a high-conviction tilt toward high-momentum names facing significant short pressure, creating several potent squeeze candidates. Most notable is the crypto-adjacent cluster comprising Hut 8 (HUT) and Core Scientific (CORZ), both sitting at a maximum short interest decile of 10. With HUT posting 135.4% momentum and CORZ showing 67.1%, these names represent extreme convexity if the VIP long consensus forces a cover. Marvell (MRVL) also stands out as a primary squeeze target, combining a massive 237.2% three-month price surge with an 8th decile short interest, suggesting a crowded trade that could accelerate further on any positive catalyst.

A distinct battleground is emerging in the utility and energy space, where shorts are actively contesting VIP positions. Talen Energy (TLN) and Vistra Corp (VST) both face heavy shorting at the 8th decile, though their momentum profiles diverge sharply; TLN maintains a positive 21.8% trend while VST has drifted -5.0%, indicating a tug-of-war between momentum followers and short sellers.

Conversely, the semiconductor space provides the most robust "clean longs" for the fund. Applied Materials (AMAT) and Lam Research (LRCX) demonstrate ideal quantitative characteristics, pairing strong momentum of 70.2% and 77.9% respectively with relatively low short interest in the 4th and 5th deciles. These names, alongside AMD’s 178.4% momentum, suggest a core of high-velocity growth that is largely unencumbered by significant bearish opposition, providing a stable foundation compared to the volatile squeeze candidates.
