Analyst Commentary

Last refreshed: 2026-05-14 16:12 UTC

Key themes: The VIP basket is signaling a concentrated, high-conviction bet on the physical and computational infrastructure required for the next phase of the AI build-out. Hedge fund managers are moving away from pure-play software and consumer platforms toward the hardware, semiconductor, and power-generation layers that underpin digital scaling.

The current positioning reflects an aggressive tilt toward the technology and communication services sectors, which together command over 47% of the portfolio, significantly outstripping broader market benchmarks. This is not merely a bet on software, but a granular focus on the semiconductor value chain and the energy-intensive infrastructure supporting it. The heavy weighting in AMD (4.17%), Micron (3.63%), and Sandisk (4.65%) suggests that the smart money is doubling down on memory and processing power. This conviction is further evidenced by the significant increases in SNDK, MU, and AMD, where managers have aggressively scaled existing positions to capture momentum in the hardware cycle.

The recent rotation is telling: the removal of Meta and DoorDash indicates a cooling interest in consumer-facing digital platforms and discretionary e-commerce, while the addition of ASML (2.0%) and Tesla (2.0%) suggests a pivot toward the foundational lithography and autonomous hardware sectors. The inclusion of Union Pacific (1.9%) alongside GE Vernova (2.47%) and Vertiv (2.85%) points to a sophisticated macro play on the "real economy" requirements of the AI era—specifically logistics and the electrical grid.

With a 3-month return of 11.25% and a YTD performance of 11.96%, the basket is clearly riding a momentum wave driven by capital expenditure cycles. The collective positioning implies a macro view that earnings growth will be driven by industrial and technological capacity rather than consumer resilience, as evidenced by the negligible 1.2% allocation to consumer defensive stocks. Hedge funds are essentially betting that the structural demand for compute and the energy required to power it will remain the primary drivers of alpha, even as they rotate out of the high-multiple consumer growth names that dominated previous cycles.

The GVIP portfolio is currently dominated by high-octane squeeze candidates, particularly within the semiconductor and infrastructure verticals. Sandisk Corporation (SNDK) stands out as the primary catalyst, combining a massive 136.6% three-month momentum surge with a heavy 9th decile short interest representing 11.4% of the float. This explosive profile is mirrored by Micron (MU), which boasts 97.4% momentum and a 7th decile short interest, suggesting that VIP longs are successfully forcing a liquidity event in the memory space. Core Scientific (CORZ) represents the most extreme squeeze risk, with a staggering 23.4% float shorted at the 10th decile, though its high 79.2 P/E suggests significant valuation volatility.

The portfolio also reveals intense battlegrounds where VIP conviction meets heavy bearish positioning. EchoStar (SATS) is the most contentious holding, with a massive 36.2% float shorted at the 10th decile, creating a high-stakes tug-of-war against its 23.3% momentum. Similarly, Carvana (CVNA) remains a point of friction, as its negative 0.3% momentum clashes with a 9th decile short interest.

In contrast, the "clean long" cluster offers a much more stable foundation for the ETF. These names exhibit robust momentum with minimal short-side friction. AMD leads this group with 122.1% momentum and a manageable 5th decile short interest, while the mega-cap tech core—including Amazon (AMZN), Alphabet (GOOGL), and NVIDIA (NVDA)—shows strong price appreciation alongside very low short interest in the 2nd to 4th deciles. This combination of high momentum and low opposition provides the most reliable trend-following signal within the current holdings.
