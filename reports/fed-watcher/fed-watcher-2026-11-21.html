Executive Summary

The Federal Reserve has shifted toward a decisively more hawkish posture following the October 28 meeting. While the FOMC held rates steady in October, released minutes and subsequent speeches reveal a strong internal consensus for further tightening in December. Key drivers include mounting inflation worries, the potential for "inflation psychology" to take root, and concerns over AI-driven price pressures. Governor Waller and Governor Cook have emerged as prominent hawks, with Waller suggesting a potential "shock" 50bp hike may be necessary. The general sentiment has evolved from "data-dependent pausing" to an active expectation of rate hikes to prevent inflation from becoming entrenched.

Thematic Analysis

1. Inflation Assessment
Inflation is viewed as persistent and potentially psychological. Governor Cook explicitly mentioned the need to break the "psychology" where people see higher inflation as normal. There is a growing concern that AI impacts and high oil prices are contributing to inflationary pressures.

2. Labor Market Views
While labor market dynamism is being monitored (Waller, 11/10), the focus has shifted from labor market risks to inflation urgency. The "labor market risk" narrative previously championed by Jefferson and Cook has been superseded by the "inflation urgency" narrative.

3. Growth Outlook
The outlook is cautious, with Michelle Bowman highlighting a specific risk of a "large correction" in equity markets if the AI productivity boom fails to materialize.

4. Financial Conditions
The Fed is monitoring banking system vulnerabilities (NY Fed 11/12) and credit card delinquency trends, particularly the divide by income level (NY Fed 11/10).

5. Balance Sheet Policy (QT)

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
