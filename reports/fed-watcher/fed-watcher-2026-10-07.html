Executive Summary

The Federal Reserve has pivoted to a tightening cycle, delivering its first interest rate hike in over three years during the September 16 meeting. The move was unanimous and driven by "stubborn inflation," with the FOMC signaling at least one more hike is likely before the end of 2026. While the labor market remains "pretty good," officials are increasingly concerned that inflation is not retreating timely enough. Recent communications from regional presidents, specifically Kashkari and Logan, suggest a strong hawkish tilt, with Logan explicitly calling for "50 bps or more" in further hikes. The committee is now balancing the need to lower inflation pressures against the risk of a "doom loop" in housing and the impact of tariffs on consumer prices.
