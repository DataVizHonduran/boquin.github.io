Executive Summary

The Federal Reserve has pivoted toward a more aggressive tightening bias over the last 30 days. Following a rate hike on September 16, recent communications and the release of the September FOMC minutes indicate a consensus that inflation remains persistent, with several officials now signaling further hikes are likely before the end of 2026. Key drivers of this hawkish shift include soaring energy prices, a recent inflation uptick to 3.7%, and the emerging view that AI-driven demand is replacing tariffs as a primary driver of core price pressures. While some officials emphasize flexibility in the pace of hikes, the overall trajectory has shifted from a neutral/pause stance to an active tightening cycle to prevent an "inflationary mindset."
