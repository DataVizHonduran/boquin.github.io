Executive Summary

The Federal Reserve has pivoted back to a tightening cycle, raising interest rates by 25 basis points on September 16, 2026—the first hike since 2023. This move was driven by "stubborn inflation" and a desire for a "timelier" drop in price levels. The policy shift follows a period of internal debate, highlighted by Chair Kevin Warsh’s August signals at Jackson Hole that hikes might be necessary. While some members, like Governor Waller, initially advocated for holding rates steady to "give disinflation a chance," the FOMC ultimately opted for tightening. The decision occurred amid political tension, as reports indicate the move defied demands for rate cuts from the Trump administration.
