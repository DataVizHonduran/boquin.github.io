Executive Summary

The Federal Reserve has pivoted to a tightening cycle, implementing its first interest rate hike since 2023 during the September 16 meeting. This shift is driven by "stubborn inflation" and faster-than-expected economic growth. Chair Kevin Warsh, who signaled the possibility of hikes during his August Jackson Hole speech, now leads a committee that has signaled at least one more hike is likely before the end of 2026. While some governors (Waller) initially urged patience to "give disinflation a chance," the prevailing consensus has shifted toward combating sticky inflation. The policy reversal marks a significant departure from the previous easing/pause regime, defying external political pressure for rate cuts.
