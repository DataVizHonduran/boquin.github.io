The Federal Reserve has pivoted back to a tightening bias, evidenced by a rate hike on September 16, 2026—the first in over three years. The labor market is perceived as a source of strength that allows for tighter policy. Kashkari described the labor market as "pretty good," suggesting that the risk of premature easing is lower than the risk of entrenched inflation.

3. Growth Outlook
Growth remains sufficient to support further tightening, though the focus has shifted toward how AI adoption is altering employment expectations and energy demand (SF Fed president noted AI could extend energy shocks).
