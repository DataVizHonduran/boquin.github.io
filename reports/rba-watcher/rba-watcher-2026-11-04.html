Executive Summary

The RBA has pivoted sharply back to a tightening bias, culminating in a cash rate hike to 4.85% on November 3, 2026—the fifth increase this year and an 18-year high. This decision followed a "materially worse" inflation outlook signaled by Deputy Governor Hauser in October and a September trimmed mean CPI print of 3.7%, which exceeded forecasts. The Board is currently split over the risks of further tightening, but Governor Bullock has emphasized that "pain now" is necessary to prevent more severe economic outcomes later. The November Statement on Monetary Policy (SMP) confirms that inflation is forecast to remain stubbornly high, overriding the easing cycle initiated in early 2025.

Thematic Analysis

1. Trimmed Mean CPI & Inflation Outlook
The primary driver for the November hike was the September trimmed mean CPI rising to 3.7%, which was above forecasts. The RBA views the inflation outlook as "materially worse," suggesting that the return to the 2-3% target band is proving more difficult than previously anticipated.

2. Labor Market (employment, participation, wages)
Data from October indicates that wage growth slowed in September after an August bounce. However, the RBA remains concerned about the persistence of inflation, suggesting that labor market slack has not yet reached a level sufficient to anchor prices.

3. Housing Market & Credit Conditions
The rate hike to 4.85% (an 18-year high) is expected to further dampen housing market activity. External reports highlight a "housing slump" as a consequence of the RBA's aggressive return to tightening.

4. AUD / REER & External Sector
Governor Bullock has specifically addressed the relationship between inflation risks and the AUD/USD exchange rate, indicating that the currency's movement is a key variable in the transmission of monetary policy.

5. Neutral Rate Estimate & Real Rate Stance
Chris Kent (RBA) noted a "reasonable indication" that neutral rates are increasing both domestically and globally, suggesting the RBA may need to maintain a higher nominal rate to achieve the same real restrictive effect.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The RBA has abandoned the dovish tone of the Feb 2025 cut cycle. Guidance has shifted from "data-dependent" to an active tightening phase, with the Governor explicitly signaling a willingness to inflict short-term economic "pain" to secure long-term price stability.
