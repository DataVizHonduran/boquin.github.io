Executive Summary

The past 30 days mark a significant hawkish pivot for the RBA. Following a September CPI print of 4.3% and a trimmed mean CPI rise to 3.7%, the Board has shifted from a cutting cycle (initiated Feb 2025) toward a restrictive posture. Deputy Governor Hauser explicitly warned that the inflation outlook is "materially worse," and the November 3rd Monetary Policy Decision reflects a regime of "higher-for-longer" or potential further hikes. The RBA is now grappling with "stubborn" inflation and a potential loss of public trust, with market participants pricing cash rates potentially exceeding 5.0%. The primary focus has shifted from managing a soft landing to combating a resurgence in inflation risks.

3. Housing Market & Credit Conditions
Financial conditions are described as restrictive, with interest rates reaching levels not seen since 2008. The RBA is monitoring the impact of these high rates on mortgages and consumer spending.

4. AUD / REER & External Sector
External focus remains on global fragmentation and the "safe-haven" status of the USD, though the immediate domestic inflation crisis has superseded external sector concerns in recent board communications.

5. Neutral Rate Estimate & Real Rate Stance
There is an emerging consensus (supported by Chris Kent's comments) that the "neutral rate" is increasing. This implies that even if the cash rate remains steady, the "real" restrictive power may be diminishing, necessitating higher nominal rates to achieve the same effect.

6. Forward Guidance Evolution
The RBA has moved from the Feb 2025 easing cycle to a "warning shot" phase. Guidance is now heavily skewed toward the upside, with the Board signaling that the case for further hikes has "firmed."
