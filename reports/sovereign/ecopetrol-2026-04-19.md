## 2. KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths:**
- **Dominant market position:** National oil company of Colombia (88.49% government-owned), controlling ~70% of domestic hydrocarbon production; sole operator with integrated upstream, midstream, refining, and transmission assets (ISA subsidiary).
- **Reserve replacement resilience:** 121% reserve replacement ratio in 2025 — highest in four years — with proven reserves of 1.944 Bboe and reserve life index of 7.8 years; positive reserve trend despite moratorium on new exploration contracts.
- **Strong EBITDA generation:** ~41% EBITDA margin on ~COP 120 trillion ($28–29 billion) annual revenue base (FY2025); EBITDA of ~COP 49–50 trillion ($11.5–12 billion) provides substantial debt service headroom.
- **Controlled leverage:** Gross debt/EBITDA of 2.3× (FY2025) — below company's 2.5× ceiling; ex-ISA leverage of 1.6× reflects healthy upstream-only profile; $1.25 billion five-year loan closed April 2026 extends maturity profile.

**Credit Constraints:**
- **Sovereign ceiling cap:** All three agencies cap Ecopetrol at or near Colombia's sovereign rating; S&P's April 2026 one-notch sovereign cut directly translated to a one-notch Ecopetrol downgrade to BB-, narrowing the rating buffer.
- **Exploration moratorium / reserve depletion risk:** President Petro's no-new-exploration policy leaves the company reliant on enhanced recovery; without new exploration contracts, reserve life erodes toward an estimated 2031 depletion horizon at current production rates under worst-case extrapolation.
- **FX mismatch:** Majority of financial debt is USD-denominated while revenues and costs are partly in COP; COP depreciation (4,000–4,500 COP/USD range) creates balance-sheet translation risk and elevates USD debt service costs relative to peso revenues.
- **Government dividend extraction:** 50.1% payout ratio on FY2025 net income (~COP 9 trillion); Colombian government's fiscal dependence on Ecopetrol dividends limits financial flexibility under stress — government owns 88.49% and appoints eight of nine board directors.
- **Commodity price exposure:** FY2025 reserves certified at $68.64/bbl Brent; S&P's base case assumes leverage above 2.0× for the next two years — vulnerable to oil price downside in a global demand-slowdown scenario.

### 3B. Debt Trajectory Narrative

Ecopetrol's consolidated leverage (2.3× gross debt/EBITDA) has stabilized below the company's 2.5× ceiling following a period of elevated debt issuance linked to the ISA acquisition (2021). The ex-ISA leverage of 1.6× is industry-competitive and suggests the ISA transmission subsidiary — itself investment-grade on a standalone basis — contributes disproportionate indebtedness relative to earnings. Under the S&P base case, consolidated leverage is expected to remain above 2.0× through 2027 driven by a $5.4–6.7 billion capex program and continued dividend distributions; without sustained oil prices above $70/bbl or material production growth, the company faces a narrow path to deleveraging.

The critical tension: real interest rates on USD debt (~5–6%) likely exceed Ecopetrol's real growth in USD-equivalent cash flows (~2–4%), creating mild self-reinforcing debt dynamics. Management's stated commitment to no incremental net debt for organic capex in 2026 is a mitigating signal.

### 3C. Liquidity Position

Cash of COP 14.1 trillion (~$3.4 billion) as of Q3 2025 provides approximately 1–2 years of near-term maturities coverage. The April 2026 authorization of a $1.25 billion five-year syndicated facility materially extends the maturity profile and reduces near-term refinancing risk. Redemption of the 2026 notes in November 2024 has cleared the most proximate maturity wall. Liquidity is adequate but not abundant given high government dividend extraction and active capex deployment.

## 8. RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Factors that could lead to a downgrade (negative pressure):**
- Colombia sovereign downgraded one further notch (automatic Ecopetrol follow-through given ceiling mechanics)
- Gross Debt/EBITDA sustainably breaches 3.0× on a consolidated basis for 2+ quarters
- Brent crude falls below $50/bbl on sustained basis, compressing EBITDA margin below 30%
- Reserve replacement ratio falls below 80% for two consecutive years without credible path to recovery
- Government demands extraordinary dividends above 60% payout, materially impairing balance sheet

**Factors that could lead to an upgrade (positive pressure):**
- Colombia sovereign upgraded, lifting the rating ceiling
- Post-2026 government restores exploration licensing framework; reserve life extends beyond 10 years
- Net Debt/EBITDA declines durably below 1.5× (ex-ISA) through FCF generation and capex discipline
- Meaningful reduction in government ownership toward 60–70%, improving governance scores and reducing political interference risk
- Sustained Brent above $80/bbl with production growth above 760k boe/d driving EBITDA expansion

## 9. ANALYST CONCLUSION

Ecopetrol occupies a structurally paradoxical credit position: a company with a **standalone credit profile** of bb+/bbb- (S&P/Fitch), reflecting genuinely strong operational fundamentals — 41% EBITDA margins, 121% reserve replacement, 2.3× leverage — that is **systematically downgraded** to the Colombian sovereign floor. The April 2026 S&P sovereign downgrade to BB- dragged Ecopetrol with it, widening the gap between fundamental quality and market-implied creditworthiness. The near-term rating path is Negative: Fitch's BB+ with Negative Outlook is the most likely agency to converge downward, potentially aligning all three agencies at BB/Ba2 within 12–18 months if Colombia's sovereign trajectory continues to deteriorate or if oil prices fall materially below $65/bbl. The 2026 Colombian presidential election is the single most important credit event: a pro-hydrocarbon successor government that restores exploration licensing would structurally address the reserve depletion risk and could catalyze a multi-notch re-rating toward investment grade on a 2–3 year horizon. A continuation of current energy policy would not.

> **We assign Ecopetrol S.A. an implied rating of Ba1/BB with a Negative Outlook, reflecting a high-quality NOC whose creditworthiness is held hostage to Colombia's sovereign trajectory and an exploration moratorium that, if sustained, structurally erodes long-term cash flow generation capacity.**
