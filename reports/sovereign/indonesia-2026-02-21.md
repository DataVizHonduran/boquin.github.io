| Item | Detail |

| **Implied Rating** | **Baa2** |
| **Outlook** | **Negative** |
| **Prior Rating** | Moody's: Baa2/Negative (outlook cut Feb 5, 2026); Fitch: BBB/Stable (Mar 2025) |
| **Action** | **Affirm Baa2 / Maintain Negative Outlook** |

> **Rationale:** Indonesia's credit profile remains underpinned by strong structural fundamentals — robust GDP growth (~5%), a low debt/GDP ratio (~40%), and ample reserve coverage (6.3 months of imports) — that distinguish it favorably within the Baa cohort. However, Moody's Negative Outlook revision of February 5, 2026 reflects genuine concerns about fiscal drift under President Prabowo Subianto: a primary balance that has slipped into deficit for the first time outside of the COVID period, rising off-budget commitments via the Danantara state investment vehicle, and governance concerns from the military's expanded role in policymaking and economic oversight. These developments introduce meaningful medium-term downside risk to a credit that has earned its investment-grade status through decades of disciplined fiscal management.

---

## 2. KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths:**
- **Structural growth strength:** IMF projects GDP growth of **5.1% in 2026** (World Bank: 5.0%), reflecting Indonesia's demographically-driven consumption base, commodity export competitiveness, and structural investment momentum; the government and Bank Indonesia target even higher growth at 5.33–5.4%.
- **Low public debt:** At approximately **40% of GDP** (central government basis), Indonesia's debt ratio is well below the IMF's 60% benchmark and peers; decades of fiscal conservatism post-Asian Financial Crisis have built a structural debt buffer rarely seen in large emerging markets.
- **Robust reserve position:** Gross FX reserves of **US$154.6bn** (January 2026) cover **6.3 months of imports** — more than double the 3-month adequacy threshold — providing a substantial cushion against external shocks and portfolio outflows.
- **Credible monetary framework:** Bank Indonesia maintained inflation at 2.92% year-end 2025 (within the 2.5%±1% target), while cutting the policy rate 150bps to 4.75% through 2025, demonstrating effective inflation management alongside accommodative growth support.

**Credit Constraints:**
- **Fiscal slippage under Prabowo:** The 2025 budget deficit reached **2.92% of GDP** — the widest in two decades outside COVID — driven by revenue shortfalls amid sustained high spending; the primary balance turned negative for the first time since 2020, signaling that regular revenues no longer cover non-interest expenditures.
- **Governance deterioration:** President Prabowo has expanded the military's role in domestic governance, appointed military personnel to senior policy positions, and launched Danantara — a large off-budget state investment vehicle that reduces fiscal transparency; Moody's cited policy uncertainty and governance concerns as primary drivers of the February 2026 Negative Outlook revision.
- **Free Nutritious Meals program fiscal drag:** The flagship Rp121 trillion (US$7.5bn, ~4.5% of 2025 budget) free meals program adds persistent expenditure pressure without a commensurate revenue offset; the program faces corruption and implementation risks, with the Corruption Eradication Commission flagging irregularities.
- **FX and external vulnerabilities:** Rising current account deficits (IMF projects -1.6% GDP in 2026) amid rupiah depreciation pressure create external financing needs in an environment of global risk-off; capital outflows have pressured the IDR, with reserve drawdowns in late 2025 signaling some external stress.

### 3B. Debt Trajectory Narrative

Under the IMF's 2025 Article IV baseline, Indonesia's debt/GDP is expected to remain anchored near 40–43% through 2030, underpinned by nominal GDP growth outpacing the fiscal deficit. However, the slippage of the primary balance into deficit territory (a structural first outside COVID) alters the debt dynamics: with nominal interest costs running at roughly 2–2.5% of GDP and the primary balance in deficit, the debt ratio is now self-reinforcing absent a return to primary surplus. The IMF estimates the fiscal deficit at ~2.8–2.9% of GDP in 2025–2026, above the government's 2.68% target and approaching the statutory 3% ceiling — a constraint that has historically anchored Indonesia's fiscal credibility.

### 3C. External Liquidity

At US$154.6bn and 6.3 months of import coverage, Indonesia's reserve position is among the strongest in Asia and provides ample protection against short-term external shocks. However, the prospective widening of the current account deficit to -1.6% GDP in 2026 (IMF projection) will require gross external financing of ~US$25–30bn annually, including capital account flows. The rupiah's depreciation pressure — a recurring concern during global risk-off episodes — remains a latent vulnerability given Indonesia's commodity-import dependence and the investment income component of the current account.

## 4. WILLINGNESS TO PAY

| Dimension | Score (1–5) | Commentary |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Governance & Rule of Law (WGI) | **3.0** | WGI scores historically in the 45th–55th percentile; the Prabowo administration's military appointments and weakened institutional oversight risk measurable deterioration in the next WGI update |
| Fiscal Policy Credibility | **2.5** | The 3% statutory deficit ceiling has historically been a credibility anchor; the 2025 breach (at 2.92%) and rising off-budget commitments via Danantara represent a meaningful erosion of that anchor; Moody's February 2026 Negative Outlook validates this concern |
| Central Bank Independence | **4.0** | Bank Indonesia has maintained credibility through multiple political cycles; the 2025 easing cycle was well-calibrated and data-dependent; no political interference observed |
| Debt Payment Track Record | **5.0** | Indonesia has maintained an uninterrupted debt service record since the post-Asian Financial Crisis restructuring in 1998/1999; no restructuring events in 25+ years |

**Synthesis:** Indonesia's willingness to pay is firmly established by its track record and the disciplined approach of the Ministry of Finance and Bank Indonesia. The primary concern is the shift in the political economy under Prabowo toward social spending expansion and off-budget vehicles that could materially weaken fiscal discipline over time. The critical test will be whether the statutory 3% deficit ceiling — Indonesia's most visible fiscal credibility anchor — is respected or amended in the coming budget cycles.

## 8. RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Factors that could lead to a downgrade (negative pressure):**
- Fiscal deficit exceeds the 3% statutory ceiling on a sustained basis, or is formally raised to accommodate Prabowo's social spending agenda
- Danantara accumulates material contingent liabilities that are ultimately absorbed by the sovereign balance sheet
- Primary balance remains in deficit for two or more consecutive years, placing debt/GDP on a rising trajectory
- Capital account reversal driven by governance concerns triggers sustained reserve drawdown, with coverage falling below 4 months of imports
- IMF Article IV flags material fiscal governance risks or downgrade of Indonesia's fiscal framework assessment

**Factors that could lead to an upgrade (positive pressure):**
- Credible medium-term revenue reform implemented (e.g., VAT broadening, improved tax compliance) restoring primary balance to surplus
- Danantara governance framework strengthened with independent oversight, reducing contingent liability risks
- Growth accelerates to 5.5%+, driven by structural investment in manufacturing and services, organically improving fiscal dynamics
- Moody's revises Outlook back to Stable following demonstrated fiscal discipline in the 2026–2027 budget cycle

---

## 9. ANALYST CONCLUSION

Indonesia enters 2026 at a pivotal inflection point: a strong structural credit profile — anchored by robust growth, low debt, and ample reserves — is being tested by a new administration that has demonstrably prioritized social spending and political consolidation over the fiscal conservatism that earned Indonesia its investment-grade ratings. The Moody's Negative Outlook revision of February 5, 2026 marks the first meaningful credit deterioration signal since the COVID period, and it reflects not a crisis but an erosion of the institutional framework that has underpinned Indonesia's credit quality for decades. The near-term default risk is negligible — US$154bn in reserves and a 40% debt/GDP ratio provide substantial protection — but the medium-term trajectory is now genuinely uncertain for the first time since the Asian Financial Crisis. The key variable to monitor in 2026 is whether the Ministry of Finance successfully defends the 3% deficit ceiling against Prabowo's social spending agenda; a breach would likely trigger a formal downgrade to Baa3.

> **We assign Indonesia an implied rating of Baa2 with a Negative Outlook, reflecting exceptional structural buffers (low debt, high reserves, strong growth) that are increasingly offset by fiscal drift, governance deterioration, and rising off-budget commitments under the Prabowo administration.**
