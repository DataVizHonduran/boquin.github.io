### 2. KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths**
* **Strong Federal Linkage:** High probability of support from the UAE federal government and Abu Dhabi, given Dubai's systemic importance to the UAE's non-oil economic strategy.
* **Robust Non-Oil Diversification:** Growth driven by tourism, construction, and financial services, with UAE non-oil sectors contributing ~80% of overall growth (IMF/WB).
* **Positive Fiscal Momentum:** 2026–2028 budget projects a surplus, with total revenues (AED 329.2bn) exceeding expenditures (AED 302.7bn).
* **Manageable Leverage:** Debt-to-GDP ratio remains low at **20.8%**, significantly below the UAE general government gross debt range of **27–32%**.
* **Strong External Position:** Beneficiary of the UAE's current account surplus (**+6.4% of GDP, 2026F**) and FX reserves of **5.3 months of imports** (CBUAE).

**Credit Constraints**
* **Concentration Risk:** High sensitivity to global trade, tourism flows, and real estate cycles compared to oil-rich peers.
* **External Debt Exposure:** UAE total external debt remains elevated at **~87% of GDP** (Jan 2025), including GREs and private sector.
* **Geopolitical Tail Risks:** Proximity to regional volatility (Iran, Israel-Gaza spillover) could pressure investor sentiment and tourism inflows.
* **Monetary Policy Dependency:** AED peg to USD limits independent monetary response; policy is dictated by the US Federal Reserve.
* **Governance Structure:** Limited political pluralism and transparency in sub-sovereign reporting compared to OECD standards (BTI 2026).

**3B. Debt Trajectory Narrative**
Dubai's debt trajectory is **resilient**, characterized by a low debt-to-GDP ratio of **20.8%** and a successful lengthening of average maturities from ~5 to ~10 years since 2015. The projected fiscal surplus for 2026–2028 (revenues AED 329.2bn vs. expenditures AED 302.7bn) suggests that debt will be serviced through organic revenues without requiring federal intervention. Under the IMF baseline (UAE GDP growth 5.0% in 2026), the real interest rate–growth differential remains favorable, supporting an auto-stabilizing debt path.

**3C. External Liquidity**
Liquidity remains **robust**, supported by the UAE's strong current account surplus (+6.4% of GDP, IMF 2025 Article IV) and investment-grade spreads (~50–55 bps over UST for adjacent Abu Dhabi 2026 bond issuances). CBUAE reserve adequacy at 5.3 months of imports provides a credible buffer against short-term rollover pressure, and the AED peg is well-supported.

### 8. RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Factors that could lead to a downgrade (negative pressure):**
- UAE federal sovereign downgrade below Aa3, removing the implicit credit ceiling
- Sustained deterioration in Dubai fiscal position, with Debt-to-GDP exceeding **40%** without credible consolidation plan
- Regional conflict escalation causing a structural decline in tourism receipts (>20% sustained drop) or triggering large GRE contingent liabilities
- Loss of the AED peg or material reserve drawdown below 3 months of import cover

**Factors that could lead to an upgrade (positive pressure):**
- Formal standalone investment-grade rating from Moody's/S&P/Fitch at Aa1 or higher
- Sustained primary fiscal surplus above **3% of emirate GDP** for two or more consecutive years, reducing gross debt below **15% of GDP**
- Significant reduction in UAE total external debt/GDP below 60%, mitigating rollover risk at the consolidated level

---

### 9. ANALYST CONCLUSION

The Emirate of Dubai presents a **resilient** quasi-sovereign credit profile, anchored by strong federal integration within the UAE (Moody's **Aa2** / S&P **AA** / Fitch **AA-**, all Stable) and a domestically driven fiscal surplus trajectory. The Dubai government's 2026–2028 budget surplus structure (revenues AED 329.2bn vs. expenditures AED 302.7bn) and a low debt-to-GDP of **20.8%** provide substantial headroom relative to peers. While the absence of a formal standalone sovereign rating and reliance on federal credit support introduce structural subordination considerations, Dubai's systemic importance to the UAE's non-oil growth strategy makes explicit support credible, as evidenced by the 2009 Abu Dhabi intervention precedent. The primary credit risks remain geopolitical tail events and concentration in tourism and real estate-linked revenues, though these are substantially mitigated by the UAE's strong external reserve position and current account surplus of **+6.4% of GDP** (IMF 2026F).

> **We assign the Emirate of Dubai an implied rating of Aa2 with a Stable outlook, reflecting its robust fiscal surpluses, low leverage, and strong federal credit support, tempered by geopolitical exposure and the structural absence of a standalone sovereign rating.**
