# Colombia — Sovereign Credit Note
**IMF-DSA Framework Analysis | Moody's/Fitch Style**
**Report Date: 2026-02-21**

---

## 1. RATING ACTION & OUTLOOK

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Sovereign** | Republic of Colombia |
| **Report Date** | 2026-02-21 |
| **Implied Rating** | **Ba1** |
| **Outlook** | **Negative** |
| **Prior Rating** | Moody's: Baa3/Stable (downgraded Jun 2025); Fitch: BB/Stable (downgraded Dec 2025 from BB+); S&P: BB/Negative |
| **Action** | **Downgrade to Ba1 / Negative Outlook** |

> **Rationale:** Colombia is at a fiscal crossroads. The Petro administration's invocation of the fiscal rule escape clause in June 2025 — extending the consolidation timeline by three years — triggered simultaneous downgrades by Moody's and S&P and directly led to Fitch's additional December 2025 downgrade to BB. Only Moody's retains Colombia at investment grade (Baa3), and the gap between Moody's and the two-agency consensus at BB reflects an analytical divergence that will likely resolve in favor of sub-investment grade absent meaningful fiscal correction. With a fiscal deficit projected at 6.2–8.1% of GDP in 2026, a primary deficit of ~2–3.4%, debt/GDP rising toward 63–65%, and BanRep raising rates to 10.25% in February 2026 to fight sticky inflation — creating a contractionary policy mix of tight monetary/loose fiscal — Colombia's credit trajectory is deteriorating. The implied Ba1/Negative reflects the median of agency views and the direction of travel.

---

## 2. KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths:**
- **Commodity export base:** Colombia is a significant oil and coal exporter; hydrocarbon revenues represent a meaningful fiscal buffer that, at higher commodity prices, can substantially narrow fiscal gaps; oil production recovery is a medium-term positive.
- **Adequate reserve coverage:** International reserves reach 131% of the IMF's Assessing Reserve Adequacy (ARA) metric as of mid-2025, providing sufficient external liquidity buffer relative to short-term obligations; BanRep's track record of prudent reserve management is a credit positive.
- **Institutional anchors:** Banco de la República (BanRep) has maintained monetary credibility, hiking the policy rate to **10.25%** (February 2026) despite fiscal loosening — demonstrating genuine central bank independence even when monetary and fiscal policy are diverging sharply.
- **Moody's residual investment-grade standing:** Moody's Baa3 rating (barely investment grade) currently provides market access at IG-eligible spreads for IG-mandated investors; if Moody's maintains this position, Colombia avoids the forced selling and spread widening of a full sub-investment grade reclassification.

**Credit Constraints:**
- **Fiscal crisis deepening under Petro:** The primary deficit reached **~3.4% of GDP in 2025** — 10 times larger than the 2023 level; the overall deficit is projected at 6.2–8.1% of GDP in 2026, with economists (BBVA) projecting 7.5% vs. the government's 6.2% target; the fiscal rule escape clause invoked in June 2025 removed the binding multi-year adjustment constraint.
- **Rising debt trajectory:** Debt/GDP has surged from 45% pre-COVID to **~59% (2025)**, projected to reach 62.8% by 2027 (Fitch); the Fitch 'BB' median is 54.4% — Colombia is set to significantly exceed this, making a further downgrade trajectory plausible without fiscal correction.
- **Inflation above target with rate hike cycle:** Inflation of **5.35%** (January 2026) against a 3% target has forced BanRep to raise rates to 10.25% (February 2026) — the contractionary monetary stance compounds fiscal drag, creating a stagflationary risk where high rates slow growth while fiscal deficits widen structural imbalances.
- **Political and reform risk:** President Petro's left-wing administration has consistently struggled to pass fiscal reform legislation; the 2026 congressional elections create additional uncertainty about the fiscal consolidation path; Petro's fiscal management has been the direct trigger for all three agency downgrades in 2025.

### 3B. Debt Trajectory Narrative

Colombia's debt trajectory is unsustainable on current fiscal policies. With a primary deficit of ~3.4% of GDP and real interest rates above 5%, the r-g differential is sharply adverse — the debt ratio is mechanically rising even under moderate growth assumptions. Fitch projects debt/GDP reaching 62.8% by 2027, well above the BB median. The government's own medium-term fiscal framework (MTFF) escape clause extension (3 years) provides a theoretical path to stabilization by 2028–2030, but requires primary surplus delivery of 0.5–1% GDP that the Petro administration has not demonstrated the political capacity to achieve. Under an adverse scenario (oil price below $65/barrel), the debt path could accelerate significantly above 65% of GDP within 3 years.

### 3C. External Liquidity

Colombia's external position is manageable but not comfortable. Reserves at 131% of the ARA metric provide adequate short-term protection, but the widening current account deficit (-3.3% GDP in 2026) and significant FX-denominated sovereign debt (~40–45% of total) create rollover risk that is amplified by the increasing sub-investment grade classification from S&P and Fitch. FDI flows of approximately 4% GDP historically finance the current account, but investor confidence is under pressure from the fiscal trajectory; a sustained reduction in FDI could require reserve drawdowns to fund the external gap.

## 4. WILLINGNESS TO PAY

| Dimension | Score (1–5) | Commentary |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Governance & Rule of Law (WGI) | **3.0** | WGI scores in the 40th–55th percentile; rule of law, anti-corruption bodies, and independent judiciary are institutional strengths; however, security challenges (ELN, drug trafficking) and political polarization under Petro erode institutional quality |
| Fiscal Policy Credibility | **2.0** | The fiscal rule escape clause invocation and repeated failure to achieve primary surplus targets have severely damaged fiscal credibility; all three agencies cited fiscal policy failure as the primary downgrade driver; the MTFF now lacks binding enforcement |
| Central Bank Independence | **4.5** | BanRep's February 2026 rate hike to 10.25% against the government's preference for monetary easing is a powerful signal of genuine central bank independence; BanRep remains one of the most credible central banks in Latin America |
| Debt Payment Track Record | **4.0** | Colombia has maintained continuous sovereign debt service with only a brief near-default episode in the 1990s; no restructuring in modern history; strong Treasury management |

**Synthesis:** Colombia's willingness to pay is intact but its fiscal credibility has been severely damaged. The Petro administration's repeated invocation of escape clauses, failure to pass fiscal reform, and tolerance of a primary deficit 10x the 2023 level represent a structural deterioration in fiscal governance that has directly caused sub-investment grade ratings from two of three agencies. BanRep's aggressive monetary tightening — the one institutional bright spot — creates a policy mix tension that further constrains growth and complicates fiscal adjustment.

## 8. RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Factors that could lead to a downgrade (negative pressure):**
- Moody's downgrade below investment grade (Baa3 → Ba1) would trigger IG-to-junk reclassification, forcing index-linked fund selling and spread widening of 150–200+ bps
- Fiscal deficit widens above 8% GDP in 2026, indicating the MTFF escape clause was insufficient to contain fiscal slippage
- Oil price falls below $65/barrel sustained, reducing hydrocarbon fiscal revenue by 1–2% GDP
- Current account deficit widens above 4% GDP, requiring reserve drawdown to fund external gap

**Factors that could lead to an upgrade (positive pressure):**
- Post-2026 election government enacts structural fiscal reform (pension reform, healthcare reform) returning primary balance to near-zero
- Oil prices recover to $80–85/bbl, providing fiscal windfall and supporting GDP growth above 3.5%
- Moody's affirms Baa3 with Stable Outlook, preventing the IG-to-junk threshold crossing
- Primary deficit narrows to under 1% GDP sustained for two consecutive years

---

## 9. ANALYST CONCLUSION

Colombia stands at the edge of a full sub-investment grade reclassification — a credit event that would have profound consequences for its bond market, pension fund allocations, and sovereign financing costs. The Petro administration's fiscal rule escape clause invocation and persistent primary deficits of 3%+ of GDP have already driven S&P and Fitch to BB, and Moody's lone Baa3 IG holdout is under severe pressure. The core credit tension is structural: an administration committed to social spending expansion is confronting an economy growing at only 2.5% with inflation above target (5.35%), forcing BanRep into rate hikes that further constrain the growth-to-fiscal-adjustment link. Colombia's institutional anchors — BanRep's independence, the judiciary, the Treasury — are credit strengths, but they cannot substitute for fiscal reform that only a willing political executive can deliver. With the 2026 congressional elections approaching, the probability of meaningful fiscal correction this year is low. The crossroads decision point will come when Moody's conducts its next scheduled review; a Moody's downgrade to Ba1 would confirm a full IG-to-junk transition and represent a structural credit deterioration event.

> **We assign Colombia an implied rating of Ba1 with a Negative Outlook, reflecting a fiscal crisis deepening under the Petro administration — with primary deficits at 3.4% of GDP, fiscal rule suspended, debt/GDP breaching 60% and rising, and two of three agencies already at sub-investment grade BB.**
