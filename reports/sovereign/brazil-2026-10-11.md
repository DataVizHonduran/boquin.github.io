## §1 RATING ACTION & OUTLOOK

| Sovereign | Date | Implied Rating | Outlook | Prior Ratings (Moody's/Fitch/S&P) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brazil | 2026-10-11 | **BB / Ba1** | Stable | Ba1 (Moody's, outlook revised to Stable from Positive, May-30-2025) / BB (Fitch, Stable) / BB (S&P, upgraded from BB- Dec-19-2023) | **Affirm** — resilient growth and a credible monetary regime are offsetting a structurally wide fiscal deficit and rising interest burden; agencies have paused the pre-2025 upgrade trajectory pending fiscal consolidation evidence. |

Brazil enters Q4 2026 as a two-sided credit: growth has outperformed (IMF projects 2026 real GDP broadly resilient, strengthening toward 2.5% medium-term — [IMF 2026 Article IV](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/07/23/pr26257-brazil-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consultation)), but the general government overall deficit widened from 6.3% of GDP in 2024 to 8.1% in 2025 as interest payments climbed to 7.7% of GDP, pushing gross debt toward 97.8% of GDP in 2026 ([IMF Country Report 26/191](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1braea2026001.pdf)). Moody's shifted Brazil's outlook to Stable from Positive in May 2025, explicitly citing "pronounced deterioration in debt affordability" and slower fiscal-credibility progress ([Moody's](https://ratings.moodys.com/ratings-news/444170)) — the rating is affirmed here but the pre-2025 path toward investment grade has stalled.

## §2 KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths**

- **Resilient, diversified growth.** The IMF's July 2026 Article IV concluded the economy "has remained remarkably resilient in the face of multiple shocks," with growth strengthening toward ~2.5% over the medium term, aided by VAT reform implementation and hydrocarbon production gains ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/07/23/pr26257-brazil-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consultation)).
- **Credible, independent monetary policy.** The BCB ran Selic at a near-20-year high of 15% through late 2025 and is easing cautiously (14.00% as of August 2026), with the 2026 FSAP finding the financial sector "resilient with systemic risks contained" ([MercoPress](https://en.mercopress.com/2026/05/07/brazil-central-bank-cuts-selic-interest-rate-25-points-to-14.50); [IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/07/23/pr26257-brazil-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consultation)).
- **Low external vulnerability.** External debt is only 29.0% of GDP (2025, up modestly from 27.6% in 2024) and reserves stand at $372.6bn (August 2026) — a deep buffer against a still-wide current account gap ([CEIC/TradingEconomics](https://tradingeconomics.com/brazil/external-debt); [Central Bank of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Brazil)).

**Credit Constraints**

- **Structurally wide fiscal deficit, rising interest burden.** The overall deficit reached 8.1% of GDP in 2025 (from 6.3% in 2024) with interest costs at 7.7% of GDP; the IMF's DSA says stabilizing debt requires improving the primary balance to ~1.5% of GDP over the medium term — a gap not yet closed ([IMF 26/191](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1braea2026001.pdf)).
- **Debt/GDP on an upward path.** Gross general government debt is projected at 97.8% of GDP in 2026 with further increases projected beyond, versus an earlier (2025) baseline that saw debt merely stabilizing near 99% by 2030 — the newer trajectory is less favorable ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1braea2026001.pdf)).
- **Governance and rule-of-law drag.** WGI Rule of Law stood at −0.45 (2024, 116th percentile) and Control of Corruption at −0.41 (116th percentile), both below the 0.0 world average, constraining fiscal-reform credibility and willingness-to-pay scoring ([TheGlobalEconomy](https://www.theglobaleconomy.com/Brazil/wb_ruleoflaw/)).

## §3B DEBT TRAJECTORY

Brazil's general government gross debt is on a rising path — 97.8% of GDP projected for 2026 per the IMF's July 2026 Article IV ([IMF 26/191](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1braea2026001.pdf)), a step up from the 2025 Article IV's baseline expectation that debt would merely stabilize near 99% of GDP by 2030 ([IMF 25/194](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2025/english/1braea2025001-source-pdf.pdf)). The deterioration is interest-cost driven, not growth-driven: interest payments rose to 7.7% of GDP in 2025 as the Selic held near a two-decade high (15% through Q4 2025), while the federal primary balance moved the wrong way — a 0.6% of GDP deficit in 2025 versus 0.4% in 2024 — leaving the overall deficit at 8.1% of GDP, up from 6.3% a year earlier.

The IMF's DSA is explicit on what closes the gap: a primary balance improvement to roughly 1.5% of GDP over the medium term, achieved by saving oil-revenue windfalls, reducing spending rigidities, improving expenditure efficiency, and phasing out inefficient tax expenditures while protecting targeted social spending ([IMF 26/191](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1braea2026001.pdf)). Brazil's debt is overwhelmingly domestic-currency and locally held, which caps FX-driven rollover risk relative to EM peers, but the sheer scale of gross financing needs at elevated real rates means debt dynamics remain primarily an interest-rate/primary-balance problem rather than an external-rollover one — a more favorable risk profile than a typical high-debt EM, but not a resolved one.

## §3C EXTERNAL LIQUIDITY

Reserve adequacy is strong in absolute terms: BCB reserves of $372.6bn (August 2026) comfortably exceed any standard IMF ARA metric threshold and dwarf Brazil's external debt stock, which itself is a modest 29.0% of GDP (2025, up from 27.6% in 2024) ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/brazil/external-debt); [Central Bank of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Brazil)). This reserve cushion is the key offset to the current account gap and is consistent with Brazil's market access remaining intact throughout 2025-2026 — EMBIG spreads near 197 bps and 5Y CDS near 131 bps (Sep-2026) imply no sudden-stop pricing from international investors.

Current account financing is the softer spot: the deficit widened to 3.02% of GDP in 2025 (-$68.8bn) before a projected narrowing to roughly 2.4% of GDP in 2026 (-$60bn), per the BCB's December 2025 Monetary Policy Report ([BCB](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/ri/inflationreport/202512/rpm202512b5i.pdf)). With external debt low and reserves high, the gross external financing requirement is readily covered by portfolio and FDI inflows attracted by Brazil's high real policy rate; the principal vulnerability is a global risk-off shock that simultaneously widens EM spreads and triggers Selic-cutting pressure domestically, narrowing the carry that currently anchors capital inflows.

Willingness to pay remains solid on institutional and market-access grounds — an independent central bank and unbroken payment history — but fiscal credibility is the swing factor dragging the composite down; the gap between stated targets and delivered primary balances is the single largest driver of the Moody's outlook downgrade to Stable in May 2025.

## §8 RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Downgrade Triggers**
- Federal primary balance fails to improve toward the IMF's +1.5%-of-GDP medium-term target, with debt/GDP breaching 105-110%.
- A political-cycle fiscal slippage (2026-2027) that stalls VAT-reform implementation or reopens spending rigidities.
- EMBIG spread sustained above 300-350 bps alongside a Selic re-hike cycle, signaling loss of market confidence in the fiscal path.

**Upgrade Triggers**
- Primary balance reaches the IMF-recommended +1.5% of GDP, placing debt/GDP on a credible downward path.
- Sustained Rule of Law / Control of Corruption percentile improvement from the current 116th-percentile reading.
- Selic normalization toward single digits without inflation re-acceleration, confirming durable disinflation and monetary credibility.

## §9 ANALYST CONCLUSION

Brazil remains a resilient-growth, low-external-vulnerability credit whose rating is capped by a fiscal trajectory moving the wrong way: the 2025-2026 period delivered a wider overall deficit (8.1% of GDP), a heavier interest bill (7.7% of GDP), and debt climbing toward 97.8% of GDP, even as reserves, monetary credibility, and market pricing (EMBIG ~197bps) stayed comfortably benign. The rating holds at the current speculative-grade tier on the strength of institutions and buffers, but the path back toward investment grade that seemed open in 2023-2024 has stalled pending concrete delivery of the IMF's recommended fiscal effort.

> **We assign Brazil an implied rating of BB/Ba1 with a Stable Outlook, reflecting durable external buffers and monetary credibility that offset a structurally wide fiscal deficit and a debt/GDP ratio still rising toward 98% of GDP.**
