| **Action** | **Affirm Aa3 / Stable Outlook** |

> **Rationale:** Saudi Arabia occupies a unique position among EMBI issuers: a high-grade sovereign (Aa3/A+) with among the largest sovereign wealth fund buffers in the world (SAMA reserves US$438.5bn), a low and rising debt-to-GDP ratio (~31–35%), and a credible economic diversification agenda under Vision 2030. Both Moody's and S&P upgraded Saudi Arabia in 2025, reflecting tangible progress in non-oil GDP expansion and institutional fiscal management. The primary credit risks — oil price dependency, Vision 2030 capital expenditure demands, and fiscal deficits widening to 5.3% of GDP in 2025 — are manageable within the sovereign's extraordinary balance sheet strength. The rating is appropriately anchored at Aa3 with Stable Outlook, reflecting balanced upside from diversification against downside from a structural fiscal position that depends on oil prices above the break-even.

---

## 2. KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths:**
- **Extraordinary balance sheet strength:** SAMA foreign exchange reserves of **US$438.5bn** (November 2025) represent among the largest sovereign buffers globally; when combined with the Public Investment Fund (PIF) assets, total sovereign wealth exceeds US$1 trillion, providing an unparalleled ability to absorb fiscal shocks and fund Vision 2030 without sovereign stress.
- **Accelerating diversification:** Non-oil GDP accounts for approximately **57% of GDP** in 2025, with Vision 2030 driving robust non-oil growth in tourism, manufacturing, financial services, and entertainment; IMF projects non-oil real GDP growth at approximately 4–5% in 2026, demonstrating meaningful progress toward the 2030 diversification targets.
- **Low public debt with benign trajectory:** Government debt at **~31% of GDP** (September 2025), rising gradually toward ~35% by 2026 — still among the lowest in the Aa cohort and providing substantial fiscal headroom; net debt, accounting for sovereign assets, is approximately 17% of GDP.
- **Strong growth with rating upgrades:** IMF projects **4.5% GDP growth in 2026** (World Bank: 4.3%), supported by OPEC+ production normalization, non-oil sector momentum, and Vision 2030 investment; both Moody's and S&P upgraded the sovereign in 2025, validating the diversification trajectory.

**Credit Constraints:**
- **Oil price fiscal sensitivity:** The fiscal break-even oil price for Saudi Arabia is estimated at ~US$90–96/barrel for 2026 (Brent currently ~US$76–80/barrel), implying continued structural fiscal deficits; the 2025 deficit reached **5.3% of GDP** — nearly double the original 2.3% target — reflecting lower oil revenues and Vision 2030 capital expenditures.
- **Vision 2030 expenditure demands:** Gigaprojects (NEOM, Red Sea, Diriyah) require sustained capital expenditure in the hundreds of billions, with cost overruns and timeline revisions increasing uncertainty; the IMF has flagged that project spending needs to be rationalized to maintain fiscal sustainability.
- **Current account shift:** Saudi Arabia recorded current account deficits in recent quarters (~-2 to -3% GDP estimated 2025), a marked shift from the surpluses of 2022–2023; the diversification capital expenditure cycle and import intensity of Vision 2030 projects are driving this deterioration.
- **Geopolitical risk premium:** The Yemen conflict, Iran tensions, and regional security dynamics impose a permanent geopolitical risk premium; any escalation affecting oil infrastructure (as occurred in 2019 with the Abqaiq drone strikes) could trigger sharp near-term economic disruption.

### 3B. Debt Trajectory Narrative

Under the IMF's 2025 Article IV baseline, Saudi Arabia's debt/GDP is projected to rise gradually from ~31% to approximately 42% by 2030, driven by continued fiscal deficits to fund Vision 2030. The r-g differential is modestly unfavorable in the near term: with fiscal deficits around 4–5% of GDP and nominal growth of 4–5%, debt accumulation is outpacing the denominator effect. However, the critical mitigant is the sovereign's extraordinary off-budget asset base — SAMA reserves and PIF assets totaling well over US$1 trillion — which means that gross debt metrics substantially overstate fiscal vulnerability. Net debt of ~17% of GDP places Saudi Arabia among the world's strongest sovereign balance sheets on a net basis.

### 3C. External Liquidity

Saudi Arabia's external liquidity position is exceptional. SAMA foreign exchange reserves of US$438.5bn (November 2025) represent well over 18 months of import coverage, providing a virtually unlimited buffer against external shocks. The SAR currency peg to the USD (in place since 1986) eliminates FX mismatch risk on external obligations and anchors monetary credibility. External debt is modest relative to the reserve stock; sovereign Eurobond maturities are manageable and well-spread across tenors. The shift to current account deficit is primarily driven by the capital goods import intensity of Vision 2030 rather than structural competitiveness issues.

## 4. WILLINGNESS TO PAY

| Dimension | Score (1–5) | Commentary |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Governance & Rule of Law (WGI) | **3.0** | WGI scores in the 50th–60th percentile overall; improved under MBS governance consolidation, but political concentration risks (single decision-maker) remain; press freedom and civil society constraints are notable |
| Fiscal Policy Credibility | **4.0** | National Debt Management Center (NDMC) has professionalized debt management significantly; fiscal consolidation during 2016–2018 and 2020–2021 demonstrated willingness to adjust; Vision 2030 spending creates structural deficit pressure, but sovereign wealth provides credibility backstop |
| Central Bank Independence | **3.5** | SAMA operates an orthodox monetary framework within the USD peg constraint; monetary policy is effectively imported from the Federal Reserve; banking system oversight is strong |
| Debt Payment Track Record | **5.0** | Saudi Arabia has maintained uninterrupted debt service historically; never restructured sovereign obligations; the Eurobond program (launched 2016) has been managed impeccably |

**Synthesis:** Saudi Arabia's willingness to pay is beyond question — the sovereign has the financial capacity to service any conceivable debt obligation many times over from its sovereign wealth assets alone. The political concentration of power under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman introduces governance risk, but this has not historically translated into payment disruption risk. The primary analytical focus for creditworthiness is medium-term **fiscal sustainability** relative to the oil price cycle, not willingness to pay.

## 7. ESG CONSIDERATIONS

| ESG Factor | Assessment |
|------------|------------|
| **E – Environmental** | Saudi Arabia's entire economic model is predicated on hydrocarbon extraction, creating significant long-term transition risk as global decarbonization accelerates; Vision 2030 includes investment in renewables (NEOM's solar ambitions, ACWA Power) but the timeline for meaningful non-oil energy transition is long. ## 8. RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Factors that could lead to a downgrade (negative pressure):**
- Sustained oil prices below US$65/barrel for two or more years, driving fiscal deficits above 7–8% of GDP and accelerating sovereign wealth drawdowns
- Vision 2030 cost overruns or project failures trigger contingent fiscal liabilities exceeding US$200bn that must be absorbed on-budget
- Geopolitical escalation (conflict with Iran, infrastructure attack, regional war) materially disrupts oil production or creates sustained uncertainty premium
- SAR peg credibility challenged via speculative attack, requiring large reserve drawdowns to defend

**Factors that could lead to an upgrade (positive pressure):**
- Oil prices sustained above US$90/barrel restoring fiscal surplus and current account surplus simultaneously
- Measurable Vision 2030 progress toward non-oil fiscal revenue self-sufficiency (non-oil revenues >12% GDP)
- Governance improvements reducing political concentration risk (e.g., institutionalization of succession, independent judiciary strengthening)
- Debt/GDP stabilized below 30% with NDMC demonstrating sustained fiscal consolidation discipline

---

## 9. ANALYST CONCLUSION

Saudi Arabia is a credit paradox within the EMBI universe: an Aa-rated sovereign issuing USD bonds into an index populated predominantly by B-to-Baa credits, reflecting the kingdom's extraordinary balance sheet strength relative to its EM classification. The Moody's and S&P upgrades of 2025 validate the structural progress under Vision 2030, particularly the non-oil GDP expansion to 57% of total output. The fiscal challenge is real — deficits of 4–5% of GDP amid oil prices below the break-even — but the SAMA reserve buffer (US$438.5bn) provides a virtually unlimited near-term fiscal cushion. The medium-term credit trajectory depends critically on two variables: whether Vision 2030's non-oil revenue diversification outpaces its capital expenditure demands, and whether oil prices recover toward the fiscal break-even. Governance concentration risk under MBS is a permanent credit feature rather than a near-term trigger, and is already incorporated into the Aa3 rather than Aaa anchor. Saudi Arabia will likely remain a stable Aa3/A+ credit over the next three to five years barring a catastrophic oil price shock or geopolitical disruption.

> **We assign Saudi Arabia an implied rating of Aa3 with a Stable Outlook, reflecting extraordinary sovereign wealth, rapid non-oil economic diversification under Vision 2030, and a low-debt balance sheet, constrained by structural fiscal deficits relative to the oil break-even, current account deterioration, and governance concentration risk.**
