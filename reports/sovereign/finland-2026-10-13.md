*Note: Finland is a developed, high-grade euro-area sovereign. EMBIG spread is replaced by the Finnish 10Y-Bund spread; reserve-adequacy metrics are presented with the caveat that they sit within the ECB/Eurosystem framework.*

## §1 Rating Action & Outlook

| Sovereign | Date | Implied Rating | Outlook | Prior Ratings (Moody's/Fitch/S&P) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Finland | 2026-10-13 | **AA/Aa1** | Negative (S&P) / Stable (Moody's, Fitch) | Aa1 (Moody's, Stable) / AA (Fitch, Stable, downgraded from AA+ Jul-2025) / AA+ (S&P, Negative) | Affirm at post-downgrade levels — Fitch cut Finland one notch in 2025 on rising debt; the European Commission is separately weighing an Excessive Deficit Procedure. |

Finland has absorbed a genuine, data-grounded credit deterioration: Fitch downgraded to AA from AA+ on 2025-07-25, citing "high and rising government debt" with no expectation of "sufficient fiscal tightening to stabilize the government debt trajectory," and projected the debt ratio reaching 86.3% of GDP in 2025 — four points higher than 2024 and well above the 49.4% median for AA-rated sovereigns [[sources below]]. Public debt has risen by roughly 20 percentage points of GDP since 2019, more than any other euro-area country (IMF), and the European Commission announced in November 2025 that it will consider placing Finland under the EU's Excessive Deficit Procedure.

## §2 Key Rating Drivers

**Strengths**

- **Current account returning to surplus.** After running deficits, Finland's current account returned to surplus in 2025 (1.3% of GDP, European Commission forecast) and is expected to stay broadly balanced over the medium term.
- **Strong institutional quality.** Finland scores among the top globally on World Bank governance dimensions historically, with a well-regarded, transparent fiscal framework and debt office (Treasury Finland).
- **Growth inflection expected.** After near-stagnant growth (0.2% in 2025), the IMF projects a pickup to 1.5% in both 2026 and 2027, supported by private demand recovery — a genuine, if modest, improvement from the post-2023 downturn.

**Constraints**

- **Fastest debt increase in the euro area.** Public debt has risen ~20pp of GDP since 2019 — more than any other euro-area country per the IMF — reaching an estimated 86-90% of GDP in 2025 depending on source (Fitch: 86.3%; IMF baseline: approaching 90%), with the IMF's baseline showing debt still rising to nearly 90% of GDP by 2030 even under current consolidation assumptions.
- **Persistent, large fiscal deficit.** The overall deficit widened to 4.5% of GDP in 2024 and remained at a comparable ~4½% level in 2025; even with ongoing consolidation, the IMF expects the deficit to remain above 3% of GDP, missing EU thresholds and prompting the EU's November 2025 EDP consideration.
- **Weak underlying growth base.** Real GDP grew just 0.4% in 2024 and an estimated 0.2% in 2025, with stagnant construction investment and weak external demand — a structural growth deficiency that has directly driven the debt deterioration via the denominator.

## §3B Debt Trajectory

Finland's debt trajectory is the most adverse among the Nordic/AA-category eurozone sovereigns: public debt has risen approximately 20 percentage points of GDP since 2019 — more than any other country in the euro area, per the IMF — reaching an estimated 86.3% of GDP in 2025 (Fitch) and set to approach 90% under the IMF's own baseline, with debt still rising to "nearly 90 percent of GDP by 2030" even assuming fiscal consolidation "slows down from 2026." Fitch's July 2025 downgrade to AA from AA+ was explicit that it does not expect "sufficient fiscal tightening to stabilize the government debt trajectory" under current policy settings, placing Finland's debt ratio well above the 49.4% median for AA-rated sovereigns globally — a striking outlier within its own rating category.

The fiscal deficit itself — 4.5% of GDP in 2024, remaining near that level through 2025 — reflects both weak growth (0.2-0.4% real GDP growth in 2024-25) and expenditure increases that outpaced the slow recovery. The IMF recommends consolidation of roughly ½ percentage point of GDP per year until the fiscal balance closes and debt turns decisively downward, explicitly reiterating prior advice to put debt on a declining path by 2027 and effectively close the overall deficit by 2029. The European Commission's November 2025 move to consider an Excessive Deficit Procedure against Finland formalizes the urgency of this consolidation need at the EU level.

## §3C External Liquidity

Finland's external position has stabilized after a period of deficit, though it remains a secondary concern relative to the fiscal/debt trajectory. The current account returned to surplus in 2025 (1.3% of GDP, European Commission) and is expected to be "broadly balanced" over the medium term, narrowing to a forecast 0.1% of GDP in 2026 — essentially at equilibrium rather than a strong net creditor position. As a euro-area member, Finland has no independent reserve-adequacy constraint in the EM sense; FX backstop functions sit with the Eurosystem.

Gross external debt-to-GDP stood at 207-208% in 2025 into Q1 2026 (Trading Economics/CEIC), with Finland characterized as a net external debtor at 64.5% of GDP (2025) — a meaningfully weaker net external position than peers like Spain (44.2% net debt) or the Netherlands (strongly net-creditor). Banks' liabilities account for over half of Finland's gross external debt, reflecting the structure of its financial sector rather than direct sovereign exposure, but the near-balanced current account and genuine net-debtor status mean Finland has less of an external buffer to lean on than most AA-category eurozone peers if fiscal pressures intensify further.

## §8 Rating Sensitivities

**Downgrade triggers**
- EU Excessive Deficit Procedure formalized without a credible, legislated government response
- Debt/GDP exceeding the IMF's ~90%-by-2030 baseline without consolidation acceleration
- Growth recovery to the IMF's 1.5% 2026-27 baseline failing to materialize

**Upgrade triggers**
- Not meaningful near-term given the current downgrade/negative-outlook trajectory
- (Stabilizing, non-rating-moving) Consolidation delivered per the IMF's ½pp/year recommendation, closing the deficit by 2029 as advised
- (Stabilizing, non-rating-moving) Current account surplus widening back above the 2025 1.3%-of-GDP level, rebuilding external buffers

## §9 Analyst Conclusion

Finland's credit profile has deteriorated more acutely than almost any other eurozone sovereign over the past five years — a roughly 20-percentage-point rise in debt/GDP since 2019, the fastest in the euro area, has already produced a Fitch downgrade and an EU Excessive Deficit Procedure review. The silver lining is a return to current account surplus and an expected growth inflection to 1.5% by 2026-27, but the IMF's own baseline shows debt continuing to rise toward 90% of GDP by 2030 even with assumed consolidation, meaning the fiscal adjustment required — closing the deficit entirely by 2029, per IMF advice — has not yet been matched by legislated policy.

> **We assign Finland an implied rating of AA/Aa1 with a mixed Negative (S&P)/Stable Outlook, reflecting the fastest debt increase of any euro-area sovereign since 2019, an EU Excessive Deficit Procedure review now underway, and a still-unresolved gap between the fiscal consolidation needed and what has been legislated to date.**
