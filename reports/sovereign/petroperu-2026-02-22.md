# PETROPERU: QUASI-SOVEREIGN CREDIT ANALYSIS
**Petroleos del Peru S.A. (Petroperu) — IMF-DSA Adapted Framework**
**Report Date:** 2026-02-22

> *Note: Petroperu is a 100% state-owned enterprise (SOE) of Peru, not a sovereign issuer. This analysis applies the Moody's/Fitch sovereign credit note framework adapted for a quasi-sovereign SOE, incorporating both company-level financial metrics and the sovereign backstop provided by the Republic of Peru (Moody's Baa1 / S&P BBB- / Fitch BBB, all Stable).*

---

## 1. RATING ACTION & OUTLOOK

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Issuer** | Petroleos del Peru S.A. (Petroperu) |
| **Report Date** | 2026-02-22 |
| **Implied Rating** | **Caa1** (Moody's-equivalent) |
| **Outlook** | **Negative** |
| **Prior Moody's Rating** | B3 (Stable) — First-time assigned March 2025 |
| **Prior Fitch Rating** | CCC+ — Downgraded May 2024 (standalone: CC) |
| **Action** | **Downgrade** from B3 to Caa1; Outlook revised to Negative |

**Rationale for Action:** The Moody's B3 assigned in March 2025 was predicated on gradual Talara refinery ramp-up improving metrics through 2027. Since that assignment, Petroperu has accumulated a further $479M in losses (Jan–Oct 2025), cash has declined to a critical $19.6M (~$20M USD), and the government was compelled to issue an emergency restructuring decree on January 1, 2026. The structural operating inability to service debt independently, combined with heightened political uncertainty following the removal of President Boluarte in October 2025, warrants a one-notch downgrade to **Caa1** with a **Negative** outlook.

---

## 2. KEY RATING DRIVERS

### Credit Strengths

- **Sovereign backstop (Peru Baa1/BBB stable):** The Republic of Peru has injected approximately **$5.3 billion** in financing support between 2022 and 2024, demonstrating a track record of fiscal commitment to preventing a disorderly Petroperu failure. Peru's low sovereign debt (~31% of GDP) and abundant reserves (~$82–95B, ~27% of GDP) preserve fiscal space for continued support.
- **Talara refinery asset value (~$6.5B replacement cost):** The modernized Talara refinery is a strategically critical asset representing the largest industrial investment in Peru's history. Its $6.5B replacement cost and operational ramp-up (reaching positive EBITDA of $4M in Q1 2025) provide tangible collateral value and a basis for private investor interest.
- **Emergency restructuring framework (January 2026 decree):** The government's emergency decree designating ProInversion as the lead restructuring agent — with authority to split Petroperu into asset blocks and invite private management of Talara — creates the first credible operational reform pathway since the crisis onset in 2022.
- **Marginal EBITDA recovery (Q1 2025):** Petroperu reported a positive EBITDA of $4M in Q1 2025, the first positive operational result after two consecutive years of losses ($359M in 2023; $292M in 2024), suggesting Talara's ramp-up is beginning to contribute.

### Credit Constraints

- **Extreme liquidity distress:** As of October 2025, Petroperu held only **66 million soles (~$19.6M USD)** in operating cash — insufficient to cover even a single day of debt service — while supplier payables in arrears totalled **$764M** through December 2025. The company is operationally insolvent on a standalone basis.
- **Unsustainable debt burden ($5.45B total):** Total debt of approximately **$5.45B** (per S&P) against near-zero or negative EBITDA renders conventional leverage metrics (Net Debt/EBITDA) non-calculable. This debt load is the direct legacy of the Talara refinery cost overruns (final cost ~$6.5B vs. original ~$3.5B estimate).
- **Persistent operating losses:** Accumulated losses of **$479M (Jan–Oct 2025)** — following $359M (2023) and $292M (2024) — suggest structural cost issues beyond the Talara ramp-up, including high crude procurement costs, pipeline losses, and overstaffing.
- **Political uncertainty — transition risk:** President Boluarte was removed by Congress in October 2025 on "moral incapacity" grounds; interim President Jerí leads Peru into **2026 general elections**. Policy continuity for the Petroperu restructuring is not guaranteed across administrations.
- **Execution risk on restructuring:** ProInversion was given 60 days (from January 1, 2026) to deliver a private management promotion plan for Talara. Historically, Peruvian SOE concession processes have faced legal challenges, political opposition, and delays.

| FX-Denominated Debt Share (%) | ~**95%** (USD-denominated bonds) | Stable | <50% = lower rollover risk | **High risk** |

### 3B. Debt Trajectory Narrative

Petroperu's debt trajectory is structurally unsustainable without either (a) a significant debt restructuring or equity-for-debt conversion, or (b) a private management concession generating the cash flows needed to begin amortization. Under the current baseline — where Talara reaches 60–70% utilization by late 2026 — annualized EBITDA could reach $200–300M, implying Net Debt/EBITDA of 18–27x: still far above any creditworthy threshold. The traditional IMF "r-g" stabilization logic (where real interest rates must not persistently exceed growth) is inapplicable at current leverage; debt is self-reinforcing through capitalized interest and working capital borrowing. Full debt stabilization requires a private capital injection of at minimum $1.5–2.0B to break the debt feedback loop.

### 3C. External Liquidity

Petroperu's liquidity position is critically deficient: cash of $19.6M is dwarfed by $764M in supplier arrears and annual coupon obligations on $5.45B in bonds. All meaningful liquidity is sovereign-provided — the company lacks independent access to capital markets at viable rates following Fitch's CCC+ downgrade. Rollover risk is effectively sovereign risk; without continued government guarantee issuances or direct transfers, a technical default on supplier obligations has already materialized, and bond default risk is material on a standalone basis.

## 4. WILLINGNESS TO PAY

| Dimension | Score (1–5) | Commentary |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Government Commitment to SOE Support | **3** | ~$5.3B injected 2022–2024 demonstrates high historical willingness; the January 2026 decree shifts model from direct support to privatization, which could reduce unconditional fiscal backstop |
| Restructuring Credibility & Execution | **2** | Emergency decree is positive signal, but ProInversion's 60-day plan timeline, combined with an interim government and pre-election Congress, creates high implementation risk |
| Debt Payment Track Record | **3** | No formal bond default to date; government guarantees have backstopped all coupon service; supplier arrears ($764M) represent a selective soft default to trade creditors |
| Petroperu Institutional Governance | **2** | History of political appointees to the board, alleged mismanagement, cost overruns on Talara doubling original estimates, and multi-year losses indicate weak internal governance; WGI for Peru overall is moderate (~40th percentile in Control of Corruption) |

**Synthesis:** Petroperu's willingness-to-pay framework is entirely derivative of sovereign willingness. The Peruvian state has demonstrated consistent — if costly — commitment to preventing an outright default, but the January 2026 restructuring shift signals a move away from unconditional support toward a managed privatization. Under an interim government with a new president awaiting 2026 elections, policy continuity for the restructuring is uncertain. The board governance deficit and mismanagement track record remain structurally problematic and will weigh on investor confidence through any concession process.

**Positioning:** Petroperu is most analogous to PEMEX in its structural dependency on sovereign support and inability to service debt independently, but at a fraction of the scale and with a weaker sovereign backstop (Baa1 vs. Baa2 previously for Mexico). Unlike PEMEX, which benefits from enormous oil production scale (~$18B EBITDA), Petroperu's operational base is too small relative to its debt for standalone creditworthiness to emerge without debt reduction. ENAP (Chile) demonstrates that well-governed Latin American energy SOEs can achieve strong standalone profiles — but this requires a decade of sustained operational discipline that Petroperu has yet to demonstrate. Relative to YPF, Petroperu benefits from a stronger sovereign backdrop, but YPF's post-restructuring path is more advanced.

## 7. ESG CONSIDERATIONS

| ESG Factor | Assessment |
|------------|------------|
| **E – Environmental** | Petroperu operates legacy pipelines in the Amazon basin with a documented history of oil spills. As of 2024, financial distress raised concerns that the company lacked resources for environmental remediation obligations — creating a material contingent liability. The Talara refinery upgrade improves Peru's refining emissions profile but does not address upstream spill risk. Climate vulnerability increases contingent fiscal liability risk for the sovereign. |
| **S – Social** | Petroperu's pipeline spills have impacted indigenous Amazonian communities, creating ongoing social license disputes and litigation exposure. Financial distress-driven cuts to remediation spending compound ESG-S risks. At the sovereign level, Peru's Gini coefficient (~43, 2023) reflects moderate inequality; social protests have historically been linked to mining and energy sector grievances. |
| **G – Governance** | Peru's WGI scores are moderate: approximately 40th percentile in Government Effectiveness and ~35th percentile in Control of Corruption (2023 data). Petroperu's board has been dominated by political appointees, contributing to mismanagement and the Talara cost overruns that doubled original estimates. The corporate governance deficit is the primary driver of the standalone credit weakness and is a direct causal factor in the current distress. A privatization framework could improve governance but requires sustained implementation. |

## 8. RATING SENSITIVITIES

### Factors that could lead to a further downgrade (additional negative pressure):
- **Failure to secure private Talara concession by Q4 2026:** If ProInversion's process stalls, cash depletion will force a bond default (Ca territory)
- **Government capital support withdrawn or materially reduced** under new post-2026 election administration
- **Bond coupon missed or payment rescheduled** — technical default would trigger immediate Ca/C action
- **Continued negative EBITDA beyond Q2 2026:** If Talara utilization fails to reach 60%+, EBITDA will remain insufficient to service debt

### Factors that could lead to an upgrade (positive pressure):
- **Private management concession executed for Talara** with credible private partner committing fresh capital ≥$1B
- **EBITDA sustained above $300M annualized** for two consecutive quarters, demonstrating operational viability
- **Sovereign debt-for-equity conversion ≥$2B,** materially reducing total financial obligations
- **Government increases direct capital support** under a structured recapitalization plan endorsed by Congress

---

## 9. ANALYST CONCLUSION

Petroperu sits at the intersection of two overlapping crises: a deeply insolvent balance sheet carrying $5.45B in debt against near-zero operational cash generation, and a sovereign political environment defined by institutional instability, an interim government, and an imminent general election. The January 2026 emergency restructuring decree represents a genuine policy inflection — the government has acknowledged it cannot continue unconditional support and has invoked market mechanisms (ProInversion, private concessions) to stabilize the entity. However, Peru's history with SOE reforms cautions against assuming execution will be smooth: legal challenges, political populism around the Talara asset, and a compressed timeline (ProInversion's 60-day mandate) create substantial downside risk. The Q1 2025 EBITDA turning marginally positive is an operationally encouraging signal, but at $4M against $5.45B in debt, it is economically irrelevant as a de-leveraging mechanism. Peru's sovereign strength — Baa1, 31% debt/GDP, $82–95B in reserves — remains the ultimate safety net, but the fiscal cost of continued unconditional support is eroding political viability.

> **We assign Petroperu an implied rating of Caa1 with a Negative outlook, reflecting an extreme liquidity position ($19.6M cash against $5.45B debt), structural operating loss history, and high execution risk on the January 2026 restructuring decree — partially offset by Peru's investment-grade sovereign backstop and the strategic value of the Talara refinery as a privatization asset.**
