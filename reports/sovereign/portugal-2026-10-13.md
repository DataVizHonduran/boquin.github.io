## §1 Rating Action & Outlook

| Sovereign | Date | Implied Rating | Outlook | Prior Ratings (Moody's/Fitch/S&P) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Portugal | 2026-10-13 | **A+/A3** | Positive | A3 (Moody's, Stable) / A+ (Fitch, Stable, upgraded Sep-2026 from A) / A+ (S&P, Positive) | Affirm with positive momentum — Fitch's September 2026 upgrade to A+ is Portugal's best rating in 15 years. |

Portugal has delivered one of the clearest sustained sovereign-credit turnarounds in the eurozone. Fitch upgraded to A+ from A on 2026-09-05 — Portugal's best Fitch rating since 2011 — citing "years of falling public debt and stronger-than-expected growth," while S&P maintains a Positive outlook on its A+ rating [[sources below]]. The budget recorded a surplus for the third consecutive year in 2025, bringing public debt to just below 90% of GDP — down from a 2020 peak of 134% — even as growth continued to outpace the euro-area average. The IMF's 2026 Article IV (concluded 2026-06-17) frames the core policy challenge not as crisis management but as how to lock in these gains while raising chronically low productivity and investment.

## §2 Key Rating Drivers

**Strengths**

- **Sustained fiscal surpluses.** Portugal posted its third consecutive budget surplus in 2025, a multi-year track record — not a one-off — that Fitch explicitly cited as the basis for its September 2026 upgrade.
- **Dramatic debt reduction.** Public debt has fallen from a 2020 peak of 134% of GDP to just below 90% in 2025 (with some sources citing 93.5%, see caveat below), a decline the IMF and rating agencies both describe as "impressive" and durable rather than cyclical.
- **Growth outperformance versus euro-area average.** Real GDP growth of ~1.9-2.0% in 2025, moderating only slightly to 1.8-2.3% depending on source through 2026-27, continues to run ahead of the broader euro-area average.

**Constraints**

- **Still-elevated absolute debt level.** Even after the dramatic improvement, debt near 87-90% of GDP remains well above the eurozone's 60% Maastricht reference and leaves limited buffer against future shocks.
- **Low productivity and living standards gap.** The IMF explicitly flags that living standards remain well below the EU average and that higher medium-term growth requires productivity-enhancing reforms — innovation support, streamlined bureaucracy, product/labor market reforms, and continued education improvement — that have not yet been fully delivered.
- **Housing affordability and aging pressures.** Fast-rising housing prices are weighing heavily on households (an IMF-flagged social/structural risk), while aging-related pension and healthcare cost pressures are an explicit medium-term fiscal risk the IMF urges be addressed alongside continued debt reduction.

## §3B Debt Trajectory

Portugal's debt trajectory is the standout positive story among the ten largest eurozone sovereigns covered in this series. From a 2020 peak of 134% of GDP, public debt has fallen to just below 90% in 2025 — a reduction the IMF's 2026 Article IV describes in explicitly positive terms, driven by three consecutive years of budget surplus alongside moderate nominal growth. Separate projection sets show debt continuing to decline to roughly 82.9% of GDP by 2028, supported by the "maintenance of primary surpluses and moderate nominal growth" (per rating-agency commentary accompanying the September 2026 Fitch upgrade). This is a genuinely sustained, multi-year consolidation rather than a one-off improvement, and both the IMF and rating agencies treat it as durable.

The IMF's forward-looking policy advice is notably different in character from peers like Belgium, France, or Finland: rather than urging Portugal to find new consolidation measures to stabilize an still-rising debt path, the Fund urges Portugal to "lock in debt gains" while "reorienting public spending toward higher investment through tax reform, improved efficiency of spending, and measures to contain spending pressures from aging." In other words, the near-term sustainability question is largely resolved; the remaining debt-sustainability risk is now about using the improved fiscal space wisely rather than finding a way out of a worsening trajectory.

## §3C External Liquidity

Portugal's external position has improved markedly and is now a genuine strength rather than a legacy vulnerability from the 2011-2014 program era. The current account balance is forecast at 1.0% of GDP in 2025, narrowing toward near-balance at 0.1% in 2026 (European Commission) as import growth outpaces exports and energy-price effects only partially reverse — a moderating but not distressed position. As a euro-area member, Portugal has no independent reserve-adequacy constraint in the EM sense; FX backstop functions sit with the Eurosystem.

External debt stood at 44.5% of GDP in 2024 — the lowest level since the beginning of 2005 — a dramatic structural improvement from the pre-2011-crisis era, even as gross external debt in absolute euro terms reached €476.5 billion in Q2 2026 reflecting overall economic growth. This is one of the strongest relative external-debt improvements among the ten largest eurozone sovereigns and directly supports the multi-agency upgrade cycle; Portugal's 2026 Financial Sector Assessment Program (the first in 20 years) separately found the financial system "broadly resilient" with contained risks.

## §8 Rating Sensitivities

**Downgrade triggers**
- Reversal of the three-year primary surplus trend, e.g. via pre-election fiscal loosening
- Aging-related pension/healthcare costs materializing faster than current IMF projections
- Productivity reforms stalling, leaving Portugal's EU living-standards gap unaddressed and growth vulnerable to external shocks

**Upgrade triggers**
- Continued primary surpluses sustaining the debt decline toward the IMF's ~83%-by-2028 path or better
- Delivery of productivity-enhancing structural reforms (innovation, labor/product markets, education) lifting medium-term growth
- Debt/GDP approaching the 60% Maastricht reference ahead of current trajectory, opening room for further upgrades

## §9 Analyst Conclusion

Portugal stands alongside Spain as one of the clearest positive sovereign-credit stories in the eurozone: three consecutive years of budget surplus have driven debt down from a 134%-of-GDP peak in 2020 to just below 90% in 2025, culminating in Fitch's September 2026 upgrade to Portugal's best rating in 15 years. The remaining credit tasks are no longer about crisis avoidance but about using hard-won fiscal space wisely — raising chronically low productivity, addressing a housing-affordability crisis, and managing aging-related cost pressures — while preserving the multi-year political commitment to fiscal discipline that has defined this turnaround.

> **We assign Portugal an implied rating of A+/A3 with a Positive Outlook, reflecting a sustained, multi-year fiscal turnaround that has driven debt from a 2020 peak of 134% of GDP to below 90%, with the key forward risk being whether productivity-enhancing reforms can be delivered to convert this fiscal space into durable medium-term growth.**
