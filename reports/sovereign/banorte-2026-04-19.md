## 2. KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths:**
- **Dominant domestic franchise:** Mexico's largest domestically-owned bank; #2 in total loan portfolio (14.9% market share, Dec 2024) behind only BBVA México; top-3 in assets (~MXN 1.5 trillion / ~$84B), net income, and deposits — providing critical mass for funding cost advantage and cross-sell.
- **Best-in-class profitability:** FY2025 ROE of **29.1%** and ROA of **2.5%** are among the highest for investment-grade LatAm banks; Q4 2025 ROE reached **31.5%**, demonstrating resilience through Mexico's rate-cutting cycle; NIM of **6.6%** (FY2025) expanded +13bps despite 300bps of Banxico cuts.
- **Deep capitalization buffer:** CET1 of **14.82%** and CAR of **22.31%** (Sep 2025) represent a 10.91 percentage point buffer over the Systemically Important Financial Institution minimum of 11.40%; LCR of **159.92%** and NSFR of **132.74%** confirm robust liquidity well in excess of Basel III thresholds.
- **Pristine asset quality relative to peers:** NPL ratio of **0.9–1.37%** (range across 2025 quarters, with the high end driven by an isolated commercial case); Cost of Risk of **2.01%** remains controlled; diversified revenue from insurance (GNP Seguros) and pension (Afore XXI Banorte) reduces credit cycle sensitivity.

**Credit Constraints:**
- **Sovereign ceiling compression:** Moody's changed Banorte's outlook to Negative in November 2024 following Mexico's sovereign outlook revision; any further Mexico sovereign downgrade would automatically flow through to Banorte's issuer ratings, given the bank's structural nexus with the sovereign (government client, peso-currency operations, regulatory environment).
- **Macro headwinds in 2026:** Mexico GDP growth projected at only 1.2–1.6% (BBVA Research, Vanguard) — significantly below potential — amid USMCA/tariff uncertainty; business insolvencies are expected to peak in 2026; Fitch notes credit growth slowdown ahead of USMCA review; government fiscal consolidation reduces public-sector lending pipeline.
- **Government lending concentration:** Surge in government loans at year-end 2025 compressed portfolio-level margins; heavy reliance on government clients introduces concentration risk and political-cycle exposure to fiscal spending decisions.
- **Rate-cycle NIM pressure:** Banxico accumulated 300bps in cuts during 2025 with further reductions likely in 2026; NIM will face incremental pressure as asset repricing outpaces liability repricing, particularly in the government and corporate segments.
- **Fintech and digital competition:** BBVA México (backed by global parent) and neobank entrants are intensifying competition for deposits and consumer lending — Banorte's digital franchise (Banorte Digital app) is competitive but faces resource constraints vs. foreign-parent peers.

### 3B. Earnings & Capitalization Narrative

Banorte's FY2025 performance was resilient against a challenging backdrop: Banxico cut rates by 300bps during 2025, yet NIM expanded 13bps to 6.6% through active balance-sheet sensitivity management and a deliberate shift toward higher-margin consumer and SME loans. ROE of 29.1% places Banorte in the top decile globally for investment-grade banks. Capitalization at 22.31% CAR provides a 10.91pp buffer over regulatory minimums, enabling dividend sustainability and loan growth without external capital issuance. The primary capitalization risk is a sharp deterioration in Mexico's macroeconomic environment driving NPL normalization — under a moderate stress scenario with NPLs rising to 3–4%, the bank would still maintain CET1 well above minimum thresholds.

### 3C. Funding & Liquidity

LCR of 159.92% and NSFR of 132.74% confirm funding resilience substantially above Basel III minimums. A loan-to-deposit ratio of ~97–98% indicates a broadly self-funded balance sheet with modest reliance on wholesale markets. Banorte's core retail and SME deposit franchise — deepened by the Banorte Digital app and its 3rd-largest deposit market position — provides stable low-cost funding relative to foreign-owned peers. Rollover risk is contained: the bank does not face near-term maturity cliffs, and Afore XXI Banorte's long-duration liability matching reduces overall group duration mismatch.

The residual constraint is Mexico's macro and fiscal environment: if government fiscal consolidation sharply reduces public-sector lending volumes, Banorte's profitability would be pressured, though not to ratings-threatening levels at current capitalization buffers.

## 8. RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Factors that could lead to a downgrade (negative pressure):**
- Mexico sovereign downgraded by Moody's to Baa3 or below — automatic Banorte follow-through given ceiling mechanics
- NPL ratio rises sustainably above 3.5% for two consecutive quarters (cost-of-risk deterioration to >3.5%)
- CET1 ratio declines toward 11% on a sustained basis due to loan losses or dividend over-extraction
- NIM compression below 5.5% on a durable basis, driving ROE below 18% and undermining internal capital generation
- Material CNBV regulatory action or governance event impairing franchise trust

**Factors that could lead to an upgrade (positive pressure):**
- Mexico sovereign upgraded — primary and most impactful catalyst; no organic upgrade path while sovereign is Baa2/Negative
- Sustained ROE above 25% with NPL below 1.5% for 4+ consecutive quarters, demonstrating through-cycle resilience
- CET1 expansion above 16% with concurrent reduction in government lending concentration
- Formal credit enhancement via multilateral guarantee or cross-border strategic partnership elevating parent support assumption

## 9. ANALYST CONCLUSION

Grupo Financiero Banorte presents the clearest case of rating-to-fundamental mismatch in the Mexican banking sector: the bank's standalone financial profile — 29.1% ROE, 14.82% CET1, sub-1.4% NPL, and NIM expansion through a 300bps rate-cutting cycle — is consistent with a Baa1 or even A3 rating for an EM bank. The binding constraint is Mexico's sovereign ceiling (Baa2/Negative at Moody's), which caps Banorte and aligns all major agency outlooks in the negative direction despite no bank-specific deterioration in sight. The 2026 outlook is cautiously stable for Banorte's fundamentals: loan growth will decelerate (Fitch projects a credit slowdown), NIM will face modest compression as Banxico continues easing, and USMCA uncertainty will dampen corporate capex lending demand. None of these headwinds threaten capital adequacy or debt service capacity given current buffers. The single critical watchpoint is Mexico's sovereign fiscal trajectory: if the Claudia Sheinbaum administration fails to deliver credible fiscal consolidation by mid-2026, a Moody's downgrade to Baa3 — and automatic Banorte follow-through — becomes the baseline, not the downside.

> **We assign Grupo Financiero Banorte an implied rating of Baa2/BBB with a Negative Outlook, reflecting Mexico's best domestically-owned bank whose investment-grade credit quality is constrained entirely by sovereign ceiling mechanics, not by any fundamental weakness in its franchise, capital, or asset quality.**
