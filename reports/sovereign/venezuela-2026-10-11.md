# Venezuela — Sovereign Credit Note | IMF-DSA Framework | Moody's/Fitch Style | Report Date: 2026-10-11

*Note: Venezuela is an active, unresolved restructuring case undergoing simultaneous political transition. Moody's and Fitch have withdrawn ratings (sanctions-driven data unavailability); S&P carries a Selective Default designation dating to 2017. This note assigns a forward-looking implied rating consistent with sovereign-in-restructuring convention, pending completion of the DSA and exchange terms.*

## §1 RATING ACTION & OUTLOOK

| Sovereign | Date | Implied Rating | Outlook | Prior Ratings (Moody's/Fitch/S&P) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Venezuela | 2026-10-11 | **CCC** (post-default, pre-reorg) | Positive | WR / WD (withdrawn, sanctions) / SD | **Affirm at distressed level, Outlook revised to Positive** — reflects the Jan 3, 2026 capture of Nicolás Maduro, the Jan 5, 2026 swearing-in of interim President Delcy Rodríguez, partial US sanctions relief (Mar 2026), resumed IMF relations (Apr 16, 2026), and the May 13, 2026 launch of a comprehensive ~$219bn debt restructuring — set against bonds still in default since 2017, an unpublished DSA now a full quarter overdue, and oil output near historic lows. |

Venezuela remains in payment default on $60bn+ of external bonds since November 2017, and S&P's SD rating has not been revised; Moody's and Fitch dropped coverage once US sanctions cut off reliable data access ([SPGlobal](https://www.spglobal.com/marketintelligence/en/news-insights/trending/z8iPJqnqAZ9hJoePSnWLow2); [cbonds](https://cbonds.com/news/1457055/)). The 2026 political rupture is the first credible catalyst for credit repair in a decade: Washington restored diplomatic relations and lifted sanctions on the Central Bank of Venezuela in March 2026, and a US Department of Energy-brokered oil-for-debt arrangement (announced Jan 7, 2026) is the mechanism markets are pricing as the eventual source of restructuring cash flow. EMBIG spreads nearly halved on the capture news alone ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/latam-country-risk-embi-q1-2026/)). The rating stays capped in deep distress until the DSA, haircut terms, and a sanctions-compliant payment mechanism are confirmed.

## §2 KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths**

- **Political catalyst for sanctions relief.** Maduro's Jan 3, 2026 capture and the installation of a Washington-aligned interim government under Delcy Rodríguez removed the single largest impediment to creditor engagement — US recognition, restored diplomatic ties, and BCV sanctions relief all followed within 90 days ([CFR](https://www.cfr.org/articles/guide-maduros-capture-and-venezuelas-uncertain-future); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-03/delcy-rodriguez-maduro-leadership-team-assert-control-after-capture)).
- **Hydrocarbon reserve base intact.** Proven reserves remain the world's largest; a DOE-brokered oil-for-debt channel (Jan 7, 2026) offers a credible, sanctions-exempt path to hard-currency generation even with output depressed ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sm594)).
- **Formal restructuring process now launched.** The May 13, 2026 announcement of a $219bn ($159bn external / $60bn domestic) comprehensive restructuring, advised by Centerview Partners under stated principles of sustainability, comprehensiveness, transparency and speed, is the first structured creditor process since the 2017 default ([Centerview](https://www.centerviewpartners.com/docs/20260513_BRV_Debt_Restructuring_Press_Release.pdf)).

**Credit Constraints**

- **Oil output near three-decade lows.** Production fell to ~800,000–934,000 bpd in Nov–Dec 2025, under a third of early-2000s levels, leaving minimal organic export capacity to fund any restructuring coupon ([AA.com.tr](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/factbox-venezuela-s-oil-wealth-reserves-output-and-exports/3778476)).
- **DSA and macro framework overdue.** The framework and Centerview-authored DSA promised for June 2026 remained unpublished as of early September 2026 — a full quarter's slippage that delays any exchange timeline and keeps terms (haircut size, instruments, treatment of arrears) unknown ([DevTech](https://devtechsys.com/insights/2026/06/03/venezuela-stabilization-under-stress/)).
- **Hyperinflation undermines fiscal and institutional credibility.** CPI hit 475% for full-year 2025 — the world's highest — with 2026 prints oscillating near 534–576% y/y; this corrodes tax-base real value, pricing coordination, and any pre-restructuring primary balance signal ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-06/central-bank-says-venezuela-inflation-soared-to-475-then-cooled)).

## §3B DEBT TRAJECTORY

Venezuela's public debt stock is reported at roughly $219bn as of the May 2026 restructuring launch ($159bn external, $60bn domestic), against IMF-implied 2026 nominal GDP of only ~$111bn — a debt/GDP ratio in the 180%–200%+ range by IMF-consistent methodology, or as high as 308.7% on Trading Economics' broader definition for 2025 ([Modern Diplomacy](https://moderndiplomacy.eu/2026/01/04/venezuelas-debt-storm-the-global-race-to-collect/); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/venezuela/government-debt-to-gdp)). No IMF Article IV consultation has been completed since 2004 — delayed 217+ months as of January 2025 — so the debt stock, amortization profile, and currency composition rest on externally-compiled estimates (Centerview, Harvard HKS, bondholder trackers) rather than an IMF-validated DSA ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2025/02/06/pr-2531-venezuela-imf-executive-board-holds-informal-briefing)).

The growth-interest-primary-balance dynamic is effectively suspended: Venezuela has made no coupon or principal payments on ~$60bn of defaulted bonds since November 2017, so the conventional debt-dynamics identity (r-g differential, primary balance) is not operative until an exchange resets the stock and schedule. The IMF has explicitly declined to co-author the restructuring DSA — a point one investor publicly flagged, calling instead for an IMF-orchestrated process on an audited data perimeter ([GFMag](https://gfmag.com/news/imf-wont-participate-in-venezuela-debt-restructuring/)). That absence of a Fund-anchored framework, plus the slippage of Centerview's own June 2026 deadline into Q3 2026 without publication, is the principal source of near-term rollover and restructuring-execution risk: terms (haircut depth, instrument mix, treatment of PDVSA claims and bilateral arrears to China/Russia) remain undefined, and 100% of the external debt stock carries FX/legal-jurisdiction exposure to US courts and sanctions-compliance constraints.

## §3C EXTERNAL LIQUIDITY

Reserve Adequacy: BCV reserves stand at $9.8bn, but roughly 64% is held as physical gold bars in Germany — a composition that is balance-sheet-supportive but operationally illiquid for near-term external debt service or import financing, and well below any IMF ARA metric threshold for a economy this distressed ([Wikipedia/BCV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Venezuela)). With no market access and bonds in default, there is no conventional gross external financing requirement to roll — the practical liquidity question is whether oil-for-debt proceeds (the Jan 7, 2026 DOE arrangement) can be scaled into a sustained, sanctions-compliant hard-currency channel ahead of any restructuring closing.

Current Account & Sudden-Stop Risk: The current account ran an estimated $3.7bn surplus in 2025, continuing into 2026 (+$2.45bn reported for March 2026), but this surplus is itself a symptom of distress — it reflects suppressed imports and zero external debt service rather than competitive export strength. Oil production at ~800,000–934,000 bpd (vs. >3mn bpd historical peak) caps the surplus's durability once any restructuring reinstates coupon payments, and the economy remains acutely exposed to a reversal in the fragile US sanctions-relief trajectory ([AA.com.tr](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/factbox-venezuela-s-oil-wealth-reserves-output-and-exports/3778476); [IMF/Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/20/imf-warns-venezuelas-economy-and-humanitarian-situation-is-quite-fragile)).

## §8 RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Downgrade Triggers**
- DSA and macroeconomic framework remain unpublished beyond 2026, signaling restructuring paralysis.
- Reimposition of US sanctions on the Rodríguez government or the Central Bank of Venezuela.
- Oil output fails to recover from ~800k–934k bpd range, eliminating restructuring-relevant cash flow.

**Upgrade Triggers**
- Publication of an IMF-consistent DSA with defined haircut terms and creditor buy-in (>66% participation threshold typical of prior EM exchanges).
- Resumption of any coupon/principal payment on restructured instruments.
- Formal IMF financing arrangement (SBA/EFF) following the April 2026 resumption of relations.

## §9 ANALYST CONCLUSION

Venezuela sits at a genuine inflection point rather than a steady-state credit: the January 2026 capture of Nicolás Maduro and the swift US-aligned transition under interim President Delcy Rodríguez removed a decade-long sanctions deadlock, but converted a frozen default into an active, unresolved restructuring of roughly $219bn in public debt with no published DSA, no resumed payments, and oil output still near historic lows. Every forward-looking catalyst — sanctions relief, IMF re-engagement, the oil-for-debt channel — is real but unconsummated, while every backward-looking metric (debt/GDP, EMBIG spread, inflation, governance scores) remains at or near worst-in-class.

> **We assign Venezuela an implied rating of CCC with a Positive Outlook, reflecting an unprecedented but still-unconfirmed political and sanctions catalyst for credit repair, offset by a nine-year-old bond default, an overdue debt sustainability analysis, and oil production too depressed to self-fund any near-term restructuring settlement.**
