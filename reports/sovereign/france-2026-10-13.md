*Note: France is a developed, high-grade euro-area sovereign. EMBIG spread is replaced by the OAT-Bund spread, the standard euro-area risk-premium metric; reserve-adequacy metrics are presented with the caveat that they sit within the ECB/Eurosystem framework.*

## §1 Rating Action & Outlook

| Sovereign | Date | Implied Rating | Outlook | Prior Ratings (Moody's/Fitch/S&P) | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| France | 2026-10-13 | **A+/A1** | Negative | Aa3 (Moody's, Negative) / A+ (Fitch, Stable) / A+ (S&P, Stable) | Affirm at current levels — rating already migrated down one-to-two notches since late 2025; risk skewed further negative on political fragmentation and stalled consolidation. |

France's three major agencies now sit clustered in the A-category after a wave of downgrades: Moody's cut to Aa3 with a Negative outlook (2025-10-24, reaffirmed 2026-04-10), while Fitch and S&P both moved to A+ Stable in late 2025 [[sources below]]. The deficit narrowed to 5.1% of GDP in 2025 — beating the initial budget target — but IMF, OECD and European Commission all project debt/GDP climbing toward 118–120% by 2026 and beyond, with the IMF's 2026 Article IV (concluded 2026-07-17) flagging that France's planned consolidation path, even if delivered, would still miss the EU's 2029 Excessive Deficit Procedure exit target. A fragmented parliament, recurring government turnover, and an approaching electoral cycle leave execution risk firmly to the downside.

## §2 Key Rating Drivers

**Strengths**

- **Deficit reduction beat expectations in 2025.** The fiscal deficit fell to 5.1% of GDP in 2025, below the government's initial target, reflecting proactive in-year spending management (IMF, 2026 Article IV Concluding Statement, 2026-05-21).
- **Deep, liquid government bond market.** Despite the sell-off, France retains one of the largest and most liquid sovereign bond markets in the euro area, with the Agence France Trésor able to continue placing large OAT volumes even at wider spreads.
- **Diversified, large economy.** A broad industrial, services, and agricultural base and EU/euro-area membership support structural resilience that pure fiscal metrics understate.

**Constraints**

- **High and rising debt burden.** Gross debt is projected by the IMF to rise from 116.0% of GDP (2025) to 118.4% (2026) and 120.5% (2027), among the highest in the euro area outside Italy and Greece.
- **Political fragmentation blocking consolidation.** Moody's cited "increased risk that political fragmentation will impair France's legislative institutions" as the basis for its negative outlook (2025-10-24); the IMF's 2026 Article IV judges the baseline consolidation path insufficient to exit the EU's Excessive Deficit Procedure by the 2029 target.
- **Widening sovereign risk premium.** The OAT-Bund 10-year spread widened to 152bps — the largest premium since 2011 — with the OAT yield itself briefly exceeding 5% before settling near 4.75–4.8% (Trading Economics, 2026).

## §3B Debt Trajectory

France's debt/GDP path is on a clear upward trend with no near-term stabilization under current policy. IMF WEO data show gross debt rising from 116.0% of GDP in 2025 to 118.4% in 2026 and 120.5% in 2027; the IMF's own staff baseline for the 2026 Article IV notes that even the government's planned consolidation during 2027-29 would only meet "minimum EU requirements" and relies on a less durable mix of revenue measures and spending cuts than originally envisaged, leaving the debt-stabilizing primary balance unreached until 2027 at the earliest (IMF, 2026-05-21 Concluding Statement). The deficit improved to 5.1% of GDP in 2025 versus the initial budget target, but Fitch separately projects the deficit widening again to 5.2% in 2026 and 5.5% in 2027 — divergent paths that underscore the forecast uncertainty tied to France's unstable governing coalitions.

The amortization and funding profile itself is not the primary risk — OATs are almost entirely euro-denominated, long-dated, and placed through a well-established primary dealer network — but the *cost* of rolling that debt has risen sharply: the OAT-Bund spread reached 152bps in 2026, the widest since the 2011 euro-area crisis, and the 10-year OAT yield briefly topped 5% (Trading Economics, 2026) before retreating to ~4.75-4.8%. With a growth-interest-rate gap that has turned unfavorable (nominal growth near 2-3% against OAT yields near 4.8%), debt dynamics are now adverse on both the numerator (primary deficits) and the growth-interest differential, a combination the IMF explicitly flags as insufficient to meet the EU's 2029 Excessive Deficit Procedure exit absent stronger political commitment.

## §3C External Liquidity

France's external position is a secondary risk relative to the fiscal story but has deteriorated. The current account swung from a small surplus in late 2024 to deficits through 2025-2026, running at roughly -€12.0 billion over the twelve months to July 2026, an improvement from -€18.2bn a year earlier but still negative (Banque de France balance-of-payments releases, 2026). As a euro-area member, France has no independent reserve-adequacy constraint — the relevant backstop sits with the Eurosystem — and the euro's reserve-currency status means there is no classic sudden-stop channel of the EM type.

Gross external debt reached an all-time high of €8.03 trillion in Q1 2026 (Trading Economics), a whole-economy figure dominated by banking-sector cross-border claims rather than sovereign external borrowing; France's sovereign debt is placed almost entirely in euros to a broad global investor base. The practical external-liquidity risk channel for France runs through investor sentiment and spread widening (as seen in the OAT-Bund move) rather than a classical balance-of-payments or reserves crisis.

## §8 Rating Sensitivities

**Downgrade triggers**
- Failure to pass a credible 2027 budget with growth-friendly, expenditure-led consolidation
- Debt/GDP trajectory exceeding IMF baseline (120.5% by 2027) without a credible path back toward EU Excessive Deficit Procedure exit
- Further sustained widening of the OAT-Bund spread beyond ~175-200bps

**Upgrade triggers**
- Political stabilization enabling multi-year fiscal consolidation legislation
- Debt-stabilizing primary balance achieved ahead of the 2027 IMF baseline estimate
- Narrowing of OAT-Bund spread back toward pre-2025 norms (~50-80bps) on a sustained basis

## §9 Analyst Conclusion

France's credit profile has deteriorated materially over the past twelve months, driven not by a single fiscal shock but by the compounding effect of political fragmentation on an already-high and rising debt stock; the 2025 deficit beat (5.1% of GDP) is a positive data point but does not alter the IMF's assessment that the planned medium-term consolidation path falls short of what is needed to exit the EU's Excessive Deficit Procedure by 2029. Market pricing — a 152bp OAT-Bund spread, the widest since 2011 — has moved ahead of the rating agencies' own one-notch migrations, suggesting the rating path remains more likely to move further down than to stabilize absent a credible multi-year budget.

> **We assign France an implied rating of A+/Aa3 with a Negative Outlook, reflecting a high and rising debt burden compounded by persistent political fragmentation that has repeatedly undermined credible fiscal consolidation, with execution of a durable multi-year budget the key swing factor for the rating path.**
