**IMF-DSA Framework Analysis | Moody's/Fitch Style**
**Report Date: 2026-02-21**

## 1. RATING ACTION & OUTLOOK

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Sovereign** | Republic of Türkiye |
| **Report Date** | 2026-02-21 |
| **Implied Rating** | **Ba3** |
| **Outlook** | **Positive** |
| **Prior Rating** | Moody's: Ba3/Stable (upgraded Jul 2025); Fitch: BB-/Positive Outlook (Jan 2026); S&P: BB-/Stable |
| **Action** | **Affirm Ba3 / Revise Outlook to Positive** |

> **Rationale:** Turkey's credit trajectory has undergone one of the most dramatic reversals in emerging market history over 2023–2026. Finance Minister Şimşek's orthodox policy program — reintroducing positive real interest rates, rebuilding FX reserves from near-zero to over US$200bn gross, and compressing the current account deficit from 5.4% to under 1% of GDP — has directly supported Moody's July 2025 upgrade to Ba3 and Fitch's January 2026 Positive Outlook revision. Core vulnerabilities persist: inflation at 30.65% (January 2026) remains the highest in the OECD, the TCMB policy rate at 38% reflects still-entrenched inflation expectations, governance scores have deteriorated to the 32nd WGI percentile, and the fiscal deficit of ~4.3% GDP in 2025 requires further consolidation. The Positive Outlook reflects conviction that Şimşek's orthodoxy is durable and that the disinflation path will support a return to investment grade over a 2–3 year horizon.

---

## 2. KEY RATING DRIVERS

**Credit Strengths:**
- **Extraordinary macro rebalancing:** Turkey's current account deficit narrowed from a crisis-level 5.4% of GDP in 2023 to just **0.8% in 2024**, with 2025 expected to remain modest; the underlying current account (excluding gold) achieved surplus in 2024, reflecting a genuine structural adjustment rather than a cyclical compression.
- **Reserve rebuilding:** Gross FX reserves more than doubled to **over US$200bn**, with net reserves recovering to approximately US$80bn — a balance sheet improvement of roughly US$260–265bn from the 2023 nadir when net reserves were deeply negative; this transformation dramatically reduces external liquidity risk.
- **Ultra-low sovereign debt:** At approximately **24–25% of GDP**, Turkey's government debt ratio is the second-lowest in the Middle East and among the lowest in EM; the debt stock provides exceptional fiscal headroom and renders debt sustainability a secondary concern relative to inflation and external dynamics.
- **Growth recovery:** IMF projects **4.2% GDP growth in 2026** (World Bank: 3.7%), reflecting a rebound from the policy-induced slowdown of 2024; improved real wages, resilient domestic demand, and easing credit conditions support the recovery.

**Credit Constraints:**
- **Inflation remains the dominant credit risk:** At **30.65% year-on-year** (January 2026), Turkey's inflation — while declining from the 64–65% peak of 2022–2023 — is still the highest in the OECD and far above the TCMB's own targets; IMF projects end-2026 inflation at ~22%, and entrenched inflation expectations (requiring the TCMB rate to hold at 38%) constrain monetary easing and real income recovery.
- **Governance deterioration:** Fitch notes that Turkey's governance ranking has fallen to the **32nd WGI percentile**, reflecting political centralization, reduced judicial independence, and legal unpredictability that create material investment climate risks and constrain private sector-led growth.
- **Fiscal consolidation insufficient:** The general government deficit is projected at **4.3% of GDP in 2025** (improving to ~3.6% in 2026), partially driven by earthquake reconstruction expenditures; the primary balance remains near zero rather than in the surplus required to accelerate debt reduction, limiting fiscal credibility improvement.
- **TCMB credibility remains work-in-progress:** Despite the Şimşek orthodoxy restoration, the TCMB's track record of policy U-turns under political pressure (2021–2023 interest rate cuts despite surging inflation) means investors require a longer track record before fully pricing in credibility; any political interference in monetary policy decisions would be a severe credit negative.

### 3B. Debt Trajectory Narrative

Turkey's debt sustainability is not the primary credit concern — with debt at only ~24% of GDP, the debt trajectory is benign even under adverse scenarios. With nominal GDP growth of 15–20% (nominal, combining real growth and inflation), the debt denominator is growing rapidly, keeping the debt/GDP ratio stable despite fiscal deficits. The IMF projects debt/GDP to remain in the 24–28% range through 2030 under baseline conditions. The key fiscal risk is not sustainability per se but the persistence of elevated deficits that could undermine the disinflation narrative; for Turkey, the inflation–expectations–policy feedback loop is more important than the debt/GDP trajectory.

### 3C. External Liquidity

The reserve rebuilding since mid-2023 represents the most critical credit improvement for Turkey. With gross reserves exceeding US$200bn and net reserves at approximately US$80bn, Turkey's external liquidity position has transformed from a critical vulnerability into a credible buffer. Short-term external debt (primarily rolling corporate FX obligations) of approximately US$70–80bn annually is now comfortably covered by gross reserves on a one-to-one basis. The current account near-balance further reduces the gross external financing requirement. Key monitoring risk: carry trade positioning means that rapid capital outflows in a risk-off environment could rapidly erode reserves, requiring policy rate defensiveness that is in tension with the disinflation objective.

## 4. WILLINGNESS TO PAY

| Dimension | Score (1–5) | Commentary |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Governance & Rule of Law (WGI) | **2.0** | 32nd WGI percentile as noted by Fitch; political centralization, reduced judicial independence, and governance deterioration under Erdoğan; this is the single biggest structural credit constraint |
| Fiscal Policy Credibility | **3.0** | Şimşek framework is credible and has demonstrated measurable results; however, it represents a sharp reversal from the 2021–2023 unorthodox period and faces political sustainability risk given Erdoğan's historical intervention in monetary policy |
| Central Bank Independence | **2.5** | TCMB operational independence has been restored under Governor Karahan and the Şimşek framework; the 38% policy rate reflects genuine monetary orthodoxy; however, institutional independence is not legally or politically entrenched, and the 2021–2023 experiment creates a credibility ceiling |
| Debt Payment Track Record | **4.0** | Turkey has maintained continuous sovereign debt service, including through the 2001 financial crisis (which was a banking/currency crisis, not a sovereign default); IMF program history provides a framework for crisis management |

**Synthesis:** Turkey's willingness to pay is intact and backed by a strong sovereign debt service record. The critical uncertainty is political: Şimşek's orthodox framework has been delivered under Erdoğan's political authorization, and history demonstrates that this authorization can be withdrawn — as it was in 2021–2023. Investors are pricing Turkey at BB- rather than BBB because the institutional independence necessary for sustained policy credibility is not structurally embedded. Erdoğan's long-term health and political succession create additional governance uncertainty.

The path from Ba3 to investment grade is longer for Turkey than for peers like Romania due to the institutional credibility deficit, but the macro improvement is real and accelerating.

## 7. ESG CONSIDERATIONS

The concentration of executive power in President Erdoğan is the single largest governance risk. |

## 8. RATING SENSITIVITIES

**Factors that could lead to a downgrade (negative pressure):**
- Political intervention in TCMB monetary policy triggers interest rate cuts before inflation falls below 20%, reigniting inflation spiral
- Current account deficit widens above 4% of GDP driven by credit expansion or energy price shock, depleting rebuilt reserves
- Geopolitical escalation (Syria, Greece, NATO tensions) creates external financing disruption or defense spending surge
- Governance deterioration — post-election political instability or constitutional changes limiting institutional independence — impairs investor confidence

**Factors that could lead to an upgrade (positive pressure):**
- Headline inflation falls durably below 20% by end-2026, validating TCMB credibility and enabling controlled policy rate normalization
- S&P upgrades Turkey to BB Stable, with all three agencies at BB or above, creating investment-grade momentum
- Current account surplus or near-balance maintained for two consecutive years alongside continued reserve accumulation
- Governance improvements: strengthened judicial independence, press freedom, and institutional checks on executive power validated by WGI percentile improvement

---

## 9. ANALYST CONCLUSION

Turkey's sovereign credit represents one of the most compelling macro turnarounds in the 2023–2026 EM universe. The Şimşek-Karahan policy framework has delivered a reserve transformation of ~US$260bn, a current account adjustment of ~5 percentage points, and the beginnings of a credible disinflation path — achievements that justify the Moody's July 2025 upgrade to Ba3 and the Fitch January 2026 Positive Outlook revision. The near-term credit challenge is to sustain this orthodoxy through the political cycle: Erdoğan's historic pattern of monetary policy interference means investors require a prolonged track record before awarding Turkey the institutional credibility premium embedded in investment-grade ratings. The path to investment grade exists — ultra-low debt (24% GDP), strong growth (4.2%), and improved reserves support it — but requires inflation to fall below 20% on a sustained basis and governance indicators to stabilize. At current spreads (~300–350 bps), Turkey offers meaningful value for investors willing to take political risk on continued orthodox policy delivery.

> **We assign Turkey an implied rating of Ba3 with a Positive Outlook, reflecting a dramatic macro adjustment and reserve rebuilding under the Şimşek framework, constrained by persistent 30%+ inflation, governance deterioration at the 32nd WGI percentile, and the institutional credibility ceiling imposed by Turkey's history of policy reversals.**
